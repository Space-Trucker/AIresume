# Testing the goal

These tests check the goal itself, before there is any system. Each test has an **oracle**, the source of the
right answer, that lies outside the goal, so the goal is never used as evidence for itself. Run them
from this folder (Python 3.11 or later, standard library only):

```bash
python3 -m goaltests                      # tests goal.toml (the current goal)
python3 -m goaltests goal_v1.toml         # tests the goal as first agreed in conversation
python3 -m goaltests --only 1,2,7         # a subset
python3 -m goaltests --json results.json  # also writes the results
```

Every bar a check applies is the tests' own, fixed in `goaltests/anchors.py` with its source, or with
when it was set and what was known then; the goal's tolerances are claims, and a claim looser than a
bar fails. Each check says what it rests on, and the totals are reported apart: an outside answer with
an outside bar ("external"), the goal's numbers against each other ("consistency"), statements and
wording ("presence"), and GOAL.md against goal.toml ("hygiene").

Simulations use fixed seeds, so every run repeats exactly. GT-2, GT-3 and GT-5 need real prices. Those
come from data files that public Python projects ship for their tests and examples, downloaded once:
three packages from PyPI, pinned by version and checked against PyPI's SHA-256, and three files from
GitHub, pinned by commit and checked against a recorded SHA-256. Only their CSV text is read
(`goaltests/data.py`). Without network access those checks report "no data" instead of a verdict.

A hygiene check never counts as a catch. ## Limits of these tests

- Volatility by horizon scales the daily volatility of two large stocks by the square root of time.
  Real intraday volatility is U-shaped through the day and noisier at very short horizons, which
  would make short horizons look worse, not better.
- GT-5's real prices are daily, so its strategies hold for days. Intraday dynamics enter through
  GT-4's simulations, not through intraday history: this environment cannot reach an intraday data
  vendor. Daily bars also can't split a position's market exposure into overnight and intraday, as
  the goal's excess return does.
- The real stocks are five large technology names, more volatile than a typical stock in the
  universe, and they survived and grew. Their gaps make the overnight checks stricter. The stop
  slippage measured on them rests on 61 stress days and is driven by a few (19 October 2000,
  19 September 2008); a different sample could move it either way.
- Without an earnings calendar, GT-2 drops each stock's 2% largest gaps as a stand-in for earnings
  nights in its 1-in-100 gap. The two named stress gaps were checked by hand: neither was a scheduled
  earnings release.
- Some anchors are judgments, said as such in `anchors.py`: the joint power floor (set after version 5's
  power was known), the reference prior and edge sizes, "starting small" as a quarter of the capital,
  the 1-in-20 stress day. Judgment bars set before results (round 2 or 3) are kept even where version 5
  failed them, and the goal changed instead.
- GT-4 judges power and the live test on the estimate, not its bound. Judged at their bounds, the
  live test's bars could not all be met by any stop threshold tried (−2.0 to −2.5).
- The gate rules are evaluated as written in `goaltests/gates.py`. That file defines what each rule
  means, and it is part of the goal's precision. G3 is the owner's decision: KR1.8 has no rule the tests
  can run.
- GT-7 checks that the controls claiming an item name the elements it asks for, not that the wording
  is enough to meet it in practice. That judgment is GT-9's.
- GT-10 catches what its 70 defects test, no more. One defect has no outside anchor and stays a known
  gap: an order's share of the volume while it works (any value passes).
- The jump assumptions (50% overnight, 20% within the session) come from named events and market rules.
  Larger jumps have happened: SVB lost its whole value. The goal bounds those by the jump-loss limit, not
  by the daily loss limit.

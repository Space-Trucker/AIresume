## Q1. What do the named studies and the 2025–2026 benchmarks find about look-ahead and memorization bias, and how large is it?

### Takeaway
Every study that tested for it finds the same thing. LLMs have memorized financial facts and outcomes from before their training cutoff, and that memory inflates forecasting results inside the training window. Instructions and masking don't stop it, and it disappears after the cutoff. Its size depends on how the model is used. For a fixed text encoder with a supervised head trained on top, the cost is about 2% of the Sharpe ratio (ChronoBERT 4.80 vs Llama 3.1 4.90). For generative or agentic use, the loss is large. LLM trading agents lose 51–62% of their backtested Sharpe ratio and 50–72% of their return after the cutoff. A point-in-time generative model trails models without point-in-time training by 38–46%, an upper bound on the leak.

### Cited Findings
**Glasserman & Lin, "Assessing Look-Ahead Bias in Stock Return Predictions Generated by GPT Sentiment Analysis" (SSRN, Sept 2023)**
- Setup: GPT-3.5 scores the sentiment of news headlines. The study compares trading on the original headlines with trading on de-identified headlines, where the company is removed. It uses two headline sets: a scraped set and a Thomson Reuters set for S&P 500 firms. [1 src] — [SSRN 4586726](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4586726); [arXiv 2309.17322](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.17322)
- Inside the LLM's training window, the anonymized headlines *outperform* the originals. The authors read this as a "distraction effect" that outweighs look-ahead bias: general knowledge of the firm muddies the sentiment score. The effect is strongest for large firms. They propose anonymization both for out-of-sample use and for de-biased backtesting. [2 src: abstract; restated by later citing work per search excerpt] — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4586726); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2309.17322); [Look-Ahead-Bench (citing)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.13770)
- Later work disputes what anonymization proves: Lopez-Lira, Tang & Zhu find that LLMs reconstruct masked entities and dates from minimal context (below). A review cited in search results (not identified) says the same of Sarkar & Vafa. [2 src for the masking result] — [Lopez-Lira, Tang & Zhu](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390924734_The_Memorization_Problem_Can_We_Trust_LLMs'_Economic_Forecasts); [Gao, Jiang & Yan](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23847)

**Sarkar & Vafa, "Lookahead Bias in Pretrained Language Models" (SSRN, March 2024, labeled preliminary; listed at ICML 2025)**
- The authors build direct tests on events assumed unpredictable from a fixed information set. They find look-ahead bias in two applications: risk factors predicted from corporate earnings calls (using Llama 2) and election winners predicted from candidate biographies. Prompting-based counter-measures have problems. Their remedy is models pre-trained only on text produced before the analysis period, with no survivorship bias. [2 src] — [SSRN 4754678](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4754678); [ICML 2025](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/51018); [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/investing-expertise/lookahead-bias-in-large-language-model-training-data/)
- Asked for risks in late-2019 earnings calls, Llama 2 raised Covid-19 in over 25% of cases, even when told to use only earlier information. [1 src: secondary; not checked against the paper] — [ResearchGate entry](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/379767258_Lookahead_Bias_in_Pretrained_Language_Models)

**Lopez-Lira & Tang, "Can ChatGPT Forecast Stock Price Movements?" (arXiv April 2023; ScienceDirect lists it with a *Journal of Financial Economics* article ID, 2026)**
- The GPT-4 overnight-news strategy, evaluated from 2021Q4, had an annualized Sharpe ratio of 6.54 in 2021Q4, 3.68 in 2022, 2.33 in 2023 and 1.22 from January to May 2024. The sample is meant to start after the models' training cutoff (Sept 2021). That date is from background knowledge and was not re-verified here. These figures are older and end in May 2024. [1 src] — [arXiv 2304.07619](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07619); [JFE listing](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X26001066)
- The authors call this decline suggestive evidence that AI adoption is reducing information-processing frictions in real time, and secondary summaries read it the same way. [2 src] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07619); [Swedroe summary](https://larryswedroe.substack.com/p/can-chatgpt-forecast-stock-price); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/chatgpt-forecast-stock-price/)
- Full-sample Sharpe ratios depend on the version. A later version reports 2.97 for GPT-4 against 1.66 for GPT-3.5 (next-day drift). An earlier draft reports 3.8 against 3.1. [1 src] — [Wharton 2024 version](https://jacobslevycenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Lopez-Lira.pdf); [arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07619v3)

**Lopez-Lira, Tang & Zhu, "The Memorization Problem: Can We Trust LLMs' Economic Forecasts?" (arXiv 2504.14765; v1 April 2025, revised Dec 2025)**
- This is the first systematic test of LLM memory for economic indicators, news headlines, stock returns and conference calls. The models recall exact numerical values of key economic variables from before their cutoff, and that recall falls at random across dates and data types. Explicit instructions to respect historical boundaries do not prevent recall-level accuracy. Masking also fails, because LLMs reconstruct entities and dates from minimal context. **After the cutoff, the models cannot recall the data.** Apparent predictive success may therefore be memorization, which affects backtests of trading strategies. [2 src: abstract; characterized the same way by Gao, Jiang & Yan] — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390924734_The_Memorization_Problem_Can_We_Trust_LLMs'_Economic_Forecasts); [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/es/abs/2504.14765); [Gao, Jiang & Yan](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23847)

**Levy, "Caution Ahead: Numerical Reasoning and Look-Ahead Bias in AI Models" (Chicago Booth working paper Dec 2024; *Journal of Accounting Research*, 2026)**
- Commercial LLMs show substantial look-ahead bias that may explain a large part of their apparent predictive power in accounting and finance, and their numerical reasoning is weak. [2 src] — [JAR 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/1475-679x.70058); [Chicago Booth](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/research/center-for-applied-artificial-intelligence/research/our-faculty-research/2025/caution-ahead-numerical-reasoning-and-look-ahead-bias-in-ai-models); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/investing-expertise/innumeracy-and-look-ahead-bias-in-llms)
- Representations from models trained on data after the earnings announcement raise prediction accuracy by about 5 percentage points (absolute). GPT-4's accuracy falls from 60% to chance (50%) when only the least significant digit of each financial-statement item is randomized. [1 src] — [JAR](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/1475-679x.70058)

**He, Lv, Manela & Wu, "Chronologically Consistent Large Language Models" (arXiv Feb 2025; ScienceDirect lists it with a JFE article ID, 2026)**
- ChronoBERT and ChronoGPT are trained only on text from before each model's cutoff. In a test that predicts next-day returns from news, their real-time Sharpe ratios are comparable to a much larger Llama model, so look-ahead bias is "modest". The bias is "model and application-specific", because a prediction model on top can make up for weaker language comprehension. [2 src] — [arXiv 2502.21206](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.21206v1); [AFA listing](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=177856); [authors' slides](http://asaf.manela.org/papers/hlmw/chronollm/slides.pdf)
- Long-short Sharpe: ChronoBERT 4.80 vs Llama 3.1 4.90, and ChronoGPT 4.92 (slides). [1 src] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.21206v1); [slides](http://asaf.manela.org/papers/hlmw/chronollm/slides.pdf); [JFE listing](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X26001455)
- ChronoGPT-Instruct, a point-in-time *generative* model (He, Lv & Manela, Oct 2025), reaches a real-time Sharpe of 0.95 against 1.53 and 1.76 for larger models without point-in-time training. The authors read this as at least 54–62% of the apparent return predictability persisting without data leakage. [1 src] — [arXiv 2510.11677](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11677)

**Drinkall, Rahimikia, Pierrehumbert & Zohren, "Time Machine GPT" (NAACL Findings 2024)**
- TiMaGPT is a series of point-in-time ("nonprognosticative") LLMs, each trained only on data from before its cutoff (yearly, e.g. end-2011, end-2012). The models and training data are released. The authors argue that adapting an existing model by further pre-training on time-specific data does not work, because the base model has already seen the future. [2 src] — [arXiv 2404.18543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18543v1); [ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/dashboard/2024.findings-naacl.208)

**2025–2026 benchmarks and newer work**
- **Look-Ahead-Bench** (Benhenda, arXiv 2601.13770, Jan 2026) runs end-to-end agentic trading in the "AI Hedge Fund" multi-agent framework on AAPL, MSFT, GOOGL, NVDA and TSLA. It measures look-ahead bias as "alpha decay" from an in-sample period (Apr–Sep 2021) to an out-of-sample period (Jul–Dec 2024). Standard LLMs (Llama 3.1 8B and 70B, DeepSeek 3.2) show significant look-ahead bias. The point-in-time "Pitinf" models do not, and they generalize better as they scale. [existence 2 src, results 1 src] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13770); [GitHub](https://github.com/benstaf/lookaheadbench); [described by HindsightBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18867v1)
- **Gao, Jiang & Yan, "Detecting Lookahead Bias in LLM Forecasts"** (SSRN Dec 2025; arXiv v2 June 2026; earlier titled "A Test of Lookahead Bias in LLM Forecasts"):
  - Method: "Lookahead Propensity" (LAP) is the first-token probability of "up" plus "down" for a query that gives only the firm's name, ticker and a date.
  - LAP is materially positive inside the training period and falls to about zero right after the cutoff.
  - In two applications (headlines predicting returns, and earnings calls predicting capex), forecast accuracy rises with LAP, and that interaction loses significance after the cutoff. The result holds across model families. [1 src] — [arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23847); [SSRN 5985277](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5985277)
- **HindsightBench** (Jia, arXiv 2607.18867, July 2026, a single-author preprint):
  - Method: a black-box audit that changes the stated date across four arms, runs two memory probes and computes six metrics, including a "behaviorally effective knowledge cutoff".
  - Tested 15 models from 7 vendors on a 258-node macro panel. Date-triggered behavior was absent in the 2024 open-weight models (1B–70B) and present in the 2026-generation models.
  - Effective cutoffs span 22 months across vendors and can fall up to 8 months before the vendor-reported date, so calendar-window placebo designs are invalid.
  - An audit costs $19–30 per model at 2026 list prices. [1 src] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18867); [GitHub](https://github.com/Khaozhe/hindsightbench)
- **DatedGPT** (Yan, Tang et al., arXiv 2603.11838, March 2026):
  - Twelve 1.3B-parameter models trained from scratch on about 100B tokens each, with yearly cutoffs from 2013 to 2024, then instruction-tuned on data that respects the same cutoffs.
  - Perplexity probing shows each model's knowledge stops at its cutoff.
  - DatedGPT-instruct reaches an annualized Sharpe of 3.20 in stock-return prediction free of look-ahead bias. [1 src] — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.11838v1); [AFA listing](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=198940)
  - A third-party comment notes that the instruction-tuned models are only as clean as the teacher model's own cutoff. [1 src] — [Pith](https://pith.science/paper/2603.11838)
- **Kelly, Malamud, Schwab & Xu, "Scaling Point-in-Time Language Models"** (NBER WP 35247, May 2026; arXiv 2607.11889):
  - Decoder-only models of up to 4B parameters, trained on 1 trillion chronologically filtered FineWeb tokens.
  - They approach open-weight models of similar size, though a gap remains on several benchmarks. Earlier point-in-time models lagged substantially.
  - In an out-of-sample portfolio test, the point-in-time embeddings earn robust positive Sharpe ratios, close to full-sample embeddings that break temporal validity. [1 src: one paper, three listings] — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35247); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11889); [SSRN 6681860](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6681860)
- **Kong et al.**, a position paper (arXiv 2602.14233, Feb 2026; ICML 2026), reviewed 164 LLM-in-finance papers from 2023–2025. No single bias (look-ahead, survivorship, narrative, objective, cost) is discussed in more than 28% of them. [1 src] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14233); [ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/67204)
- **FINSABER** (arXiv 2505.07078) reports that LLM advantages claimed in recent studies often vanish under broader and longer evaluations that control for survivorship and look-ahead. [1 src, via a citing summary] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v5)

### Inferences
- The finding is consistent across studies and methods (recall tests, unpredictable-event tests, LAP, alpha decay, membership inference): memorization of pre-cutoff financial data is pervasive and cannot be prompted away. Two independent tests also confirm it vanishes after the cutoff (Lopez-Lira, Tang & Zhu; Gao, Jiang & Yan).
- The size follows the use:
  - small for a frozen encoder whose output feeds a supervised head trained only on past data (ChronoBERT vs Llama about 2%; Kelly et al. "close");
  - large for direct generative or agentic judgment (ChronoGPT-Instruct 38–46% lower than larger models without point-in-time training, which is an upper bound since capability also differs; agents lose 51–62% of their Sharpe; see Q2).
  The system's "AI model for the slower thinking" is the second kind.
- The bias cannot be measured for a given frontier model on a given task without a point-in-time twin of that model, and none exists for current frontier LLMs. A gate meant to be unfoolable (O6) therefore cannot accept such a model's forecast on in-window history.
- Glasserman & Lin show that anonymizing the input improves a GPT-3.5 headline-sentiment signal. They do not show that look-ahead bias is absent in the anonymized version, and later work shows masked entities are recovered.
- The goal's own checks would likely miss this leak.
  - KR6.4's lag audit cannot detect it. The leak sits in the model's weights and is triggered by entity and date context, which is still there when inputs are lagged one bar.
  - G1's "Sharpe above 4 is audited for leaks" rule may never fire. LLM leakage inflates performance by tens of percent at plausible Sharpe levels (Q2), not to absurd levels.

## Q2. How large is the gap between in-sample and post-training-cutoff performance in published LLM trading and forecasting studies?

### Takeaway
Published gaps run from about 2% for embedding features with a trained head to 50–72% for LLM trading agents. Sharpe decay for those agents is 51–62%, total-return decay 50–72%, and returns after the cutoff "collapse to a statistical zero". Signals that were genuinely post-cutoff were real but faded fast: the GPT-4 news strategy fell from a Sharpe of 6.54 to 1.22 within about 2.5 years.

### Cited Findings
- **Profit Mirage** (Li et al., arXiv 2510.07920, Oct 2025) [1 src: abstract, plus a secondary review that derives from it] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07920); [review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/profit-mirage-revisiting-information-leakage-in-llm-based-financial-agents)
  - FinGPT, FinMem, FinReport, HedgeAgents and others look strong in backtests. Re-run after their base LLM's cutoff, the return "collapses to a statistical zero".
  - Sharpe decay ranges from 51.48% (QuantAgent) to 62.23% (FinCON). Total-return decay ranges from 50.18% (TradingAgents) to 71.85% (FinMem).
  - Their FinLake (or FinLeak) benchmark has 2,000 Q&A pairs covering Jan 2022–Jun 2023. One secondary summary says leading LLMs answer over 85% correctly; that figure is unverified.
- **Look-Ahead-Bench** (Jan 2026): significant alpha decay from 2021 in-sample to 2024 out-of-sample for Llama 3.1 8B/70B and DeepSeek 3.2; the point-in-time models do not decay. [1 src] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13770)
- **FinCAD** (Li, Wang & Ma, arXiv 2605.24564; EMNLP 2026 Main) tested five 7–14B models on five mega-cap stocks. Removing the memorized component cut in-sample returns by up to 67.1%. Across eleven models, the rank correlation between in-sample Sharpe rankings and 2025 Sharpe rankings is only +0.779 before correction. [1 src] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24564); [GitHub](https://github.com/waylonli/FinCAD)
- **MemGuard-Alpha** (Roy et al., arXiv 2603.26797, March 2026) [1 src] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26797); [SSRN 6881781](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6881781); [Sept 2026 retitle listing](https://github.com/bddk520/DailyArXiv/issues/401)
  - In-sample accuracy rises with the contamination quintile (40.8% to 52.5%), while out-of-sample accuracy falls (47% to 42%).
  - Signals flagged as clean earn 14.48 bp a day against 2.13 bp for contaminated ones.
  - A September 2026 revision is retitled "Limits of Membership Inference for Detecting and Filtering …".
- **Levy** (JAR 2026): about +5 percentage points of earnings-prediction accuracy from post-announcement training data. [1 src] — [JAR](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/1475-679x.70058)
- **Gao, Jiang & Yan**: LLM forecast accuracy is concentrated in firm-dates where LAP is high, and the effect is insignificant after the cutoff. [1 src] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23847)
- **Point-in-time vs unconstrained models:**
  - ChronoBERT 4.80 vs Llama 3.1 4.90 [1 src] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.21206v1)
  - ChronoGPT-Instruct 0.95 vs 1.53 and 1.76 [1 src] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11677)
  - Kelly et al.: point-in-time embeddings close to full-sample [1 src] — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35247)
  - DatedGPT-instruct: Sharpe 3.20 free of look-ahead bias [1 src] — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.11838v1)
- **Post-cutoff decay without leakage:** GPT-4 overnight-news Sharpe went 6.54 (2021Q4) → 3.68 (2022) → 2.33 (2023) → 1.22 (Jan–May 2024). [1 src] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07619)

### Inferences
- For generative or agentic use, about half of a backtested Sharpe ratio inside the training window is leakage. That is the same size as G1's margin. An apparent Sharpe of 1.5 with half of it leaked is a true 0.75, below the KR1.2 floor of 1.0. G1 cannot tell the two apart.
- Even on clean history, a signal from a commodity LLM decays as adoption spreads (6.54 → 1.22 in about 2.5 years). A point-in-time backtest over 2016–2026 can still overstate what remains in 2027 and later. That is not look-ahead bias but non-stationarity, and it argues for G2 and the live gates, not against KR6.3.
- Most published Sharpe ratios (4.8, 3.2, 2.97) come from daily-rebalanced news portfolios across broad universes, and appear to be before trading costs (not confirmed). They are not comparable to the goal's *net* Sharpe target.

### Gaps
- No in-sample vs post-cutoff comparison was found for the 2026 frontier models (the kind a "slower thinking" layer would use).
- The cost treatment in these studies was not verified.
- Profit Mirage's figures depend on its agent configurations, and no independent replication was found.
- No figures were obtained for horizons other than daily (intraday, or 2–5 days).

## Q3. Which mitigations exist, and how well do they work?

### Takeaway
Two mitigations remove the bias by construction: training only on data dated before each decision (point-in-time models), and evaluating only after the model's cutoff. The first now costs little performance for embedding-style use. The second leaves too few days inside G1's window. Prompt restrictions don't work. Masking is partial, because models re-identify firms and dates. Inference-time "unlearning" (Merchant & Levy; FinCAD) is new, partial and limited to open-weight models. Detection methods (LAP, membership inference, HindsightBench) can show the bias exists but cannot certify its absence.

### Cited Findings
- **Prompt-level restrictions fail.**
  - Explicit instructions to respect historical boundaries do not prevent recall-level accuracy (Lopez-Lira, Tang & Zhu). Prompting-based approaches have problems: the model raises Covid risks for pre-pandemic dates even when told to use only earlier information (Sarkar & Vafa). [2 src] — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390924734_The_Memorization_Problem_Can_We_Trust_LLMs'_Economic_Forecasts); [SSRN 4754678](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4754678)
  - A 2026 survey: prompt-level and retrieval-based mitigations reduce obvious memory cues but do not remove information stored in the model's parameters. [1 src] — [arXiv 2604.02279](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.02279)
- **Entity and date masking is partial.**
  - Anonymization improved in-sample performance by removing distraction (Glasserman & Lin). [2 src] — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4586726)
  - But LLMs reconstruct masked entities and dates from minimal context (Lopez-Lira, Tang & Zhu), and this is confirmed by Gao, Jiang & Yan's summary. [2 src] — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390924734_The_Memorization_Problem_Can_We_Trust_LLMs'_Economic_Forecasts); [Gao, Jiang & Yan](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23847)
  - MemGuard-Alpha's authors note that anonymizing also removes useful information. [1 src] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26797)
- **Evaluating only after the cutoff is clean.**
  - No recall after the cutoff (Lopez-Lira, Tang & Zhu), and LAP collapses to about zero after it (Gao, Jiang & Yan). [2 src] — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390924734_The_Memorization_Problem_Can_We_Trust_LLMs'_Economic_Forecasts); [arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23847)
  - Vendor cutoffs are imprecise: behaviorally effective cutoffs span 22 months across vendors and can fall up to 8 months before the reported date. [1 src] — [HindsightBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18867)
  - Inside G1's window, a 2025–2026 cutoff leaves only 80–450 usable days (this project's own review). — [reviews/quant-statistics.md Q-3](/home/user/AIresume/trading/goal/reviews/quant-statistics.md)
- **Point-in-time models exist and are close to unconstrained ones for embedding use.**
  - Families: TiMaGPT (2024, yearly vintages) — [ACL](https://preview.aclanthology.org/dashboard/2024.findings-naacl.208); ChronoBERT and ChronoGPT (2025) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.21206v1); ChronoGPT-Instruct (2025) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11677); DatedGPT (2026; 12 vintages, cutoffs 2013–2024, 1.3B parameters) — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.11838v1); Kelly et al. (2026; up to 4B parameters, 1T tokens) — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35247).
  - The cost is small for embedding or encoder use: 4.80 vs 4.90, and "close" to full-sample. It is larger for generative use (0.95 vs 1.53–1.76).
  - Continued pre-training of a modern model on old data does not remove its future knowledge. [2 src] — [arXiv 2404.18543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18543v1); [ACL](https://preview.aclanthology.org/dashboard/2024.findings-naacl.208)
  - A point-in-time guarantee holds only if the corpus dates and any instruction-tuning "teacher" are also clean. [1 src] — [Pith on DatedGPT](https://pith.science/paper/2603.11838)
- **Embedding-based features carry the least measured bias.** A regression or prediction layer on top makes up for weaker language comprehension (He et al.), and point-in-time embeddings perform close to full-sample ones (Kelly et al.). [2 src: two independent papers agree] — [arXiv 2502.21206](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.21206v1); [NBER w35247](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35247)
- **Inference-time unlearning (no retraining).**
  - Merchant & Levy (NeurIPS 2025; arXiv 2512.06607) adjust the base model's logits at inference using a pair of auxiliary "forget" and "retain" models, removing both verbatim and semantic knowledge without retraining. The method is described the same way by three citing papers. [2 src] — [NeurIPS](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/loc/san-diego/132478); [Look-Ahead-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.13770v1); [HindsightBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18867v1); [FinCAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24564)
  - FinCAD (EMNLP 2026) uses context-aware decoding scaled per entity and date. It corrects in-sample returns by up to −67.1%. Out-of-sample 2025 returns stay within $8K of baseline and mean Sharpe within ±0.10 for the three larger models. General-benchmark accuracy holds for 4 of 5 models. The in-sample vs 2025 Sharpe rank correlation rises from +0.779 to +0.846. [1 src] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24564); [GitHub](https://github.com/waylonli/FinCAD)
- **Detection and filtering.**
  - The LAP regression test (Gao, Jiang & Yan) needs token probabilities. [1 src] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23847)
  - MemGuard-Alpha combines five membership-inference attacks with temporal features (Cohen's d 18.57 for its composite score, against 0.39–1.37 for the attacks alone). It was later retitled to stress its limits. [1 src] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26797); [listing](https://github.com/bddk520/DailyArXiv/issues/401)
  - HindsightBench is a black-box audit at $19–30 per model. [1 src] — [GitHub](https://github.com/Khaozhe/hindsightbench)
  - "Debiasing LLMs by Fine-tuning" (arXiv 2604.02921) exists; its results were not obtained. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02921v2)

### Inferences
- Ranked by strength of evidence:
  1. Point-in-time training, and evaluation only after the cutoff, remove the bias by construction, provided the dating is right.
  2. Embeddings feeding a head trained walk-forward: small bias, but not zero for an encoder without point-in-time training, and not measurable without a point-in-time twin.
  3. Inference-time unlearning: partial (rank correlation of 0.846, not 1). Tested only on open-weight 7–14B models and a few stocks. Cannot be applied to closed frontier APIs that don't expose logits.
  4. Detection: shows the bias exists but cannot certify its absence.
  5. Masking: partial.
  6. Prompts: ineffective.
- None of these can be shown to remove leakage from a closed frontier model over 2016–2026. Mitigations do not open a route for such a model into G1.
- KR6.3's evidence is "each model's documented cutoff". Two additions are needed:
  - a behavioral cutoff audit (perplexity probing as in DatedGPT, a LAP-style recall test, or a HindsightBench-style date test), because documented and effective cutoffs differ;
  - the rule must cover fine-tuning and instruction data, and any teacher model, not only pre-training.

### Gaps
- Merchant & Levy's effectiveness numbers were not obtained.
- No independent replication of FinCAD or MemGuard-Alpha was found.
- No source examined whether web-scale corpora with crawl-date stamps (e.g. FineWeb) contain later-edited or mis-dated pages that would leak into a "point-in-time" vintage.
- The compute and hosting cost of running point-in-time vintages was not found.

## Q4. Were the time-series foundation models (Chronos, TimesFM, Moirai, Lag-Llama and others) trained on financial series that overlap 2016–2026, and how do they perform out of sample on stock returns?

### Takeaway
Generic time-series foundation models (TSFMs) forecast daily stock returns poorly, both zero-shot and fine-tuned. Gradient-boosted trees beat them, and they do not beat simple volatility benchmarks. Models pre-trained from scratch on financial data do better. Whether the generic models' corpora overlap 2016–2026 could not be verified this session. Either way, KR6.3 covers any model whose corpus reaches into the window, and excluding generic TSFMs costs little forecasting value.

### Cited Findings
- **Rahimikia, Ni & Wang, "Re(Visiting) Time Series Foundation Models in Finance"** (arXiv 2511.18578, Nov 2025; SSRN 5770562; FoFI 2026) [paper's own claims, consistent across versions and two separate search results] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18578); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5770562); [FoFI 2026 PDF](http://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2026/files/2026/03/FoFI-2026-020-Eghbal-Rahimikia.pdf); [code](https://github.com/DeepIntoStreams/TSFM_Finance)
  - Off-the-shelf pre-trained TSFMs perform poorly zero-shot and after fine-tuning on daily excess returns. Zero-shot Chronos and TimesFM do substantially worse than CatBoost and LightGBM. The paper's verdict: "the zero-shot claim does not hold within the financial forecasting domain".
  - Most fine-tuned Chronos variants and both TimesFM models keep a weak out-of-sample fit. TSFMs improve with longer context windows: one TimesFM run lost money with a 5-day window and gained with a 512-day window.
  - Models pre-trained from scratch on financial data give substantial forecasting and economic gains. More data, synthetic augmentation and tuning add further gains.
- **"Pretrained Time-Series Foundation Models for Financial Return Forecasting"** (arXiv 2606.27100, 2026) ranks models by average rank: Moirai-2.0 2.9, TimesFM-2.5 3.1, Chronos (v1) 3.4. No model wins clearly. [1 src] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27100)
- **"Foundation models do not beat simple volatility benchmarks: Evidence from 5000 Chinese stocks"** (Finance Research Letters, 2026): the TSFMs tested (including the finance-specific Kronos, and Moirai and Lag-Llama) at best match HAR-RV. [1 src] — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612326011347)
- Related work exists but its findings were not obtained: TSFMs for realized volatility (arXiv 2505.11163) and an ACM survey of TSFM pre-training corpora and financial benchmarks. — [arXiv 2505.11163](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.11163); [ACM survey](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3785706.3785728)

### Inferences
- Generic TSFMs have weak evidence as return forecasters, so keeping them out of G1's forecast forgoes little.
- The route that works, pre-training from scratch on financial data, is in effect the system building its own model. It is compatible with KR6.3 if each vintage's pre-training data ends before the decision dates it serves.
- A finance TSFM pre-trained on multi-exchange price data that spans 2016–2026 has seen the very price paths G1 would test. That is direct look-ahead, and KR6.3 would bar it. No study was found that measures TSFM memorization of specific stock series.
- TSFMs could serve monitoring, for example forecasting operational metrics or flagging anomalies, where look-ahead does not matter. No source was gathered for this use.

### Gaps
- **Not verified (search budget exhausted).** The pre-training corpora, and their date ranges, of Chronos (arXiv 2403.07815), TimesFM (2310.10688), Moirai/LOTSA (2402.02592), Lag-Llama (2310.08278), Kronos (2508.02739), Time-MoE (Time-300B) and Sundial. Unverified leads from background knowledge:
  - TimesFM's corpus includes Google Trends, Wikipedia page views and synthetic series;
  - Moirai's LOTSA spans nine domains, including econ/finance;
  - Kronos was pre-trained on candlestick data from about 45 exchanges.
  Check each against its paper or model card before relying on it.
- Most results are recent preprints, and their methods were not checked.

## Q5. How long would a forward-only test after the cutoff need to confirm a net Sharpe of 1.5, and what do practitioners actually do with LLMs today?

### Takeaway
Years, as Decision 9 says. With daily returns:
- ~1.2 years only to show that a realized Sharpe of exactly 1.5 is above zero at 95% (it falls short half the time);
- ~2.8 years for 80% power, or 3.5–4.1 years with the autocorrelation that multi-day holds cause;
- 4.6–7.8 years once 10–100 variants are counted (G1's Deflated Sharpe Ratio rule);
- 11–25 years to show the Sharpe beats G1's floor of 1.0.

G2's 120 days has about 27% power even against zero. On practitioners, this session gathered no direct evidence (budget exhausted), only indirect signals.

### Cited Findings
- **Formulas.** Minimum track record length (MinTRL), from Bailey & López de Prado (2012, *J. of Risk*), uses the per-period Sharpe:
  MinTRL = 1 + [1 − γ₃·SR + (γ₄−1)/4·SR²]·(z_α/(SR − SR*))²,
  with γ₃ the skewness and γ₄ the kurtosis. The Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio comes from the same paper, and the Deflated Sharpe Ratio from Bailey & López de Prado (2014). — [Sharpe Ratio Efficient Frontier (risk.net)](https://www.risk.net/journal-risk/2223785/sharpe-ratio-efficient-frontier); [Deflated Sharpe Ratio (SSRN 2460551)](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551). These citations come from this project's [METHOD.md](/home/user/AIresume/trading/goal/METHOD.md) and were not re-fetched.
- **Cross-check.** The same formula is implemented in [goaltests/stats.py](/home/user/AIresume/trading/goal/goaltests/stats.py). An independent implementation written this session gives identical results (305.37 days in both; 3,040.67 days in both for the fat-tailed case). Script: `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-AIresume/47ede29a-4b89-5f91-8fc4-0134e08832eb/scratchpad/mintrl.py`.
- **Computed this session.** True or observed annualized net Sharpe 1.5 (daily 0.0945), 252 days a year, one-sided 95%:

  | Bar to clear | Normal returns | Skew −0.5, kurtosis 6 | Skew −1, kurtosis 10 |

- **Market-level signal.** Lopez-Lira & Tang attribute the falling Sharpe of LLM news signals to rapid adoption of AI tools that process news. [2 src] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07619); [Swedroe](https://larryswedroe.substack.com/p/can-chatgpt-forecast-stock-price)

### Inferences
- Decision 9's "years, not months" is correct, and if anything understated.
  - A realistic forward proof, with 80% power against zero, autocorrelation from 1–5-day holds, and at least 10 variants counted, takes about 4–5 years.
  - Matching G1's own bar of 2,400 days takes about 9.5 years.
  - Showing that the Sharpe beats 1.0 takes 11–25 years.
- Sequential testing can stop early only if the realized edge is large: a Sharpe of 3 needs about 78 days. Post-cutoff evidence (6.54 → 1.22 in about 2.5 years) suggests large edges from LLM news signals do not last.
- A forward test must pin one model version, preferably open weights, since a hosted model can change. Under KR6.1, any model change is a new variant and restarts the clock. Frontier models are replaced much faster than a 4–5-year test can finish, so the test would judge an obsolete model.
- If an LLM is an *add-on* to a forecast that already passed G1, a forward test of the add-on's return difference (its information ratio) takes about 2.7 years at an information ratio of 1.0, and about 10.9 years at 0.5 (MinTRL against zero, 95%).

## Q6. Recommendation: no pre-trained model in the forecast, point-in-time models only, or LLM features with mitigations plus a forward test?

### Takeaway
**Recommend "point-in-time models only".**
- Keep current frontier LLMs and generic TSFMs out of the forecast G1 judges. They may serve monitoring, operations, explanation and engineering help.
- Reword KR6.3 so that the cutoff that matters is each decision date, not the window start. That admits the system's own walk-forward models and audited point-in-time vintages (ChronoBERT/ChronoGPT, DatedGPT, TiMaGPT, Kelly et al.), whose embedding features have measured costs close to zero.
- A forecast driven by a frontier LLM can come only later, through a separate forward gate of about 4–5 years.

Confidence:
- about 85% that frontier LLMs and generic TSFMs must not shape G1's forecast;
- about 65% that the point-in-time allowance is worth its audit cost;
- about 90% that a forward-only proof takes several years.

### Cited Findings
**Evidence for keeping pre-trained frontier models out of G1's forecast**
- Memorization is pervasive and is not removed by instructions or masking. [2 src] — [Lopez-Lira, Tang & Zhu](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390924734_The_Memorization_Problem_Can_We_Trust_LLMs'_Economic_Forecasts); [Sarkar & Vafa](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4754678)
- The in-window performance of LLM agents is 50–72% leakage. [1 src] — [Profit Mirage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07920)
- Forecast accuracy is concentrated where the model shows look-ahead propensity. [1 src] — [Gao, Jiang & Yan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23847)
- Commercial LLMs carry substantial look-ahead bias. [2 src] — [Levy, JAR 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/1475-679x.70058)
- Effective cutoffs are uncertain by months. [1 src] — [HindsightBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18867)

**Evidence that point-in-time models make the restriction cheap**
- ChronoBERT ≈ Llama 3.1 (4.80 vs 4.90) [1 src] — [arXiv 2502.21206](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.21206v1)
- Kelly et al.'s point-in-time embeddings are close to full-sample ones [1 src] — [NBER w35247](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35247)
- DatedGPT-instruct: Sharpe 3.20 free of look-ahead bias, with vintages 2013–2024 that cover a 2016–2026 window [1 src] — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.11838v1)

**Evidence against, or for a looser rule**
- With embeddings, look-ahead bias is "modest" [2 src] — [He et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.21206v1); [AFA listing](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=177856)
- Distraction outweighs look-ahead for GPT-3.5 headline sentiment [2 src] — [Glasserman & Lin](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4586726)
- Inference-time debiasing works in part [1 src] — [FinCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24564); [Merchant & Levy](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/loc/san-diego/132478)
- LLM news signals had real value after the cutoff [1 src] — [Lopez-Lira & Tang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07619)

**Caveats on point-in-time models**
- They are small (1.3–4B parameters) and weaker as generators (0.95 vs 1.53–1.76) [1 src] — [ChronoGPT-Instruct](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11677)
- Their guarantee depends on clean corpus dates and on any teacher model used for instruction tuning [1 src] — [Pith](https://pith.science/paper/2603.11838)

### Inferences
- **Rule wording.** Read literally, KR6.3 ("no model trained on data after the start of the G1 window … shapes G1's return forecast") also bars two things that are not look-ahead: walk-forward retraining of the system's own models, and point-in-time vintage families. Proposed wording:

  > No model shapes the forecast for a decision date unless all of its training data predates that date by a margin. That includes pre-training data, fine-tuning data, instruction data and any teacher model's data. Each model's cutoff is documented and checked by a behavioral audit, such as perplexity probing, a look-ahead-propensity recall test, or a date-manipulation test.

  A model with a cutoff before the window starts satisfies this trivially. Current frontier LLMs and generic TSFMs (cutoffs in 2023–2026) do not.
- **Gate fit.** Add the cutoff audit to G1's evidence. KR6.4's lag audit and the "Sharpe above 4" check cannot catch leakage that lives in the model's weights (Q1).
- **Uses that count as shaping the forecast.**
  - *Data extraction.* LLM-extracted data that feeds the forecast, such as figures parsed from filings, shapes the forecast. It needs a point-in-time model or a deterministic check against structured sources, because LLMs substitute memorized numbers (Levy's least-significant-digit result).
  - *Idea generation.* A frontier LLM proposing features or hypotheses can carry 2016–2026 hindsight into the design. KR6.1 counts variants but cannot measure this channel. Mitigation: log LLM-suggested ideas as variants, and require a pre-registered economic rationale. Human researchers share this hindsight anyway; the LLM amplifies it. G2 and the live gates remain the real defense.
- **Swapping models.** Testing with a point-in-time vintage in G1 and then running a frontier LLM live breaks fidelity: it is a different model, and a new variant under KR6.1. The model G1 judges must be the model that trades.
- **Forward route.** If the owner wants a forecast driven by a frontier LLM, define a separate gate after G1 and G2:
  - a pinned model version (open weights preferred);
  - pre-registration and sequential stopping boundaries;
  - an expected 4–5 years at a true Sharpe of 1.5 (Q5).
  G2's 120 days cannot do this job (power about 27%).
- **Why the preferred option.** "No pre-trained model at all" is safe but discards a near-free benefit: point-in-time encoders perform nearly as well as unconstrained ones. "LLM features with mitigations plus a forward test" relies on mitigations that cannot be verified for closed models, and on a test that outlives the model it tests.

- No point-in-time model has been tested at the system's horizon (seconds to 5 days), on net-of-cost US long/short results.
- The confidence figures are the researcher's judgment from the evidence above, not computed probabilities.

# Keep the guardrails, repair the goal's arithmetic

On the evidence, the goal's protective limits should mostly stay and several of its numbers should be repaired. **Keep the 4% overnight cap**: at the planned ~15 overnight names an 8% cap raises return by only ×1.00–1.25, not the ×2 the goal's decision text claims, while doubling the loss from any single gap. **Let the risk budget, not a count, size the book**, with a 0.1% size floor and a rotation rule that charges switching cost, the opportunity cost of the risk budget and a forecast-error margin, switched on only after a counterfactual ledger shows it pays. **Plan the edge on holds of one to five trading days**: the goal's 2.5-hour basis is where a good intraday signal merely breaks even at 8 bps, not where it meets the goal's own rule that expected gain be twice the cost. To protect the evidence, raise the research budget from 10 to **about 30 counted trials spread over at least 10–15 distinct ideas** (the chance of finding a real edge roughly doubles while false passes stay below 0.3%). Replace the single same-family AI reviewer with **a blind cross-vendor AI panel at every gate plus one fixed-fee human review before material real money**, the owner ruling under rules the owner cannot waive alone. Admit **only point-in-time models** into the forecast G1 judges. Operationally, replace the 15/60-minute alert timer with **automatic, tiered containment that checks stops against the broker's records and never liquidates after 15:45**. Keep orders inside 09:30–16:00 for now while building a narrow, later exception for reduce-only, collared limit orders in pre-market and after-hours, never overnight. Before real money, note that the $2,000 G3 floor cannot reproduce the tested sizes, since a typical 4% position is $80. On the coordinator's arithmetic the floor is **about 250 times the universe's median share price, roughly $12,500–$50,000** (the median price is unverified). Meanwhile the 0.02%-of-volume liquidity cap starts narrowing the universe at about $100,000, so the intended capital is squeezed from both ends, and a US owner needs a dated §475(f) and wash-sale plan. The research also found **nine contradictions inside the goal**: for example, a 9% halt cannot keep equity above the 10% line when a permitted jump lands near it, and KR6.3 as worded forbids the walk-forward retraining G1 requires. Evidence quality is uneven. Page fetches were blocked and the shared search allowance ran out, so most external facts come from search-result extracts, many of them single-source. Decisions 1, 2 and 5 and the capital part of decision 10 rest mainly on arithmetic on the goal's own limits.

## Size positions for the jump, and let the budget set the count

### An 8% overnight cap buys at most a quarter more return, not double (Decision 1)

The goal's decision text says an 8% overnight cap "doubles the aim to about 10% a year" ([GOAL.md](/home/user/AIresume/trading/goal/GOAL.md)). Under the goal's other limits it does not. Those limits are 2% total loss at the stops, 75% overnight gross, 100% gross and a volatility target of 0.2% a day. With about 15 concurrent overnight names, the open-risk budget or the overnight gross limit binds before the per-name cap. Raising the cap from 4% to 8% therefore changes return by **×1.00–1.25 with the hard limits alone, and ×1.00 if the volatility target is kept** [computed from the goal's limits]. The cap matters a lot only in a signal-scarce book of about eight or fewer overnight names. Even there, doubling return means doubling the volatility budget, a decision to take more risk, not a free effect of the cap.

| Overnight names | Stop distance | At a 4% cap: size (binding limit) | At an 8% cap: size (binding limit) | Return, 8% ÷ 4%: hard limits / volatility target kept |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 2–3.3% | 4.0% (cap) | 8.0% (cap) | ×2.00 / ×1.58 |
| 10 | 2% | 4.0% (cap) | 7.5% (overnight gross) | ×1.88 / ×1.23 |
| **15 (plan)** | 2% | 4.0% (cap) | 5.0% (overnight gross) | **×1.25 / ×1.00** |
| **15 (plan)** | 3.3% | 4.0% (cap) | 4.0% (open risk) | **×1.00 / ×1.00** |
| 20 | 2–5% | 2.0–3.8% (open risk or gross) | the same | ×1.00 / ×1.00 |

What 8% would cost is concentrated in the jump tail. In the project's cached daily prices for five large tech stocks (14,953 overnight periods, 59.3 stock-years from 2003), gaps of 10% or more occurred **22.7 times per 10,000 stock-nights**, gaps of 20% or more 2.0 times, and gaps of 30% or more 1.3 times. No gap reached 50% [computed, local; one dataset of survivors] ([local price files](/home/user/AIresume/trading/goal/.data-cache/)). Two of the three gaps of 20% or more had nothing to do with earnings. One was **Yahoo's +49.5% on Microsoft's bid** (1 February 2008), opening at about 85% of a $31 offer that carried a 62% premium. The other was **NVIDIA's −28.0% on an unscheduled revenue warning** (3 July 2008) ([local yhoo.csv](/home/user/AIresume/trading/goal/.data-cache/yhoo.csv); [GT-2 annotation](/home/user/AIresume/trading/goal/goaltests/gt02_consistency.py)). Ordinary overnight volatility is modest: with the largest 2% of gap nights removed, overnight variance was only 18–21% of daily variance, against 33–42% with all nights [computed, local]. The tail is the whole problem. At 8%, a single adverse gap of 25% or more breaches the 2% daily limit by itself and forces the whole book flat at the open, the most expensive moment to trade. At the sample's non-earnings rate, about 1.3 per 10,000 nights with half of them adverse, a book running 3,300–3,800 position-nights a year would hit that **about once every four years**; the 95% interval runs from about yearly to once in 35 years [computed]. At 4% it takes a gap beyond 50%, which the sample never produced. A total loss, the SVB case the goal itself uses for KR2.8, costs 8% of equity at an 8% cap, twice KR2.8's 4% bound; at 4% it lands exactly on the bound. The liquidity cap adds a further limit: an 8% position at $100,000 ($8,000) exceeds the 0.02%-of-volume cap in **every name trading less than $40M a day**, so even on paper the extra room would be usable only in the more liquid part of the universe [computed].

Theory and practice point the same way. With jumps included, growth-optimal (Kelly) sizing barely distinguishes the caps. At an assumed 5 bp a day edge and 2% daily volatility, 4% and 8% are about 3% and 6% of the single-name Kelly fraction, so the guardrails decide, not growth [computed, assumed inputs]. Value-at-risk and expected shortfall at 97.5–99% cannot see an event with a nightly probability near 10⁻⁴. A 50% jump on an 8% position adds only about 0.016% to a 97.5% expected shortfall [computed]. The right tool is therefore a stress-loss limit: cap = loss budget ÷ design jump. The one professional reference found is a BNY Mellon market-neutral fund that caps any single long or short at **5% of net assets at the time of purchase or short sale** ([SEC 497K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/787623/000093041310005104/c63023_497k.htm)) [1 src]. A hard 4% cap checked daily at 15:45 is about as strict as a 5% cap applied only at entry. Other fund documents found limit net exposure, such as Steward's −20% to +40% ([Crossmark](https://www.crossmarkglobal.com/stewardfunds/steward-funds-08-28-23-summary-prospectus-emn/)) [1 src], or total shorts, not single names. Holding overnight is still worth protecting. Lou, Polk and Skouras find momentum profits earned overnight and most other anomalies' profits intraday ([LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/119010); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/2015/02/overnight-momentum-vs-intraday-momentum/)) [2+]. In three of the five cached stocks nearly all the return came overnight [computed, local]. The lever for more return is more overnight names, not bigger ones.

Shorts carry a separate takeover drag that depends on total short exposure, not on the per-name cap. An M&A event-study guide puts a target's announcement return at "commonly 15 to 30 percent" ([EventStudyTools](https://www.eventstudytools.com/ma-event-study-example)) [1 src]. KPMG puts average US control premia at 37% ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/pl/en/insights/business-transformation/control-premium-in-poland-2020-2024.html)) [1 src]. With about 30% short gross and an annual bid rate of 2–5% [unverified], the drag is roughly **0.1–0.45% a year** [computed on partly unverified inputs]. That is material against a 3–5% target, and a backtest on survivors only will understate it.

**Recommendation.** Keep 4% as the default overnight cap for single stocks, and refine it into a tiered stress-loss rule. The overnight cap per name is min(4%, 2% ÷ J, (9.5% − current drawdown) ÷ J), where J is the jump the name must survive. J is about 15–20% for broad ETFs and 50% for ordinary operating companies. It is 100% for shorts in small and mid-cap biotech and pharma, and for longs in run-prone financials under funding stress. The drawdown term is needed whichever cap is chosen; the contradictions section below shows why. The rule keeps the 8% industry cap, budgets the takeover drag, and should be tested on survivor-free data. Confidence is about **80%** that 4% beats a uniform 8% under the current limits, 60–65% that the tiers add value, and about 75% that drawdown scaling is needed. The external evidence is thin. There is no market-wide, survivor-free gap table. The list of recent bank failures and biotech bids came from memory and is unverified, and the literature on fund diversification rules and on jumps in portfolio choice was not opened. The recommendation would change if survivor-free statistics showed adverse non-earnings gaps of 25% or more below about one per 100 stock-years in large caps, which would support 5–6% for them. It would also change if the strategy ran eight or fewer overnight names, or if the owner explicitly chose a larger volatility budget.

### Budget, bands and a proven rotation rule replace the fixed count (Decision 2)

The theory confirms the owner's choice of dynamic allocation and sharpens it. Gârleanu and Pedersen show the optimal policy is to "aim in front of the target" and "trade partially toward the current aim", weighting slowly decaying signals more ([Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12080); [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w15205)) [2+]. That result assumes quadratic, impact-type costs. With proportional costs, the optimal policy is a **no-trade band**: hold anything inside it and trade only to its nearer edge (Constantinides 1986; Davis and Norman 1990) ([Lynch and Tan, NYU](https://archive.nyu.edu/bitstream/2451/26507/2/02-63.pdf); [MPRA](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/117162/1/Sweep_paper-4.pdf)) [2+]. The band is wide even when costs are small ([Guasoni and Muhle-Karbe](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.7330)) [1 src]. With linear costs and a position cap the policy becomes a threshold rule ([de Lataillade et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.5957)) [1 src]. This system's costs are close to proportional, because every position is capped at 0.02% of daily volume and cost is essentially half-spread plus slippage plus per-share fees [computed]. So hysteresis is the operative lesson, not fractional trading. Gârleanu and Pedersen's decay lesson still carries over: a candidate's value is its expected gain integrated over its decay and net of costs, not its instantaneous signal strength.

Practice agrees that the number of positions is an output, not an input. Optimizer-based managers maximize alpha minus a risk penalty minus amortized cost under limits. Grinold and Kahn's frontier gives at least 75% of the incremental value added for 50% of the turnover ([study notes](https://people.brandeis.edu/~yanzp/Study%20Notes/Active%20Portfolio%20Management.pdf)) [1 src]. Novy-Marx and Velikov find a buy/hold spread is the **single most effective simple cost mitigation**. A buy/hold spread is a stricter bar to open a position than to keep one. They also find few anomalies turning over more than 50% a month survive costs ([NBER](https://nber.org/papers/w20721); [RFS via IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v29y2016i1p104-147..html)) [2+]. DeMiguel, Martín-Utrera, Nogales and Uppal show that netting trades across signals raises the number of jointly useful characteristics from 6 to 15 ([Lancaster manuscript](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/133724/1/DMNU_Manuscript_InternetAppendix_2019_01_08.pdf); [UC3M](https://researchportal.uc3m.es/display/act524892)) [2+]. Frazzini, Israel and Moskowitz studied $1.7 trillion of live executions and found real costs an order of magnitude below academic estimates, so cost models should be calibrated on the system's own fills ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Working-Paper/Trading-Costs); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3229719)) [2+]. Costs also rise with volatility, so trading should slow in stressed markets ([NBER w24222](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24222)) [1 src]. This system trades about 100 times its equity a year (2 × 25 × 4% = 200% a week) [computed], far beyond Novy-Marx and Velikov's 50% a month, so every extra round trip has to earn its cost.

The evidence of harm is as strong as the evidence for banding. In Odean's study of discount-brokerage accounts, the stocks investors bought underperformed the ones they sold, even before costs and excluding liquidity, tax and rebalancing trades ([Odean](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers%20current%20versions/doinvestors.pdf); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=94143)) [2+]. The gap was about 3.3 percentage points over a year ([StudyCorgi summary](https://studycorgi.com/do-investors-trade-too-much-by-odean/)) [secondary only]. In Barber and Odean's study, the most active households earned 11.4% a year net against 17.9% for the market ([Barber and Odean](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/returns/individual_investor_performance_final.pdf); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:55:y:2000:i:2:p:773-806)) [2+]. Low costs remove only the second harm. The first is a model problem, a winner's curse. The best of N noisy forecasts is inflated by about E[max of N standard normals] times the noise: **1.54σ for 10 candidates, 3.24σ for 1,000 and 3.44σ for 2,000** [computed]. A universe of plausibly 1,000–2,000 names therefore biases a naive rule toward switching, since the held position's forecast was not selected from as many rivals.

The rule that follows is set out below. The parameter values are starting points to tune in G1 [judgment].

| Part | Rule | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Allocation | No fixed count. A position opens when it passes the entry rule and every limit holds as if filled. The limits checked are open risk (total and per side), gross, overnight gross, per-position notional, the 0.02%-of-volume cap, industry, net and the daily-loss budget. Each position risks between **0.1%** (a new floor) and **0.5%** (now a maximum) of equity | the count is an output in practice; the floor bounds it |
| Values | candidate v_j = g_j − C_j − κ·R_j·H_j; held position v_i = g_i − κ·R_i·H_i − ΔX_i. The held position's own exit cost cancels, because it is paid either way | capital budgeting [computed algebra] |
| Trigger | rotate only if v_j − Σv_i ≥ m, with m = max(C_j, z·σ_S) and z = 1 to start; free the binding resource from the lowest value per unit first, trimming rather than closing whole positions | no-trade band [2+]; greedy fractional knapsack |
| Forecasts | g is calibrated on the realized outcomes of top-ranked candidates. A held position's remaining gain comes from its signal's decay curve, re-scored on current data, never from its P&L since entry | winner's curse [computed]; avoids the disposition effect |
| Safeguards | minimum hold of max(15 min, 20% of the planned hold); the advantage must persist at two consecutive evaluations; at most 3 rotations a day and 20% of the week's round trips; no same-session re-entry; never close and reopen the same name and direction; rotations count inside the cost budget; rotation suspended when market-wide spread or volatility is above its 90th percentile | judgment; regime-dependent costs [1 src] |
| Proof | a shadow ledger follows each closed position to its planned exit. Rotation is enabled only if at least 50 G1 rotations show a positive mean realized switch value with a one-sided bootstrap 90% lower bound above zero. On paper, it switches off if the first 30 rotations have a mean below zero at t < −1 | Odean's test, applied to the system's own decisions |
| Guard | a count guard above the natural bound, which is 20 positions at a 0.1% floor; reaching it blocks entries and alerts the owner; the effective number of positions, (ΣR)²/ΣR², is reported daily | operational guard |

Here g is the expected gain under the position's own stop and exit rules, and C is the stressed round-trip cost (×1.5). κ is the trailing 20-session median of entries' (g − C)/(R·H), floored at zero. R is the loss at the stop, or the notional when gross is the binding limit; H is the remaining holding time; ΔX is the extra cost of exiting now rather than as planned. One worked example shows why the margin and the ledger, not the formula, decide whether rotation helps [computed, assumed inputs]. A naive advantage of 17.6 bps of notional shrinks to about **3.6 bps** once the candidate is charged for the extra 1.5 days it occupies the risk slot, which is small next to any plausible forecast error. The cost budget leaves little room. The plan's 25 round trips a week at 4% and 8 bps cost 4.16% of equity a year against a 4.5% budget. That is about 0.34% of headroom, roughly two extra round trips a week, and the ×1.5 stress already reaches 6.24% ([goal.toml](/home/user/AIresume/trading/goal/goal.toml)) [computed]. Rotations must therefore come out of the planned round trips, not on top of them.

The working draft of goal.toml (uncommitted, read on 2026-10-15) already states most of this rule. It has no fixed count and charges the opportunity cost of the risk budget. It excludes the held position's own exit cost and requires a 15-minute/20% minimum hold. It allows 3 rotations a day and a 20% share, forbids same-day re-entry, and follows each closed position as a shadow. It calibrates on selected trades and judges rotation on at least 50 G1 rotations and the latest 30 on paper. Its margin is one round-trip cost, and it has a malfunction guard of 50 positions ([goal.toml](/home/user/AIresume/trading/goal/goal.toml)). Against the evidence, the draft lacks four things: a forecast-error term in the margin, a minimum position size, a persistence check with suspension in stressed markets, and an explicit statistical criterion for switching rotation on and off. The floor matters most. The open-risk budget limits the sum of risk, not the count, so without a floor 2% could be split into 200 positions. A 0.1% floor bounds the book at 20 positions (15 per side) [computed], which also shows that the old max_positions = 20 was redundant as an allocator. Whether the guard is set at 25 or 50 matters far less than having the floor. Confidence is about **85%** that budget-based allocation with a floor is right, about **80%** that the rotation rule has the right economic form, and only **55–60%** that rotation adds net value for this system at all. Hence the default: implemented, but enabled only after the G1 ledger test passes.

## Plan the edge on one to five days, not minutes (Decision 3)

At a fixed 8 bps round trip, the accuracy a trade needs falls with the square root of its holding time. The table shows the per-trade information ratio (IR) needed, close to the information coefficient for a one-sigma signal. It assumes a stock with 30% annual volatility [computed; the volatility is an assumption].

| Hold | IR to break even | IR for the goal's 2× entry rule (hit rate) |
|---|---|---|
| 5 minutes | 0.37 | 0.75 (77%) |
| 30 minutes | 0.15 | 0.31 (62%) |
| 150 minutes | 0.068 | 0.14 (55%) |
| 1 day | 0.042 | 0.085 (53%) |
| 3 days | 0.024 | 0.049 (52%) |
| 5 days | 0.019 | 0.038 (51.5%) |

The best-documented intraday effect is Gao, Han, Li and Zhou's finding that SPY's first half-hour predicts its last half-hour. It has an out-of-sample R² of 1.4%, and a timing strategy earned **6.67% a year with a 6.19% standard deviation, a Sharpe of 1.08, before costs**, over 1993–2013 ([paper PDF](https://assets.super.so/e46b77e7-ee08-445e-b43f-4ffd88ae0a0e/files/ee7dac49-530b-4950-b5d0-e0b5eee08f2e.pdf); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/325364670_Market_Intraday_Momentum); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/first-and-last-half-hours-of-trading-linked/)) [2+]. That is about 2.6 bps gross a day and a per-trade IR of about 0.068 [computed]. At 8 bps, that quality **breaks even at about 151 minutes**, the goal's own 150-minute basis. The goal's 2× entry rule needs the same quality to hold for about 605 minutes (1.55 trading days), or about 3.5 days at the 12 bps cost stress [computed]. The 150-minute basis is consistent with the entry rule only if a round trip costs about 4 bps.

Minute-level predictability belongs to colocated firms. High-frequency return predictability sits in the most recent ~10 milliseconds or ~10 trades and falls sharply with small delays ([NBER w30366](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30366)) [2+]. Latency-arbitrage races are decided by **5–10 microseconds**, and the top six firms take more than 80% of race wins ([NBER w29011](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w29011/w29011.pdf); [BIS](https://www.bis.org/publ/work955.pdf)) [2+]. High-frequency firms' performance ranks with their relative latency ([JFQA via IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v54y2019i03p993-1024_00.html)) [2 hosts]. Predictability from order imbalances is gone within 5–60 minutes, and within 5–15 minutes for large NYSE stocks ([CFA Institute digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/en/research/cfa-digest/2005/11/evidence-on-the-speed-of-convergence-to-market-efficiency-digest-summary); [Chung and Hrazdil](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/empfin/v19y2012i5p702-720.html)) [2+]. A retail API at 100 ms or more is 10,000–20,000 times slower than the margin that wins those races [computed], so cheaper costs alone cannot rescue minute-level holds. The documented after-cost successes sit at daily-to-weekly horizons. De Groot, Huij and Zhou found large-cap weekly reversal earned **30–50 bps a week net of costs** before 2010 ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v36y2012i2p371-382.html); [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/short-term-reversal-in-stocks)) [2+]. In Jiang, Li and Wang, news-driven returns drift for several days and a strategy on that drift "remains profitable after transaction costs" ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2679614); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v141y2021i2p573-599.html)) [same paper, 2 hosts]. Lou, Polk and Skouras give a structural reason to hold across the close.

All of these effects decay. Predictor returns are 26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication ([McLean and Pontiff](https://www.gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf); [Ivey](https://ivey.uwo.ca/media/3775549/pontiff.pdf)) [2+]. Anomaly returns roughly halved after decimalization ([SMU](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/lkcsb_research/4735)) [2+], though one cross-country study finds the US result less robust ([CESifo](https://www.cesifo.org/en/publications/2018/working-paper/have-capital-market-anomalies-worldwide-attenuated-recent-era-high)) [1 src]. Khandani and Lo show a daily contrarian strategy falling to **0.13% a day** by 2006–07 ([NBER w14465](https://nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w14465/w14465.pdf)) [2; the end year differs between sources]. Reversal is strongest in illiquid stocks, which this universe filters out ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v61y2006i5p2365-2394.html)) [2 hosts]. Longer holds also cost something under the volatility target, because they mean more, smaller positions. The net edge each trade must earn rises to about 8–14 bps at three days and 11–18 bps at five days, against about 3–5 bps at 150 minutes [computed]. The signal has to persist for days.

The 8 bps planning cost looks plausible to slightly conservative, but it has to be measured live. Alpaca charges no commission on US equities ([Alpaca docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/regulatory-fees)) [issuer-only]. The SEC fee has been **$20.60 per million of sales since 4 April 2026** ([SEC advisory](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/fee-rate-advisories/2026-2); [FINRA](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/information-notice-20260317)) [2+]. FINRA's trading activity fee is paused from 1 October to 31 December 2026 ([SR-FINRA-2026-021](https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2026-09/SR-FINRA-2026-021.pdf)) [1 src]. Regulatory fees total about 0.2–0.6 bp a round trip [computed]. What dominates is routing. Schwarz, Barber, Huang, Jorion and Odean placed identical market orders and found **mean round-trip costs from 0.07% to 0.46% across brokers** ([JF via IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v80y2025i5p2507-2541.html); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4189239)) [2+]. Top wholesalers' median effective-over-quoted spread was about 0.30–0.34 in 2025 ([Global Trading](https://www.globaltrading.net/?p=39575)) [1 publisher]. Alpaca routes to Virtu, Citadel Securities, Jane Street and GTS ([Alpaca Rule 606, 2026 Q2](https://files.alpaca.markets/disclosures/library/SEC+606a1+-+2026Q2.pdf)) [issuer-only]. Alpaca's paper account fills market orders at the far side of the quote with no price improvement. It gives random partial fills to 10% of orders and fills limit orders on touch with no queue ([Alpaca forum, staff answer](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/slippage-paper-trading-vs-real-trading/2801); [TradersPost](https://blog.traderspost.io/article/alpaca-paper-trading)) [2]. So paper can never validate a minute-level strategy, and KR1.8's live cost check is essential.

**Recommendation.** Restate the planning basis: the plan expects its profit from holds of about one to five trading days, held overnight. Intraday holds stay allowed, but they are not expected to clear the 2× rule at 8 bps unless a round trip costs about 2 bps or less. Holds of seconds to minutes serve exits, risk control and execution, and intraday patterns time the entries and exits of multi-day trades. A cheap-universe study, in index ETFs and mega-caps at about 1–2 bps, is a reasonable secondary track for holds of 30 minutes to a few hours. At 2 bps the 2× rule needs an IR of 0.05–0.11 there [computed], within reach of the SPY effect. One working paper reports a **net Sharpe of 1.33 for an SPY intraday momentum strategy over 2007–2024** ([SFI](https://www.sfi.ch/fr/publications/n-24-97-beat-the-market-an-effective-intraday-momentum-strategy-for-s-p500-etf-spy)) [1 src, not peer-reviewed]. The price is breadth: shrinking from roughly 1,000 to 200 names cuts the attainable cross-sectional IR by about √5 ≈ 2.2 [computed; name counts unverified]. Confidence is about **90%** that minute-level holds cannot clear costs for this system, about **70%** that one to five days is the right planning horizon, and only **40–50%** that any single published one-to-five-day effect will reach a net Sharpe of 1.0 after its documented decay. The recommendation would change if live Alpaca round trips measured 3–4 bps or less in the traded names. That would make an intraday sleeve of 150 minutes or more credible, but never minute-level holds.

## Make the evidence hard to fool before each gate

### Thirty counted trials roughly double the odds without loosening the gate (Decision 5)

The literature agrees on what counts as a trial and why counting matters. The Deflated Sharpe Ratio tests the best Sharpe against the expected maximum Sharpe of N unskilled trials. N is the effective number of independent trials and V the variance of their Sharpe estimates ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deflated_Sharpe_ratio); [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/deflated-sharpe-ratio.md)) [2+]. With five years of data, "no more than forty-five independent model configurations should be tried" ([Notices of the AMS](https://www.ams.org/notices/201405/rnoti-p458.pdf); [preprint](https://carmamaths.org/resources/jon/backtest.pdf)) [2+]. Arnott, Harvey and Markowitz ask researchers to track every model and variable tried, and warn that "iterated out-of-sample is not out-of-sample" ([Duke PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P138_A_backtesting_protocol.pdf); [Hudson and Thames](https://hudsonthames.org/experimental-design-and-common-pitfalls-of-machine-learning-in-finance/)) [2+]. Across 888 Quantopian algorithms, backtest Sharpe predicted out-of-sample Sharpe with **R² below 0.025**, and more backtesting widened the gap ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/quantopians-academic-paper-about-in-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-trading-alg/); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/big-ideas/in-sample-vs-out-of-sample-performance-of-888-trading-strategies)) [2+]. How strict to be depends on priors. Harvey, Liu and Zhu set a t-ratio hurdle of 3.0 ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2513152)) [2+]. Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen find most factors replicate ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28432); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/is-there-a-replication-crisis-in-finance/)) [2+]. The goal's explicit 5% prior is the right lever for that disagreement.

On the goal's own gate the cost of more trials is small and the gain is large. The figures assume 2,400 daily observations, a DSR of at least 0.95, a 5% prior per idea, real edges at a true net Sharpe of 1.5 and independent ideas. They come from a [computed] implementation that reproduces the DSR paper's worked example (0.9004) and the minimum-backtest-length anchor of 45 trials in 5 years.

| Counted trials | Sharpe needed to pass | Power at a true Sharpe of 1.5 | Chance of finding a real edge (null V / sample V) | Chance of a false pass |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 1.04 | 0.92 | **0.38** / 0.26 | 0.59% |
| 20 | 1.15 | 0.86 | 0.58 / 0.41 | 0.35% |
| **30** | **1.21** | 0.82 | **0.71** / 0.52 | 0.27% |
| 50 | 1.27 | 0.76 | 0.86 / 0.66 | 0.22% |

Each doubling of trials raises the bar by only about 0.1 Sharpe, while the chance of success roughly doubles from 10 to 30 trials. When every trial is counted, the false-pass rate falls as N grows. A larger counted budget also buys margin against search nobody counted: with 10 counted but 50 actually run, the false-pass rate is 3.2%; with 30 counted and 100 run, it is 1.0% [computed]. Two threats matter more than N [computed]. The first is spending runs on parameter variants: 10 runs spent on 3–4 ideas leave only a 4–18% chance of success. The second is the statistical conventions. If Newey-West autocorrelation halves the effective sample (T_eff) to 1,200 days, power at N = 10 falls from 0.92 to 0.52. And if V is the sample variance across trials, a real edge inflates its own hurdle.

**Recommendation.** Raise the counted-trial cap to **about 30 (range 20–50)**, spent on at least 10–15 distinct, pre-registered, economically motivated ideas, with N = max(cumulative count, 10). Fix the V convention and T_eff before any G1 run. One option is V = the larger of the null variance at T_eff and the cross-trial variance excluding the candidate; because this departs from the paper's literal definition, it is an owner decision. Count re-runs after bug hunts prompted by results, runs on sub-periods, runs by automated agents, and ideas an LLM suggested. Treat variants whose daily P&L correlates above about 0.7 as one idea when counting ideas, while every run still counts in N. The 0.7 echoes WorldQuant BRAIN's reported self-correlation limit ([quantt](https://www.quantt.co.uk/resources/worldquant-brain)) [secondary]. Reveal only pass/fail, the DSR and a few summary statistics from each G1 run. That limits adaptive leakage, the lesson of the reusable-holdout work ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.02629)) [2+]. Add pre-registered non-Sharpe diagnostics, since in the Quantopian data volatility, drawdown and hedging predicted better than Sharpe. Keep the stop final, but frame it as "the budget is final for this history", with no top-ups after seeing results. Continuing on the same history is statistically valid when every trial counts. The real argument for stopping is contamination that counting misses: researcher memory and designs informed by earlier G1 output. Online false-discovery methods such as LORD need independent tests and do not license more trials on one reused history ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.06197)) [2+]. Make the restart path explicit. A fresh 2,400-day G1 needs **about 9.5 years** of new data. The alternatives are a different market or universe with its own budget, newly obtained older history, or a pre-registered cumulative continuation that accepts the contamination risk.

No source found sets a research time budget. Calendar time does not spend statistical budget; trials and T_eff do. Keep about nine months as three staged checkpoints. Months 0–3 validate the harness, fix T_eff and V, and pre-register the idea backlog. Months 3–6 run about 15 trials, and months 6–9 about 15 more. The hard end is at most 12 months, with one pre-registered extension allowed on process criteria, never because a variant nearly passed. Measure idea throughput in the first stage. Below about three well-founded ideas a month, 30 trials will not fit. The current structure of four cycles of three variants, each informed by the last cycle's G1 output, is exactly the iterated out-of-sample testing the protocol literature warns against. Confidence is moderate that 10 is too few and that about 30 is the right order of magnitude, and low on the exact number and the time budget. The case rests mainly on computed power analysis using the goal's own gate parameters.

### Blind cross-vendor AI review at every gate, a paid human before real money (Decision 6)

The benchmark the goal cites has changed. **SR 11-7 and OCC Bulletin 2011-12 were rescinded on 17 April 2026 and replaced by SR 26-2 / OCC Bulletin 2026-13** ([Federal Reserve SR 26-2](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2602.htm); [OCC Bulletin 2026-13](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-13.html); [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/04/Agencies-Overhaul-Model-Risk-Management-Guidance-for-Banks-Heres-What-Changed); [Sullivan and Cromwell](https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/memo/2026/April/OCC-Fed-FDIC-Issue-Revised-Guidance-Model-Risk-Management)) [2+]. The coordinator's own searches cross-checked this against Baker Tilly, Forvis Mazars, MathWorks and Elevate Consult summaries. Effective challenge survives in the new guidance and requires "appropriate expertise, sufficient independence to permit objectivity, and organizational standing and influence to effect change". The guidance is non-binding, aimed at banks above $30 billion, and **places generative and agentic AI outside its scope**. The PRA's SS1/23 is now the stricter text on independence. Validators have no part in development and no stake in approval, keep separate reporting lines and incentives, and the approver must ensure recommendations are acted on ([SS1/23 PDF](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/prudential-regulation/supervisory-statement/2023/ss123.pdf); [Presencis](https://presencis.com/regulations/pra-ss1/article-P4/)) [2]. FINRA expects firms to govern an AI tool's reliability and accuracy ([RN 24-09](https://finra.org/sites/default/files/2024-06/regulatory-notice-24-09.pdf)) [2+]. It also suggests human review of AI outputs and logging of prompts and outputs ([Debevoise on FINRA's 2026 report](https://www.debevoise.com/insights/publications/2025/12/finras-2026-regulatory-oversight-report-continued)) [2]. No regulator found either endorses or forbids an LLM as the challenger.

LLM reviewers cover ground well but miss subtle errors. GPT-4's review comments overlapped individual human reviewers' 30.85% (Nature journals) and 39.23% (ICLR) of the time, against 28.58% and 35.25% between two humans ([Liang et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.01783)) [1 src]. AAAI-26's AI reviews of all 22,977 full-review papers were preferred for technical accuracy, but they misread equations and tables and struggled to rank which issues matter ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.13940)) [2]. On errors serious enough for errata or retraction, the best model reached only **21.1% recall and 6.1% precision**, and rarely found the same error twice across eight runs ([SPOT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11855v1); [EleutherAI](https://www.eleuther.ai/papers-blog/when-ai-co-scientists-fail-spot-a-benchmark-for-automated-verification-of-scientific-research)) [2+]. LLM critics caught more inserted bugs than paid human contractors, and human-plus-critic teams kept that recall while inventing fewer bugs ([CriticGPT](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00215v1)) [2]. The biases matter most for a reviewer of the builder's own family. Judges favour their own text, in proportion to how well they recognize it ([NeurIPS 2024](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/7f1f0218e45f5414c79c0679633e47bc-Abstract-Conference.html)) [2]. They favour models like themselves, and errors grow more correlated as capability rises ([ICML 2025, Goel et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/goel25b.html)) [2]. Two models agree on about 60% of the questions both get wrong, more so with a shared developer ([ICML 2025, Kim et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/kim25e.html)) [2]. Assistants are sycophantic ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548v1)) [2+], and text hidden in the material under review can steer them ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06185v1); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/07/07/scholars_try_to_fool_llm_reviewers/)) [2]. A panel of smaller judges from disjoint model families beat a single GPT-4 judge at over seven times less cost ([PoLL](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.18796v2)) [2]. Multi-agent debate does not reliably beat simple ensembling ([ICML 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/smit24a.html)) [1 src].

The JPMorgan "London Whale" case shows that standing, not insight, is what fails. The model review group spotted the spreadsheet problems, then approved the VaR model on conditions that were never carried out, under pressure from the trading side ([SOA Financial Reporter](https://www.soa.org/493734/globalassets/assets/library/newsletters/financial-reporter/2015/june/fr-2015-iss101-chan.pdf); [IFR](https://www.ifre.com/story/1744757/derivatives-jpm-report-unveils-var-errors)) [2+]. In a one-person system, standing has to come from rules fixed in advance.

**Recommendation.** At G1 and G2, use a panel of two or three AI reviewers, each blind and working alone, with at least two from vendors other than the builder's. For a Claude builder that means at least one GPT-family and one Gemini-family reviewer; a non-Fable Anthropic model may be a third member, and a Fable model never may. Each reviewer gets the pre-registered criteria, design documents, code, data lineage, the complete variant registry and raw logs. None of the builder's narrative or self-assessment goes in, and all evidence is to be treated as data. Machine-checked reproduction of the DSR, the out-of-sample results and the lag audit runs alongside the panel. Findings are pooled by union, not majority vote, and each must point to an artefact and a step to reproduce it. Plant known flaws periodically to measure the panel's recall, such as a one-bar look-ahead, survivorship or an unregistered variant. Before G3, add one fixed-fee human expert review once the capital at risk is material. If only one human review can be bought, spend it on the G1 statistics, because G2 checks fidelity, not edge. The owner rules, under four rules. First, every finding gets a written disposition: fix, accept with a compensating control, or reject with evidence. Second, a blocking finding, one that invalidates a gate metric, cannot be waived by the owner alone; it needs a fix and re-review, or agreement from a second independent reviewer. Third, conditions carry deadlines that are verified before the next gate. Fourth, everything is logged.

The human review's price is thinly evidenced. Senior quant work appears to run about $150–$300 or more an hour ([goLance](https://golance.com/hiring/best-freelance-financial-analysts-hourly-rate)) [vendor marketplace], so 10–40 hours costs about $1,500–$12,000 [inference]. Bank framework validations typically cost $75,000 or more ([RMA](https://www.rmahq.org/blogs/2021/cost-is-the-biggest-challenge-to-validate-vendor-models?gmssopc=1)) [1 src]. A review pays when P × L > C, where P is the chance it catches a gate-invalidating error the panel missed, L the loss avoided and C its cost. With P at 10–20% and C at $2,000–$6,000, the reviewer note's illustrative 20% loss limit puts the break-even at $50,000–$300,000 of capital at risk. Using the goal's own 10% drawdown line instead doubles that to about **$100,000–$600,000** [computed, illustrative]. Below it, a small G3 allocation with hard loss limits plus the AI panel is the cheaper challenge, and the human review is bought before scaling up. Confidence is about **85%** that a cross-vendor blind panel beats one same-family instance, **80%** on the owner ruling under pre-committed blocking rules, **65%** on AI-only review at G1 and G2 with a human before real money, and **50%** on the exact capital threshold.

### Only point-in-time models may shape the forecast G1 judges (Decision 9)

Every study that tested for it finds that language models memorize pre-cutoff financial facts, and that instructions and masking do not stop it. Models recall exact pre-cutoff values of economic variables, they reconstruct masked firms and dates from minimal context, and they cannot recall data after their cutoff ([Lopez-Lira, Tang and Zhu](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390924734_The_Memorization_Problem_Can_We_Trust_LLMs'_Economic_Forecasts); [Gao, Jiang and Yan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23847)) [2]. Prompting-based fixes have problems in Sarkar and Vafa's tests ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4754678)) [2]. In Levy's study, GPT-4's earnings-prediction accuracy **fell from 60% to chance when only the least significant digit of each statement item was randomized** ([Journal of Accounting Research](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/1475-679x.70058)) [1 src]. Forecast accuracy concentrates where a model shows look-ahead propensity, and that link vanishes after the cutoff ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23847)) [1 src].

The size of the leak depends on use. For a frozen text encoder feeding a trained head it is small: ChronoBERT's Sharpe of 4.80 sits next to Llama 3.1's 4.90 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.21206v1)) [1 src]. For generative or agentic use it is large. A point-in-time instruction model reached a Sharpe of 0.95 against 1.53–1.76 for larger unconstrained models ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11677)) [1 src]. LLM trading agents lost **51–62% of their backtested Sharpe and 50–72% of their return** after their base model's cutoff ([Profit Mirage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07920)) [1 src]. Vendors' stated cutoffs are also unreliable: behaviorally effective cutoffs span 22 months across vendors and fall up to 8 months before the stated date ([HindsightBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18867)) [1 src, single-author preprint].

For a generative "slower thinking" model, about half of an in-window Sharpe can be leakage, the same size as G1's margin. An apparent Sharpe of 1.5 that is half leaked is a true 0.75, below the KR1.2 floor of 1.0. KR6.4's one-bar lag audit cannot see a leak that lives in the model's weights, and the "Sharpe above 4" audit may never fire [inference]. Inside the G1 window, a 2025–2026 cutoff leaves only 80–450 usable days, with power of about 4% ([project review Q-3](/home/user/AIresume/trading/goal/reviews/quant-statistics.md)) [local].

Clean alternatives now exist. Point-in-time model families are trained only on text dated before each cutoff. TiMaGPT ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18543v1)) and ChronoBERT/ChronoGPT are earlier examples. DatedGPT offers twelve 1.3-billion-parameter vintages with cutoffs from 2013 to 2024, and its instruction-tuned version reports a look-ahead-free Sharpe of 3.20 ([alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.11838v1)) [1 src]. Kelly, Malamud, Schwab and Xu scale point-in-time models to 4 billion parameters on a trillion tokens, with embeddings close to full-sample ones ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35247)) [1 src]. Further pre-training a modern model on old data does not remove its knowledge of the future ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18543v1); [ACL](https://preview.aclanthology.org/dashboard/2024.findings-naacl.208)) [2]. Inference-time unlearning is partial and works only on open-weight models. FinCAD cut in-sample returns by up to 67.1% and raised the in-sample to 2025 Sharpe rank correlation only from 0.779 to 0.846 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24564)) [1 src]. Generic time-series foundation models forecast daily returns poorly and lose to gradient-boosted trees ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18578)) [1 paper, several versions], so excluding them costs little.

A forward-only proof takes years. The minimum track record to show that a realized Sharpe of 1.5 exceeds zero at 95% is 305 days, and a strategy at exactly 1.5 falls short half the time. Reaching 80% power takes **697 days (2.8 years)**, and 3.5–4.1 years with the autocorrelation that multi-day holds cause. Counting 10–100 variants takes 4.6–7.8 years, and showing the Sharpe beats 1.0 takes 11–25 years. G2's 120 days give about 27% power. These are [computed] and match the project's [goaltests/stats.py](/home/user/AIresume/trading/goal/goaltests/stats.py) to the day. Even genuinely clean signals fade: a GPT-4 news strategy's Sharpe fell from 6.54 in late 2021 to 1.22 by early 2024 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07619)) [1 src].

**Recommendation.** Use point-in-time models only. Reword KR6.3: "No model shapes the forecast for a decision date unless all of its training data predates that date by a margin. That includes pre-training data, fine-tuning data, instruction data and any teacher model's data. Each model's cutoff is documented and checked by a behavioral audit (perplexity probing, a look-ahead-propensity recall test or a date-manipulation test)." This admits the system's own walk-forward models and audited point-in-time vintages and excludes current frontier LLMs and generic time-series models. LLM-extracted data that feeds the forecast counts as shaping it. LLM-suggested ideas are logged as variants. The model G1 judges must be the model that trades. A frontier-LLM forecast can come only through a separate forward gate: a pinned, preferably open-weight model version, pre-registered sequential boundaries, and about four to five years. LLMs remain free for monitoring, operations, explanation and engineering. Confidence is about **85%** that frontier LLMs and generic time-series models must not shape G1's forecast, about **65%** that the point-in-time allowance is worth its audit cost, and about **90%** that a forward proof takes several years.

## Contain faults in seconds, and trade outside the session only to cut risk

### Tiered automatic containment should replace the 15/60-minute timer (Decision 7)

Regulators model monitoring as automatic alerts followed by immediate remedial action. Acknowledgement is not the control. RTS 6 Article 16 requires a process "to take remedial action as soon as possible after an alert has been generated, including, where necessary, an orderly withdrawal from the market" ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2017/589/body); [Better Regulation](https://service.betterregulation.com/document/370444)) [2+]. Article 16(5) requires real-time alerts **within five seconds of the relevant event**. The coordinator confirmed this directly against the legislation.gov.uk text of Article 16 and the FCA handbook. That settles a conflict the alert research left open and shows the goal's C7 describes its deviation correctly. Article 12's kill function must "immediately" cancel unexecuted orders. It cancels; it does not liquidate ([legislation.gov.uk, Art 12](https://legislation.gov.uk/eur/2017/589/article/12/2020-12-31?view=plain)) [2]. SEC Rule 15c3-5 places real-time controls on the broker with market access, here Alpaca, including that surveillance staff receive "immediate post-trade execution reports" ([govinfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/fr/75/69826)) [2+]. Industry guides treat kill switches and cancel-on-disconnect as order cancellation and ask for a documented process to re-enable trading ([FIA guide](https://matbarofex.com.ar/documentos/mpi/fia-guide); [FIA Europe](https://www.fia.org/sites/default/files/2019-05/FIA-Europe-MiFID-II-Minimum-Standard-Recs-for-ETD-eTrading-Algo-Conformance-Statement-on-Materiality-Included-0.pdf)) [1 src each].

Knight Capital shows why acknowledgement timers measured in minutes protect nothing against a malfunction. Before the 9:30 open, its system sent 97 automated emails starting at about 8:01 a.m. They were "not designed to be system alerts", and staff generally did not review them ([Docsumo, quoting the SEC order](https://newsletter.docsumo.com/p/97-emails-nobody-read); [SEC order](https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/admin/2013/34-70694.pdf)) [secondary]. The malfunction then lost **about $461.1 million in about 45 minutes** ([Knight Form 425 exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001060749/000119312512513608/d457296dex21.htm)) [2+], roughly $10 million a minute. At that rate a 15-minute window allows about $150 million, and a 60-minute window outlasts the whole event [computed]. A pre-open gate that refuses to start while any critical alert is unacknowledged would have prevented all of it [inference].

Alpaca's own interface makes the current rule fragile. Two Alpaca-published SDKs agree on several points ([alpaca-py 0.44.0](https://pypi.org/project/alpaca-py/0.44.0/); [alpaca-trade-api 5.0.0](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@alpacahq/alpaca-trade-api/v/5.0.0)) [issuer-only]. "Cancel all orders" also cancels the protective stops. Closing all positions with cancel_orders=true cancels first and then liquidates. A per-symbol close exists, and a suspend_trade setting blocks new orders. Neither SDK has a cancel-on-disconnect or dead-man's-switch endpoint, although several crypto venues offer one ([CCXT](https://pypi.org/project/ccxt/4.5.88/)); this is evidence of absence, not a statement by Alpaca. Day orders sent after the close queue for the next session, and stops cannot execute outside the regular session. Three consequences follow. Under a 60-minute timer, **any alert after about 15:00 lands its liquidation after the close, at the next open**. A "cancel everything" response strips the stops. And the goal's second kill path, "the broker's own controls", is at Alpaca the owner acting by hand. The timers also overlap with existing controls: KR4.4 and C5 already stop entries within 60 seconds for most alert types C7 lists. The 15-minute step really governs only a single rejected order and a limit reaching 0.8 of its threshold.

**Recommendation.** Adopt the tiered rule above, supported by three controls. First, an independent watchdog on another host with its own API key, because Alpaca offers no dead-man's switch. Second, a pre-open gate that will not start trading while a critical alert is unacknowledged. Third, a drill of the no-acknowledgement path with the existing kill-switch drill every 20 trading days. Unclassified alerts default to the integrity class. If the owner prefers a single timer, the smallest repair adds three things to 15/60: stop entries at once for integrity alerts, close at once anything not covered by a broker-confirmed stop, and impose a 15:45 cutoff with no close orders outside the session. Confidence is **high** on the direction (immediate automatic containment, conditional liquidation through the broker, none outside regular hours). It is **medium-high** on replacing the unconditional close with a conditional close and a pre-close cutoff, **medium** on keeping 60 minutes for verified holds, and **low to medium** on the 5-, 10-, 15:45 and 09:45 numbers. On-call norms from Google SRE and paging vendors could not be verified. Alpaca behaviours to confirm on paper are the order type close-all uses, whether suspend_trade blocks close-all, and how bracket legs behave on partial fills.

### A narrow, later exception for risk-reducing limit orders, never overnight yet (Decision 8)

Alpaca now supports extended and overnight sessions, but with no automatic protection outside 09:30–16:00. Alpaca offers trading from 20:00 ET Sunday to 20:00 ET Friday, in paper as well as live ([Alpaca docs: 24/5](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/245-trading); [Alpaca on X](https://x.com/AlpacaHQ/status/2016526691892670801); [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/02/259268-alpaca-introduces-24-5-trades-for-trading-api-providing-almost-round-the-clock-access/)) [2+ that it launched]. Outside the regular session it accepts only limit orders, time-in-force day or gtc, with extended_hours=true ([Alpaca API reference](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/postorder); [AlgoMax](https://algomax.ai/blog/alpaca-extended-hours-trading-bots)) [2+]. Bracket legs give no protection there ([Alpaca docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/orders-at-alpaca)) [issuer-only], and stops trigger only in the regular session [secondary]. Overnight orders go to Blue Ocean ATS with indicative quotes and 15-minute-delayed trades, and an overnight day order stays live until 20:00 the next day [issuer-only].

Trading costs much more outside the session. After-hours quoted and effective spreads are three to four times regular-hours levels ([Barclay and Hendershott, JF](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2004.00646.x)) [conflict: a Nasdaq summary says four to five times]. Overnight retail effective spreads run about three times and price impact about six times regular hours ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/looking-all-day-data-24-hour-trading)) [1 secondary], though the underlying paper calls costs "relatively low" ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5181159)). IOSCO finds off-hours trading mostly retail, with lower liquidity and wider spreads ([IOSCO](https://www.iosco.org/library/pubdocs/pdf/IOSCOPD820.pdf)) [2+]. For this universe's spreads of 10 bps or less, that implies roughly 30–60 bps outside the session [computed extrapolation].

The decisive fact is that an exit cannot beat the gap. About **95–96% of earnings releases come outside regular hours** ([Lyle et al.](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/imce-uploads/LYLE%20LRSY_April_18_2018.pdf); [Kellogg Insight](https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/traders-are-surprisingly-slow-to-respond-to-off-hours-earnings-announcements)) [2+]. Prices jump immediately after them, within milliseconds for the largest stocks, in more than 90% of after-hours announcement sessions, and post-announcement pricing has been efficient since 2016 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08962v1); [UC San Diego](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/earnings-news-cause-immediate-stock-price-jumps-sometimes-moving-whole-market)) [2+]. After hours, quotes lead and trades are sparse: a median of 16 trades follows an announcement ([JAR](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/1475-679X.12394)) [1 study]. An extended-hours exit therefore trades at the already-gapped price, paying three to six times the usual cost. Its only value is cutting exposure to developments that unfold over hours, such as macro selloffs, follow-on news and disorderly opens, and that value has not been quantified. Thin pre-markets also tend to reverse ([Perreten](https://mfc3.eventsadmin.com/Papers/ViewContribution?cid=14387&h=34E9F3229E21B084DE4A06E94CD0FC5F)) [1 src].

The overnight venue failed in the one big stress event so far. On 5 August 2024, Blue Ocean cancelled every trade between 01:45 and 03:06 ET and halted, and brokers suspended overnight trading ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/08/05/robinhood-no-24-hour-trading-monday-due-to-issue-at-third-party-venue.html); [Markets Media](https://www.marketsmedia.com/blue-ocean-ats-resumes-after-cancelling-trades/)) [2+]. FINRA fined it **$550,000** in 2026 for surveillance failures ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/media-center/newsreleases/2026/finra-fines-pictet-overseas-and-blue-ocean-ats-aml-and-supervisory); [FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/finra-fines-blue-ocean-ats-550000-over-overnight-manipulation-surveillance-failures/)) [2+]. Exchange overnight sessions are coming. The SEC approved Nasdaq's 23/5 plan on 10 April 2026, and the exchanges target **6 December 2026** ([Simpson Thacher](https://www.stblaw.com/about-us/publications/view/2026/04/29/looking-ahead-to-extended-trading-sec-approves-nasdaq-s-23-5-trading-proposal); [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260929-23x5-trading-comes-to-us-exchanges-what-firms-should-know-before-launch)) [2+]. They come with static ±20% overnight price bands and no automatic pauses ([SEC Release 34-106042](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nms/2026/34-106042.pdf)) [primary, via one summary]. Clearly-erroneous thresholds outside regular hours are double the daytime ones, 20/10/6% against 10/5/3% ([SEC exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/file/exhibit-5-8012)) [2+; not re-verified as current]. Launch dates have slipped before.

**Recommendation.** Keep the owner's rule of no orders outside 09:30–16:00 for now. Build the following exception in paper and shadow mode, and switch it on live only if it proves error-free. Scored against the opening-auction price and the first 15 minutes' VWAP, its costs must also be within expectations. Keep 20:00–04:00 closed. Revisit the overnight session only after the exchange and consolidated-tape sessions have run cleanly for about three months and Alpaca routes overnight orders to exchanges. Option B beats never trading off-hours only narrowly, because the evidence cuts both ways. For it: material news mostly arrives outside the session, stops do not work there, and under the current rule the system cannot act for **17.5 hours a day while holding up to 75% gross overnight**. Against it: the expected gain against exiting at the open is about zero, overnight realized spreads for liquidity providers turn negative within ten seconds, a sign of adverse selection ([SIFMA](https://www.sifma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/SIFMA-Roundtable-Building-the-Roadmap-to-24.7-Trading-Debrief.pdf)) [1 src], and an autonomous system would be acting in thin books while nobody watches. Confidence is about **60%** that the narrow exception beats never, about **80%** that the overnight session should stay closed, and about **90%** that any such orders must be limit-only, reduce-only and collared to the live quote. Every number in the table is a design suggestion to calibrate on Alpaca's historical extended-hours quotes, not a sourced fact.

## Real money needs more than $2,000, a dated tax plan, and a measured universe (Decision 10)

The liquidity cap is tighter than several research notes assumed, and the correction changes the capital question. The goal caps one position at **0.02%** of its stock's 20-day median daily dollar volume (max_position_adv_pct = 0.02 in [goal.toml](/home/user/AIresume/trading/goal/goal.toml)). The project chose 0.02% to keep market impact near the 1.5 bps the cost model charges ([REPORT.md, R-9](/home/user/AIresume/trading/goal/REPORT.md)) [local]. Several notes used 2%, a hundred times too loose. At 0.02%, the cap in a $20M-a-day name is **$4,000**, so a typical 4% position already meets it at $100,000 of equity, and a 10% intraday position needs at least $50M a day. At $250,000 of equity a 4% position needs at least $50M a day, and at $1 million at least $200M a day [computed, coordinator's correction]. **The universe narrows from about $100,000 of capital upward, not from the multi-million range.** G3 lets capital grow only while no liquidity limit binds on more than 5% of trades, so the volume distribution of the strategy's names will effectively set the reachable capital. No source gave the universe's volume distribution, so it has to be measured before the intended capital is fixed.

At the other end, the $2,000 floor cannot reproduce the tested sizes. A typical 4% position at $2,000 is **$80**: it cannot hold ten shares of any stock above $8. Meanwhile the universe's 10-bps spread filter removes most stocks under about $10, since a one-cent tick is 10 bps at $10 [computed]. Fractional shares cannot be shorted at Alpaca ([Alpaca docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/fractional-trading)) [issuer-only]. On the coordinator's arithmetic, a typical position holds at least about ten shares of a median-priced universe stock, keeping whole-share rounding error near 10% or less, once equity reaches **about 250 times the median share price**. That is $12,500 at a $50 median, $25,000 at $100 and $50,000 at $200; the median price is unverified and should be measured. The FINRA short-sale minimum plus a buffer for the 9% halt ($2,000 ÷ 0.91 ≈ $2,200) is not the binding term [computed]. One implication follows [inference]. Because G3 starts at no more than 10% of the intended capital, an intended capital below about ten times that floor, roughly $125,000–$500,000, cannot run G3 at tested sizes under the current rule. The owner should either set G3 at the larger of 10% of intended capital and the whole-share floor, or accept a larger first step. The paper results used for the G3 comparison should apply the same whole-share rounding. The capital note's own sizing table assumed every position carried the full 0.5% risk, which is 25% of equity at a 2% stop and far above the goal's 10% and 4% notional caps. It is superseded by these figures.

Three further corrections concern the research inputs and citations, not the goal's logic. The overnight-sizing, allocation and capital notes computed the liquidity cap at 2% of daily volume rather than the goal's 0.02%. Their conclusions that the cap "does not bind" at this size, or binds only above $0.8–$10 million, are wrong by a factor of a hundred; the corrected figures appear under Decisions 1, 2 and 10. The capital note sized every position at the full 0.5% risk, above the goal's notional caps. And the goal's controls still cite Fed SR 11-7, rescinded on 17 April 2026, where they should cite SR 26-2 / OCC Bulletin 2026-13 and PRA SS1/23. On the other side, the goal's claim that RTS 6 Article 16 asks for alerts within five seconds is confirmed, so C7's stated deviation stands.

## Conclusion

Read together, the decisions show that the goal's binding constraints are its own guardrails and evidence rules, not the parameters the owner was asked to set. The per-name cap barely moves return because open risk and overnight gross bind first. The position count stops mattering once a budget and a size floor exist. The planning horizon is dictated by the 2× entry rule, not chosen freely. The research budget's statistics are cheap, while distinct, well-founded ideas are scarce. That suggests an order of work. Fix the nine contradictions first: they are mechanical, and two of them, the drawdown arithmetic and KR6.3's wording, would otherwise make a limit or a gate unachievable as written. Then put effort where the program's odds actually move: more distinct pre-registered ideas, honest live measurement of costs, and reviewers whose blocking findings the owner cannot quietly waive. Decisions 1, 7 and 8 mostly protect against tails. Decisions 2, 3, 5 and 9 decide whether there is an edge to protect.

The other lesson is about time and money at the margins. A frontier-LLM forecast, a fresh research history and a confirmed live edge each take years to prove, not months. The realistic path therefore runs through point-in-time models, a pre-committed budget and a small but correctly sized first step with real money. Capital is squeezed from both sides. Below roughly $12,500–$50,000, whole shares distort the tested sizes; above about $100,000, the 0.02% liquidity cap begins removing the thinner names. The cheapest next steps would turn several of this report's judgment calls into measured facts. Measure the universe's volume and price distribution. Check Alpaca's close-all, suspend and bracket behaviour on the paper account. Gather survivor-free gap statistics for the actual universe.

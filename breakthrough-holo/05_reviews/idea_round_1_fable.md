# Idea round 1 (Fable): breaking the plasma-display bottleneck

Independent review, 2026-09-30. Written from physics I know, no web tools. Numbers marked **[±n×]** are my order-of-magnitude estimates; **[?]** = recalled constant, not re-checked. I read T1, R1–R4 and `plasma_model.py` first.

## 0. Where I agree and where I push back on the lab's findings

- **Line-of-sight theorem and touch lemma: correct.** I found no loophole for clean air. Two *partial* loopholes exist: the glove can be a directional emitter (E1) and room air is not "clean" (E4). Neither rescues a projector-only 4π display.
- **"Everything except plasma is dead": correct**, and I add one more no-go below (A0) that constrains plasma itself.
- **Lumen target 6–60 lm: fair.** Check: 10⁴–10⁵ voxels × 1 mm² × 25–180 cd/m² × 4π sr = 3–200 lm. Isotropic emission wastes ~half of it on the floor/ceiling; unavoidable. But the target is *film-look*, not usability: a 1 mm line at 0.5 m (7 arcmin) is comfortably legible at 2–3× background (Weber contrast ≫ 2 %), which relaxes the lumen budget 3–5× for a "usable" (not film-identical) display (E3).
- **Chemistry 10³–10⁴ m³/h: correct for a well-mixed room, wrong for the right design.** The relevant figure of merit is lm·s per NO molecule. Hot sparks: 1 lm·s/J ÷ 10¹⁷ NO/J = **10⁻¹⁷ lm·s/NO**. Well-mixed with ~25 m³/h effective removal and 20 ppb NO₂ (5×10¹⁷ m⁻³): allowed 3×10¹⁶ NO/s → needs 2×10⁻¹⁶–2×10⁻¹⁵ lm·s/NO, so 20–200× short. With source capture (D1) the allowed rate is ~10¹⁸ NO/s and hot sparks land inside the window at the low end (6–10 lm). The gap is a ventilation-geometry gap more than a physics gap.
- **Noise: the lab underestimates one term and overestimates another.** Overestimate: audible noise from a *periodic* ≥ 300 kHz pulse train with identical pulses is zero in principle; what is audible is the *modulation* (C1). Underestimate: 50–80 % of 6–60 W absorbed is 3–50 W of *ultrasound*; at 0.5 m that is ~120 dB before absorption. At 40–100 kHz this violates every public guideline (100 dB). It is survivable only if the spark spectrum sits ≥ 300 kHz where air absorbs 20–100 dB/m (C3). This is a hard argument for µJ, not mJ, kernels.
- **"Cyan is not available": partly wrong.** Hot air sparks emit the N II 500.5 nm multiplet (cyan-green, V = 0.32) plus N II 463/480 nm, O II 441 nm, N₂⁺ 428 nm and Ar II 488/496 nm (air is 0.93 % Ar); in a warm-lit room the spark's ~10 000 K continuum reads as blue-cyan by chromatic adaptation (B1, B2). Not a saturated 490 nm, but "Iron-Man-ish". Orange remains unavailable in air (B4).

## A. More visible lumens per joule, per NO and per shock joule

**A0. Two scalings that bound everything (no-go results).**
(a) *Bright needs hot.* Laser energy enters electrons; per electron, radiation rate ∝ n_ion (∝ n_e) while collisional loss to gas ∝ n_neutral. So the radiative branching ratio ∝ ionisation fraction: a weakly ionised (cold-gas) plasma cannot radiate efficiently. Check: n_e = 10¹⁷ cm⁻³, T_e = 2 eV: bremsstrahlung ~2×10² W/cm³ for ~10 ns of electron life → ~10⁻⁵ of deposited energy. That *is* the fs number. A visible-efficient plasma needs n_e ≳ 10¹⁹ cm⁻³ for ≳ 10 ns, which heats the gas to ≥ 10⁴ K, which makes NO. No "bright and chemically cold" air plasma exists.
(b) *Bright needs blast or bulk.* In a dense transient kernel the radiated fraction ≈ τ_hydro/τ_rad, τ_hydro ≈ r/c_s ~ 20 ns (100 µm, 3 eV), τ_rad ~ 100 ns → 20–30 % (matches R1). Radiation ∝ r³ but conduction loss ∝ r, so a *slowly* heated (isobaric, quiet) kernel at 1 atm radiates ≥ 10 % only above r ≈ (3κΔT/ε_v)^½ ≈ 3 mm [κ ≈ 3 W/mK, ε_v ≈ 10¹⁰ W/m³ at 12 000 K, ?]: that is an arc lamp, not a voxel. A 100 µm kernel heated over 1 µs radiates ~10⁻⁶ of its energy. ⇒ **There is no quiet-bright-small regime; the ns/ps dense spark is near the physical optimum in lm/J.** Expect ≤ 2–3× from tuning, not 10×.

**A1. µJ micro-kernels: same lm/J, far less NO/J and inaudible blast.** (a) NO forms by Zeldovich chemistry while gas sits at 2500–5000 K: τ_NO ≈ 3 ms (3000 K), ~20 µs (4000 K), ~1 µs (5000 K) [?, ±3×]. A kernel of radius r cools conductively in τ ≈ r²/χ, χ(3000 K) ≈ 10⁻³ m²/s: r = 30 µm → 1 µs, 100 µm → 10 µs, 1 mm → 1 ms. (b) Energy 30 eV/molecule (dissociate + ionise) sets the initial kernel: 1 µJ → 12 µm of original air → ~40 µm after expansion; 10 µJ → ~80 µm; 50 mJ → ~1.5 mm. The mm spark (and its shock-heated shell, R₀ = 8 mm) sits in the NO window for ~ms → full lightning yield 10¹⁷/J. A 10 µJ kernel spends ~6 µs there → NO frozen at ~10–30 % of that; 1 µJ → ~3 %. Per-voxel need: 10⁻³ lm ÷ 60 Hz = 1.7×10⁻⁵ lm·s → 6–17 µJ at 1–3 lm·s/J, so 10 µJ kernels are exactly the right size. (c) Fatal-ish: small kernels expand faster so η_rad may fall (∝ r per A0b); the recombining atom soup (N + O + M → NO, N + NO → N₂ + O) may re-form NO on the way down; needs a 0D kinetics run (extend E5 with Zeldovich + 3-body recombination on the kernel's T(t)). Claim: 3–10× gain in lm/NO **[±3×]**.

**A2. Picosecond regime (the untested middle).** (a) 10–100 ps pulses ionise via MPI/tunnel + avalanche, absorb > 85 % (unlike fs at 1–26 %), and heat electrons to 1–3 eV during the pulse (unlike fs), yet deposit 10–100 µJ, not mJ. R1 notes ps sparks radiate *less* acoustic energy than ns and centre at 90–120 kHz. (b) Industrial ps lasers exist off the shelf: 1030/1064 nm, 50–200 W, 20–200 µJ, 0.1–2 MHz. 10⁵ voxels × 60 Hz = 6×10⁶ pulses/s: 6 lasers at 1 MHz, or 1 laser + SLM 6-foci. (c) lm/J of ps kernels is unmeasured (gap G1/G2); my guess 0.3–2 lm/W **[±5×]**. Cheapest experiment in the whole programme: one ps laser, a photometer, a NOx analyser.

**A3. Seeded two-colour deposition: fs seed + 2 µm ns "heater".** (a) A sub-µJ fs pulse defines the voxel (10 µm, deterministic position); a 2 µm ns pulse at 10⁸–10⁹ W/cm² (10–100× below its own breakdown) heats it. Inverse bremsstrahlung ∝ λ²; with Spitzer e-i collisions at n_e = 10¹⁹, T_e = 2 eV the absorption length at 2 µm is ~100 µm (vs ~650 µm at 1 µm) so a 100 µm kernel absorbs most of the heater in one pass. (b) Gains: no threshold lottery (R1: ns onset is "abrupt at 55 mJ" unseeded), ~10× lower energy per voxel than unseeded ns; the heater carries 90 % of the power at a cornea-only wavelength (MPE 0.1 W/cm² CW, 1.4–1.5 or 1.8–2.6 µm) and cannot ignite plasma where no seed exists; the retinal-hazard fs beam carries ≤ 1 µJ/pulse. Same physics as UV-preionised breakdown (R1) and fs+ns coupling (JAP 2025). (c) No gain in lm/J (A0); two co-aligned scanners; 2 µm ns sources at 100 kHz+ are Tm/Ho fibre (fine) but 10 µm CO₂ is diffraction-limited to ≥ 30 µm spots (acceptable for 1 mm lines).

**A4. Pulse format tweaks that are real but small.** Spectral-phase shaping ×1.8 (R1). 532 nm needs 37 % less energy than 1064 (R1) but is worse for eyes. Elongated (filament) kernels are *worse* (τ_hydro set by the thin dimension). Double pulses re-heating an expanded kernel radiate less (n_e² falls 10³× while V rises 30×). Net: ≤ 2× **[±2×]**.

**A5. Killed on inspection.** Microwave/RF sustainment: in air the sustaining field is set by attachment balance at E/N ≈ 100 Td ≈ 3 MV/m, i.e. ~the breakdown field, 5×10⁴× the ICNIRP public limit (61 V/m at 2.45 GHz), 10⁹× in power density. Laser-sustained plasma: A0b (needs mm ball, kW). N₂ Lewis–Rayleigh afterglow (orange 580 nm from N + N recombination): B-state quenched by O₂ at 1 atm (k ~ 3×10⁻¹⁰ cm³/s → yield 10⁻⁴) and every N atom ends as NO.

## B. Colour at a point in ordinary air

**B1. Use the lines air already has.** (a) Hot-spark line list with visible weight: N II 500.5 nm (cyan-green, strongest visible line in air LIBS at 0.2–1 µs delay), N II 463.0/480.3/567.9, O II 441, N₂⁺ 391/428, Ar II 488/496 (0.93 % Ar), Hα 656 (humidity). N II upper levels sit ~21–23 eV above N⁺ ground → population ∝ exp(−22/T_e): 5×10⁻⁴ at 3 eV, 10⁻² at 5 eV. (b) Hot phase (T_e 3–8 eV) = white continuum + cyan/blue lines; cooler phase (1–1.5 eV) = Hα + N I/O I NIR + weaker continuum → redder. Pulse energy/duration therefore gives a weak hue axis: hot = cyan-white, cooler = pink-white. Line share of visible ~5–20 % **[±3×]**. (c) Saturation stays low (continuum dominates the eye's integral); no green, no orange.

**B2. Chromatic adaptation: make the room warm.** (a) Von Kries adaptation moves the perceived white to the room illuminant. A ~10 000 K spark continuum (x,y ≈ 0.28, 0.29) viewed under 2700 K lamps (0.46, 0.41) lands far on the blue-cyan side of the adapted white. (b) Estimated perceived hue 200–220° at 30–50 % saturation **[?]**; film median hue 181–199°. Cost: zero; a "hologram mode" for the room lights (warm, dimmed) is exactly what the film sets look like (R4: low-key, practical lamps). (c) Still not the film's saturated cyan; orange accents impossible this way.

**B3. Colour via a long-lived "chemical ink" left by the plasma: dead.** Species surviving ms in the kernel: N (2.5 ms), O (13 µs → O₃), O₃, NO, NO₂, O₂(a¹Δ), N₂(A). Every visible fluorescence channel is quenched at 1 atm by 10³–10⁵ (NO₂ LIF yield ~10⁻⁵; N/O LIF are 2-photon UV → NIR; NO γ is UV→UV; O₂(a) emits at 1.27 µm). Air has no visible fluorophore.

**B4. Orange/red in air: only Hα and the cool continuum.** A 4000 K optically thick emitter would be orange, but air at 4000 K is ~10⁻⁵ ionised and optically thin (radiates ~nothing). Humid-air sparks give a real Hα at 656 nm (V = 0.06), maybe 2–5 % of visible energy in a cool-phase spark **[±3×]**; enough for a pinkish tint, not an accent colour. ⇒ Orange needs matter: the glove (E1) or particles (F2).

**B5. Perceptual colour tricks: weak.** Benham/Fechner pattern-induced flicker colours need 5–15 Hz flicker (desaturated, viewer-dependent, epilepsy-adjacent; WCAG ≤ 3 flashes/s). Purkinje shift (mesopic viewing, E3) makes violet/blue N₂⁺ light ~10–20× more effective and pushes everything toward blue-grey-cyan; useful, but it means a dim room.

**B6. Scattering a coloured probe beam off the plasma/density hole: dead.** Thomson: 2.5×10¹⁰ electrons × 6.7×10⁻²⁵ cm² = 2×10⁻¹⁴ cm² total. Density-hole refraction: phase object of φ ≲ 0.5 rad, 100 µm across, scatters into a ~5 mrad cone (≈ 10⁻⁶ of 4π). Both confirm the lab's "thermal lensing" verdict.

## C. Audible noise from pulsed micro-sparks

**C1. Constant-flux scanning: make the source periodic, so the audible band is empty by construction.** (a) A pulse train at f_rep ≥ 300 kHz with identical pulse energies has a line spectrum at n·f_rep; single-pulse shape only sets the envelope. Audible sound comes from three modulations: (i) energy variation (grey scale, blanking), (ii) rate variation (frame gaps), (iii) geometry: arrival time = t_i + r_i/c. For a *smooth* trajectory (iii) is Doppler FM of the comb by ṙ/c ≤ (60 m/s)/(343 m/s) = 17 % → stays ultrasonic; for random-access voxel jumping (SLM hopping across 1 m) the 3 ms arrival jitter smears the comb into broadband noise, part of it audible. (b) Thermoacoustic hum from a heat-deposition fluctuation ΔH at frequency f: Q = (γ−1)ΔH/(γp₀) = 2.9×10⁻⁶ m³/s per W; monopole power P = ρω²Q²/(8πc) ≈ 4.6×10⁻¹⁴ f² ΔH² W. ΔH = 1 W at 1 kHz → 36 dB SPL at 1 m; 10 W at 1 kHz → 56 dB; 1 W at 60 Hz → 11 dB (+A-weighting −26 dB). Design rules: never blank (unused pulses go to a muffled in-projector decoy or the frame is 100 % drawn); grey scale by *revisit density*, not pulse energy; smooth raster order; pulse-energy jitter ≤ 1 % RMS spreads 0.1 W over 250 kHz → ~12 dB in the 1–5 kHz band. Residual ≲ 30 dB(A) looks feasible **[±10 dB]**. (c) Content is what it is: brightness edges in the scene become ΔH at scan-crossing rates unless the density-graded scheme is strict; the ultrasonic power itself (C3) is untouched.

**C2. Co-located subwoofer cancels the frame comb globally.** (a) For f < ~300 Hz the whole 1 m volume is acoustically compact (λ > 1 m), so the 60/120/180 Hz hum is one monopole; a loudspeaker in the projector base driven with −Q(t) (known exactly from the render schedule) cancels it in all directions, not just at one ear. (b) Needed volume velocity ~3×10⁻⁶ m³/s per W of modulation: trivial for a 10 cm driver. 20–30 dB reduction below 300 Hz is standard ANC performance. (c) Useless above ~500 Hz (volume not compact; cancellation becomes zonal). Ultrasonic content: impossible (λ ~ 1 mm, 4π broadband).

**C3. Push the blast into the self-absorbed band by using small kernels.** (a) Blast centre frequency f ~ c/(2R₀), R₀ = (E/p₀)^⅓: 50 mJ → 8 mm → ~20 kHz (audible, ~the 77 dB Fairy Lights buzz); 10 µJ → 0.46 mm → ~370 kHz; 1 µJ → 0.22 mm → ~800 kHz. Classical air absorption ∝ f²: 22 dB/m (370 kHz), ~100 dB/m (800 kHz) [ISO 9613 extrapolated, R2]. (b) 6–10 W of blast at 370 kHz: 122 dB at 0.5 m free-field, −11 dB absorption → ~110 dB; at 2 m → ~70 dB. No exposure guideline exists above 100 kHz (R3 N6); it cannot couple into tissue through air (impedance 10⁴ mismatch) and heating is ~1 W/m² ≪ solar. The hot bubble also shrinks on cooling, so the single-kernel spectrum falls as f² below 1/(2πτ_cool) (16 kHz for 10 µs kernels): −26 dB at 3 kHz, −40 dB at 1 kHz relative to peak. (c) Shock formation distance for 120 dB at 500 kHz is ~0.7 m, so nonlinear self-demodulation happens; for a periodic train it produces only DC and harmonics, but any residual AM is demodulated to audible sound (parametric-array effect). This must be measured, not assumed.

**C4. Slow (isobaric) heating: silent but dark.** (a) Depositing energy over τ_dep ≫ r/c_s (1 µs for a 50 µm kernel vs 150 ns transit) cuts blast efficiency roughly as (r/cτ_dep)² ≈ 0.02 → 50× less shock. (b) But per A0b such a kernel radiates ~10⁻⁶ of its energy (conduction wins). (c) Fatal for a light source; useful only for haptic/decoy pulses that need no light.

**C5. Spectral dither and psychoacoustics.** Randomising scan order between frames turns the 60 Hz comb into a noise floor (tonal penalty in ISO 1996 is 3–6 dB; annoyance drops). Stationary broadband at 30 dB(A) is "quiet office"; a 60 Hz comb at 30 dB(A) is a "hum". Worth 3–6 dB perceptually, zero physically.

## D. Cutting or removing NO/O₃

**D1. Capture at the source: the image volume is a downdraft hood, the projector base is a catalytic scrubber.** (a) Instead of diluting 10¹⁸ NO/s into 50 m³ and scrubbing the room, pull 0.2–0.3 m/s of downward flow through the 1 m² footprint (fume-hood face velocities are 0.3–0.5 m/s; open downdraft tables capture 80–95 %). The plasma is ns-scale and does not care about 0.3 m/s air; hands do not either. (b) Flow 0.25 m³/s = 900 m³/h at ~100 Pa across a bed → 25 W aerodynamic; a 0.5 m low-rpm fan can do this at 30–40 dB(A) **[±5 dB]**. Room balance with leak fraction ε ≈ 0.1 and the bed also cleaning what it ingests (CADR ≈ 800 m³/h ≫ V·λ = 25 m³/h): C = εP/CADR → for 20 ppb NO₂ (5×10¹⁷ m⁻³): P ≤ 1.1×10¹⁸ NO/s ≈ 11 W of hot-spark absorption ≈ 10–30 lm. With A1 (3–10×) → 30–100 lm. That is the target. Bed chemistry at room temperature: MnO₂/CuO (hopcalite) destroys O₃ > 95 % single-pass; NO is the weak link (poorly adsorbed) → KMnO₄-on-alumina oxidises NO → NO₂ and captures it, backed by KOH/K₂CO₃-impregnated carbon. Consumption: 10¹⁸ NO/s = 6 mmol/h = 0.28 g NO₂/h → a 1 kg cartridge at 5–10 wt % lasts 200–400 h (a "toner" consumable). (c) Everything hinges on single-pass NO capture ≥ 80 % at ~0.2 ppm and 0.1 s contact — vendor data exist for HVAC media, not verified here; heating the bed to 200 °C (Pt route) costs 54 kW at this flow and is out. Capture efficiency in an *open* room with people moving is the other risk; an air curtain around the footprint helps.

**D2. Make O₃ instead of NO: forbidden by A0a.** Cold plasmas make O₃ (easy to scrub, 3.5×10¹⁷/J at corona yield) but radiate 10⁻⁵ lm/W. Warm (500–1500 K, ms) plasmas destroy O₃ and make no NO, but also make no light. The radiative branching ratio ∝ ionisation fraction ties light to heat.

**D3. Kinetic freeze-out inside the kernel (companion to A1).** (a) In a fully dissociated kernel (T > 8000 K) NO_eq is small; NO forms on the way down through 2500–4500 K. If cooling through that window is ≪ 20 µs, Zeldovich is starved; the atoms then recombine at low T where N + N + M (k ~ 10⁻³² cm⁶/s [?]) and N + NO → N₂ + O (3×10⁻¹¹ cm³/s) compete with N + O + M → NO. (b) At [N] ~ 10¹⁸ cm⁻³ the N-consuming channels run in ~4 µs and ~30 ns, faster than dilution (~10–100 µs) and far faster than N + O₂ → NO + O (2.5 ms). Plausible outcome: N → N₂, O → O₃ (scrubbable). Gain 3–10× **[±3×, needs the E5 kinetics run]**. (c) Three-body N + O + M has a similar rate to N + N + M; the split is a real kinetics question, not a hand-wave.

**D4. Exposure-time accounting.** WHO NO₂ is 200 µg/m³ (106 ppb) for 1 h vs 25 µg/m³ (13 ppb) for 24 h. A hologram session is minutes to an hour, not 24 h. Designing to ≤ 50 ppb during use and ≤ 13 ppb daily average (device off most of the day) buys 2–4× legitimately; also log and display the room NO₂ (a $20 electrochemical sensor) and throttle brightness against it.

**D5. Duct-side NO oxidation by the plasma's own O₃ is too slow to rely on:** NO + O₃ (k = 1.8×10⁻¹⁴ cm³/s) at 50 ppb O₃ takes ~50 s; at 10 ppm injected O₃ it takes 0.2 s but costs ~180 W of ozoniser. Prefer the sorbent route (D1).

## E. Loopholes, missed mechanisms, vision science, the glove

**E1. The glove as a light-field emitter (a real but narrow loophole in the touch lemma).** (a) The lemma fails surface displays because the hand is the "first opaque surface" for rays through P. If the hand's own surface emits *directionally* (a glove with micro-lens-array light-field patches, or simpler: 2–3 tracked viewers × per-viewer view-multiplexed emission), each eye sees the correct hologram colour on the glove where P projects onto it. (b) 2–3 viewers, head-tracked by the projector's cameras, need only 2–3 views per glove pixel at ~2 mm pitch: ~10⁴ pixels × 3 views — a wearable that exists in pieces today. (c) It fixes occlusion *for the hand only*; it does not create light at P for anything else. Its value here: orange/red *held* objects (B4) and the "grabbed part" glowing in the hand at full colour.

**E2. Retinal/cortical, retro-reflective, wall-painting, mirror-at-P schemes: dead**, for the reasons in T1. I re-derived that any surface behind P, including retro-reflective ceilings, is a screen. A ceiling luminaire that is also a light-field panel is still an aperture (0.14 % of 4π).

**E3. Vision-science levers (cheap, real, ~10–100×).** (a) Lumens needed scale with background luminance: 100 lux → 1 lux cuts the requirement 100× (0.06–0.6 lm) and moves viewing into the mesopic range where V′(λ) peaks at 507 nm (cyan!) and 400–430 nm light gains 10–20× (Purkinje). (b) Legibility vs film look: 2–3× background is legible, the film's 5–12× is glow. Broca–Sulzer (brief flashes look brighter) gives ≤ 1.5–2× for 20–50 ms pulses, nothing for ns flashes (Bloch's law). Together: a dim, warm room (E3 + B2) is worth 30–300× on the chemistry/noise budget, more than any plasma trick. (c) It is a room requirement; the films are indeed low-key (R4) but a "50–150 lux" spec was chosen.

**E4. "Ambient air" contains 10³–10⁴ particles/cm³ (≥ 0.3 µm): trap the room's own dust.** (a) A photophoretic trap (OTD) holds one ambient 0.5–2 µm particle with < 25 mW at 405 nm; 1 W of RGB on it (10⁴ W/cm² at 100 µm) scatters ~4×10⁻⁵ W ≈ 5×10⁻³ lm — five voxels' worth per particle, full colour, no added medium under a literal reading of R3. (b) 10–60 m of stroke per 60 Hz frame at 1.5 m/s per particle needs 400–2400 controlled particles; the record is 1 (photophoretic) and 25 (acoustic). Dead by 10²–10³ in control, and the visible illumination beams are Class 4 and visible in a dim room. Keep for a *few* colour accents (F2).

**E5. Plasma at P but light from elsewhere: dead.** Coherent scattering from the kernel (B6), Cherenkov/X-ray air fluorescence (same N₂ bands, plus dose), Raman/anti-Stokes (10⁻¹³ suppression, R4): all confirmed dead.

**E6. Haptics via the glove only.** Plasma-shock and ultrasonic haptics both add exposure (160 dB foci, 100 µm ablation). A glove with piezo/LRA actuators (1–20 ms latency) plus camera tracking is safer and better, and the owner allows it. Delete plasma haptics from the safety budget.

## F. Grey zone: projector-supplied, recovered matter

**F1. A sparse particle cloud is fog: dead by extinction.** To have a scatterer inside every addressed 1 mm³ voxel you need ≥ 10⁹ particles/m³; at 50 µm size that is n·σ ≈ 2 m⁻¹ (opaque). Invisibility (n·σ·L ≲ 0.05 over 2 m) caps the cloud at ~2×10⁷ m⁻³, one particle per 50 mm³. Particles must be *brought* to P (trapped), never scattered as a cloud.

**F2. Hybrid: plasma for the 90 % cyan line art, a handful of trapped particles for accents.** (a) 5–20 photophoretically or acoustically trapped 10 µm cellulose/PS particles, RGB-lit, each drawing ~2.5 cm of line per 60 Hz frame (1.5 m/s) → 0.1–0.5 m of orange/green/red stroke per frame: callouts, selection highlights, the "grabbed part". (b) Power ~1 W per particle of visible light; the particles are held, not released (a lost 10 µm cellulose particle is ordinary dust); haptics unaffected (traps break when a hand enters, re-acquired after). (c) Multi-particle trapping at 5–20 is a research result away (Bond 2023, Marzo 2019); trap beams cross the volume (safety); accents lag the plasma content at 10 Hz POV.

**F3. Noble-gas jet (Xe, Ar) into the voxel region: forbidden and bad.** Xe I 460–500 nm lines are the cyan you want (SIGGRAPH 2024 used a Xe cell for exactly this) but "no gases" is explicit; a recovered laminar Xe curtain would cost ~$10³/h and be visible as schlieren.

**F4. Micro-drones / tethered emitters: not a projector; skip.**

## Top 3 by (probability it works) × (impact)

1. **D1 — source capture + room-temperature scrubber (P ≈ 0.7, impact: removes the 20–200× chemistry gap).** Downdraft at 0.25 m/s through the image footprint into a hopcalite + KMnO₄/alumina + KOH-carbon cartridge; room NO₂ ≤ 20 ppb at ~10¹⁸ NO/s (≈ 11 W absorbed ≈ 10–30 lm). Risk: NO single-pass capture at 0.2 ppm; open-room capture efficiency; fan noise budget.
2. **C1+C2+C3 — constant-flux acoustics (P ≈ 0.6, impact: turns "far above 35 dB(A)" into "≈ 30 dB(A)").** ≥ 300 kHz periodic pulse train, µJ kernels (self-absorbed ultrasound), density-coded grey scale, smooth raster, subwoofer cancellation of the < 300 Hz frame comb. Risk: nonlinear self-demodulation of any residual AM; unregulated 110 dB ultrasound at 0.5 m needs a measurement campaign.
3. **B2+B1+E3 — warm dim room + hot-spark cyan lines (P ≈ 0.8, impact: "cyan not available" → "cyan-ish available", and 30–300× on every other budget).** Zero-cost, but it is a room spec: 2700 K lamps at ≤ 20–50 lux.
(4th, and the biggest physics bet: **A1/A2/A3 — ps or fs+2 µm seeded 10 µJ micro-kernels**, P ≈ 0.4, 3–10× on lm/NO plus eye-safety and threshold control; first experiment is one industrial ps laser plus a photometer and a NOx analyser.)

## Verdict

An Iron-Man-quality display in the strict sense (room-scale, saturated cyan with orange accents, 25–180 cd/m² in a 100 lux room, silent, everyday-safe, projector-only) cannot be engineered with known physics in ordinary air: the two scalings in A0 tie visible light to hot dense transient plasma, which ties it to NO and to blast, and air has no colour channel other than white-blue tinted by N II/N₂⁺ lines and Hα. What *is* achievable, with every number above at ±3–5×: a ceiling-mounted ps (or fs-seeded 2 µm) engine drawing ~10⁴ one-millimetre voxels at 60 Hz in a ~0.5–1 m³ downdraft volume at 5–30 lm, looking cyan-white under warm ≤ 50 lux lighting, ≤ 30–35 dB(A) with a periodic ≥ 300 kHz train, room NO₂ held ≤ 20 ppb by a consumable cartridge, hands inside (glove haptics, occlusion-aware scheduling), with orange/red accents only on the glove or on a few trapped particles (grey zone). That is a "Fairy Lights at 100× scale in a dim lab", not Tony Stark's workshop. The non-physics blocker is larger than any of these: an open-air focus at 10¹¹–10¹⁴ W/cm² is Class 4 by construction, and IEC 60825-1 / EN 50689 do not accept presence sensing as a way down to a consumer class, so the honest product path is an enclosed (interlocked, transparent) Class 1 volume — which the owner has excluded — or a professional/exhibition variance, not a living-room product.

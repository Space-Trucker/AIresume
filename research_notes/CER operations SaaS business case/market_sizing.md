## Q1. How many CACER configurations are active (by type), with how many members and how many MW, and how fast is the number growing (2025–2026)?

### Takeaway
The count of configurations in operation roughly doubled in H1 2026, from 1,805 (31 Dec 2025) to 3,630 (30 Jun 2026). By 31 Aug 2026 more than 4,800 were "active or finalising", with about 400 MW and more than 51,000 POD. That is about 83 kW and about 10.5 POD per configuration on average. The distribution is very skewed: about 46% of configurations are 10 kW or smaller, and about 79% are 50 kW or smaller. I found no 2026 split by type (CER / group / remote self-consumer). The only split I found is older: CER about 48%, groups about 29%, remainder about 23% (probably mid-2025).

### Inferences
- **[Derived] Averages per configuration:**
  - 31 Dec 2025: 174.5 MW / 1,805 = **96.7 kW**; 18,263 / 1,805 = **10.1 POD**
  - 30 Jun 2026: 302.9 / 3,630 = **83.4 kW**; 37,821 / 3,630 = **10.4 POD**
  - 31 Jul 2026: 361 / 4,385 = **82.3 kW**; 45,502 / 4,385 = **10.4 POD**
  - 31 Aug 2026: 400 / 4,800 = **83.3 kW**; 51,000 / 4,800 = **10.6 POD**
  - The average size is falling (97 → 83 kW) because many small configurations are being added. POD per configuration is stable at about 10–11. That is small: a typical configuration has around 10 members, not hundreds.
- **[Derived] Growth:**
  - H1 2026: configurations +101% (1,805 → 3,630), MW +73.6%, POD +107%.
  - Net additions: about 304 per month in H1 2026 ((3,630 − 1,805) / 6); 755 in July; about 415 in August on the wider perimeter. That averages about 585 per month over Jul–Aug.
- **[Derived] Skew:** 46.1% of configurations are ≤10 kW, 67.4% are ≤20 kW and 79.4% are ≤50 kW. Only 477 (13.1%) exceed 100 kW, and only 319 (8.8%) exceed 200 kW. At 31 Jul 2026 the ≤10 kW share was still 45.6% (2,001 / 4,385).
  - The long tail of small configurations is probably mostly single-plant groups/condominiums and remote self-consumers. The type split is not available to confirm this.
  - The roughly 450–500 configurations above 100 kW likely carry most of the MW, and therefore most of the incentive value. They are the natural paying segment.
- **Terminology risk:** press headlines call all of these "comunità energetiche". The GSE figures are "configurazioni" (CACER, which includes groups and AID). The report should say "CACER configurations" unless a CER-only figure is quoted.

### Gaps
- No 2026 official split of the 3,630 / 4,385 / 4,800 configurations into CER vs groups vs AID. The GSE data page (gse.it) could not be fetched. The 48% / 29% split is older and its year is not confirmed.
- No official 2026 split of POD into consumers vs producers/prosumers, or of MW by technology. E&S says configurations are "mostly PV".
- No count of distinct CER legal entities, as opposed to configurations. One CER entity can hold several configurations, one per primary substation. This matters for per-client SaaS pricing.

## Q2. What is the pipeline: PNRR applications, admissions, concession agreements, MW and € granted, and deadlines?

### Takeaway
48,750 applications for the 40% PNRR capital grant were filed between 8 Apr 2024 and 30 Nov 2025. By 31 Jul 2026, 30,299 had been admitted via six concession acts, for **€779m and 1,763.6 MW**. About 18,000 applications (about 1.5 GW, about €630m) fell outside the funded envelope. Plants must enter service within 24 months of the concession agreement, and no later than **31 Dec 2027**. This is roughly 4.4 times the capacity in operation at Aug 2026, arriving in 2026–27.

### Cited Findings
- **Applications:** 48,750 requests submitted between 8 Apr 2024 and 30 Nov 2025. The measure was converted into a grant of up to 40% of eligible costs and extended to municipalities with up to 50,000 inhabitants. About 30,000 initiatives were funded using about €800m, reaching the target of about 1.8 GW. ### Inferences
- **[Derived] Admission rate:** 30,299 / 48,750 = **62.2%**. Not admitted: 48,750 − 30,299 = 18,451, consistent with the "about 18,000" reported. QualEnergia's 15,465 probably counts formally excluded applications only, leaving the rest withdrawn or pending. That is unverified.
- **[Derived] Budget used:** €779m / €2.2bn = **35.4%** of the original envelope. That is consistent with the "–64%" cut headline: the measure was re-sized to about €0.8bn.
- **[Derived] Average admitted application:**
  - Size: 1,763.6 MW / 30,299 = **58.2 kW**
  - Grant: €779m / 30,299 = **€25.7k**
  - Grant per kW: €779m / 1,763.6 MW = **€442/kW**
  - Implied eligible capex at 40%: €442 / 0.40 = **≈ €1,104/kW**
- **[Derived] Scale of the pipeline:** 1,763.6 MW admitted vs about 400 MW in operation or finalising at 31 Aug 2026, i.e. **4.4 times**, due into service by 31 Dec 2027.
  - Applications are per plant, not per configuration, and one configuration can host several plants. So 30,299 applications does **not** mean 30,299 new configurations.
  - Scenario: at 1.5–3 PNRR plants per configuration, that is about 10,000–20,000 configurations touched or created. This ratio is an assumption and is not sourced.
- **Demand signal for back-office tooling:** about 30,000 grant beneficiaries must report progress to GSE (rendicontazione) and commission plants before end-2027, while running the operational tariff flow alongside. That is a compliance workload in 2026–27 on top of the operating workload.
- **Upside:** about 18,000 unfunded applications (about 1.5 GW) can still go ahead with the tariff only, with no grant and no 50% tariff cut. Some will.

- Whether GSE published a seventh or later act after 31 Jul 2026, and what the cumulative total was in September 2026.
- How many PNRR plants are already inside the 4,800 operational configurations, as opposed to still to be built. Without this, adding the 400 MW and 1,763.6 MW risks some double counting.

## Q3. What is the tariff incentive per MWh shared, what is the ARERA valorisation, and what incentive flow does that imply per CER and nationally?

### Takeaway
Shared energy earns the MASE **tariffa premio**: a base amount plus a price-linked component, capped at **€100 / €110 / €120 per MWh** for plants **>600 / 200–600 / ≤200 kW**. PV plants get a zonal uplift of **+€4 (Centre) or +€10 (North)** per MWh. This is paid for **20 years**, within a **5 GW** contingent and for plants applying up to **31 Dec 2027**. On top, ARERA pays a **valorisation of about €9–12/MWh**. Plants with a 40% PNRR grant have their tariff cut by 50%.

With explicit assumptions:
- An average configuration (about 83 kW) earns **about €5.7k/yr**, with a range of €2.5–10.4k.
- The median configuration (≤10 kW) earns **under €700/yr**.
- National flow is **about €27m/yr today**, **about €100m/yr** once the PNRR plants are online, and **about €280m/yr** at 5 GW.

### Cited Findings
- **Tariffa premio formula (DM MASE 414/2023, "Decreto CACER"):**
  - P > 600 kW: TIP = 60 + max(0; 180 − Pz), capped at €100/MWh
  - 200 kW < P ≤ 600 kW: TIP = 70 + max(0; 180 − Pz), capped at €110/MWh
  - P ≤ 200 kW: TIP = 80 + max(0; 180 − Pz), capped at €120/MWh
  - Pz is the hourly zonal price.
  - PV zonal correction: +€4/MWh in Central regions, +€10/MWh in Northern regions.
  - Duration: 20 years from commercial operation.
  - Source: [Regione Emilia-Romagna – Tariffa premio](https://energia.regione.emilia-romagna.it/comunita-energetiche/contributi-economici-alle-cer/tariffa-premio)
- Press summary: GSE pays roughly €60–120/MWh on shared energy, up to about €0.14/kWh including zonal uplift. — [Solare Industriale](https://www.solareindustriale.it/incentivi/incentivi-cer-energia-condivisa); [Ingenio (GSE 2025 balance)](https://www.ingenio-web.it/articoli/gse-nel-2025-oltre-16-miliardi-di-incentivi-e-4-4-miliardi-gestiti-per-il-pnrr/)
- **Cumulation with the PNRR grant:**
  - The tariff can be combined with capital grants of up to 40%, with a reduction factor F that goes linearly from 0 (no grant) to 0.50 (40% grant). So TIP_reduced = TIP × (1 − F): −50% at a 40% grant, −25% at a 20% grant.
  - The reduction does **not** apply to energy attributable to withdrawal points held by local authorities, religious bodies, third-sector entities or environmental-protection bodies. It also no longer applies to natural persons.
  - No tariff is paid if the capital grant exceeds 40%.
  - Sources: [MASE FAQ – cumulabilità](https://culturaeconsapevolezza.mase.gov.it/faq/cer-e-possibile-cumulare-la-tariffa-incentivante-con-il-contributo-pnrr-o-altri-contributi); [ReteAgevolazioni](https://www.reteagevolazioni.it/comunita-energetiche-rinnovabli-incentivi/)
- **Cap and deadline:** the tariff can be requested until 30 days after 5 GW of incentivised capacity is reached, and in any case not after 31 Dec 2027. This is from DM Modifica CACER, signed 15/16 May 2025 and published 25 Jun 2025, which also extended PNRR eligibility to comuni with up to 50,000 inhabitants and raised the advance to 30%. — [Rinnovabili.it – DM modifica CACER](https://www.rinnovabili.it/energia/comunita-energetiche-rinnovabili/dm-modifica-cacer-testo/); [Unioncamere – DM text](https://www.unioncamere.gov.it/sites/default/files/Ambiente/CER/DM%20MASE%20modifica%20DM%20CACER_16.05.25.pdf)
- **ARERA valorisation (TIAD, the CACV payment):**
  - Formula: monthly self-consumed energy × (variable part of the LV transmission tariff TRASE + highest variable LV distribution component).
  - 2023: 8.48 + 0.60 = **€9.08/MWh**
  - 2024: 10.57 + 0.65 = **€11.22/MWh**
  - A more recent source cites **about €11.90/MWh**. The year is not stated; it is probably 2025 or 2026.
  - Paid for 20 years.
  - Sources: [Regione Emilia-Romagna – corrispettivo di valorizzazione](https://energia.regione.emilia-romagna.it/comunita-energetiche/contributi-economici-alle-cer/il-corrispettivo-di-valorizzazione); [Studio Madera](https://www.studiomadera.it/news/690-comunita-energetiche-2); [ARERA TIAD](https://www.arera.it/fileadmin/allegati/docs/22/727-22TIAD.pdf). Attribution of the individual yearly values between these sources could not be checked on the pages.
- **Shared-energy ratio:** E&S (Chiaroni, Sep 2026) reports average self-consumption of 45%, rising to 65% in configurations with storage. — [MalpensaNews](https://www.malpensanews.it/2026/09/ricette-per-comunita-energetiche-rinnovabili-tra-innovazione-condivisione-e-abitudini-da-cambiare/959713/)
- GSE handled more than €16bn of incentives across all schemes in 2025. I found no CACER-specific payout. — [Ingenio](https://www.ingenio-web.it/articoli/gse-nel-2025-oltre-16-miliardi-di-incentivi-e-4-4-miliardi-gestiti-per-il-pnrr/)

### Inferences
- **[Derived] The cap almost always binds.** For P ≤ 200 kW, TIP = 80 + (180 − Pz), which reaches the €120 cap whenever Pz ≤ €140/MWh. For P > 600 kW the €100 cap also binds whenever Pz ≤ €140. So in most hours the effective tariff is simply the cap: €100–120, plus €0–10 zonal uplift for PV. The €60 floor applies only when Pz ≥ €180 (large plants).
  - Modelling value, non-PNRR: **€110/MWh** as a blended base, with a range of €100–130.
  - Modelling value, PNRR-funded (−50%, ignoring exempt POD): **about €60/MWh**.
- **Modelling assumptions** (researcher's, not sourced):
  - PV specific yield: 1,200–1,350 kWh/kWp/yr, base 1,250.
  - Shared share: 35% / 45% / 65% (low / base / high), anchored on E&S's 45% and 65%.
  - ARERA valorisation: €11/MWh.
  - Tariff + ARERA: base €121/MWh (110 + 11); low €71 (PNRR-cut 60 + 11); high €142 (130 + 12).
- **[Derived] Annual incentive flow per configuration**, calculated as kW × yield × shared % × €/MWh:

  | Configuration | Low (1,200 h, 35%, €71) | Base (1,250 h, 45%, €121) | High (1,350 h, 65%, €142) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Average, 83.3 kW (Aug 2026) | 35.0 MWh → **€2.5k** | 46.9 MWh → **€5.7k** | 73.1 MWh → **€10.4k** |
  | Median band, 10 kW | 4.2 MWh → **€0.3k** | 5.6 MWh → **€0.68k** | 8.8 MWh → **€1.2k** |
  | E&S average, 97 kW | €2.9k | €6.6k | €12.1k |
  | Large, 500 kW | €14.9k | €34.0k | €62.3k |

  Worked example, base case for the average configuration: 83.3 kW × 1,250 h = 104.1 MWh produced; × 45% = 46.9 MWh shared; × €121 = €5,670 per year.
- **[Derived] National annual incentive flow (tariff + ARERA):**
  - **Today (about 400 MW, Aug 2026):** 400 MW × 1,250 h = 500 GWh; × 45% = 225 GWh; × €121/MWh = **about €27m/yr**, with a range of €12–50m.
  - **After PNRR build-out (about 2028):** add 1,763.6 MW × 1,250 h × 45% = 992 GWh × €71 = €70m. Total **about €98m/yr**. This is an upper bound if some PNRR plants are already counted in the 400 MW.
  - **At the 5 GW cap:** 5,000 MW × 1,250 h × 45% = 2,812 GWh × a blended €100 = **about €281m/yr**, i.e. about **€5.6bn over 20 years**.
- **Implication for SaaS:** the incentive "wallet" per configuration is small. Half of configurations generate under about €700/yr. A 15% management retention on a ≤10 kW configuration is about €100/yr, which cannot fund dedicated per-configuration software. The pricing has to be portfolio-based (per POD or per member) and sold to the entity running many configurations.

### Gaps
- No official GSE figure for 2025 or 2026 CACER payouts (€) or shared-energy volumes (GWh).
- The exact ARERA CACV values for 2025 and 2026 are not confirmed. €11.90/MWh is cited without a year.
- No sourced national average shared-energy ratio from GSE. The 45% figure is E&S's average "self-consumption".
- The tariff regime after 31 Dec 2027 is unknown: whether there will be a new decree or whether the cap is reached first.

## Q4. Who administers CERs (utilities, ESCos, installers, cooperatives, municipalities), how many CERs does a typical provider manage, and what management fee does the referente retain?

### Takeaway
Public bodies are the dominant promoters: 58% of CERs by end-2024 (E&S). But 79% of initiatives involve an external ESCo, utility or energy company that invests in the plants, and that party is the natural buyer of operations software. Providers range from national utilities (Sorgenia, Hera, Iren, etc.) and cooperatives (ènostra: more than 30 CERs, about 20,000 members) to aggregator foundations (Fondazione Energie Comuni: about 40 MWp across Southern Italy), plus a crowded field of about 10+ CER-software vendors, including a free one (Hopee). Retention on incentives is typically a percentage; one published CER regulation sets 20%. Retentions by CER-ETS entities are tax-neutral (Agenzia Entrate, Feb 2026). I found no reliable per-provider CER counts for the utilities.

### Inferences
- **Buyer map:** the comune is often the formal promoter and member, but the operator is usually a third party: an ESCo, utility, installer, cooperative or foundation (79% involve one). The SaaS buyer is that operator, which runs N configurations, plus any installers bundling "CER management" with their PV sales.
- **Portfolio size (indicative, from sparse data):** ènostra has 30+ CERs; Fondazione Energie Comuni about 40 MWp (≈ 400+ configurations at the national average of 83 kW, or far fewer if its plants are larger). I found no utility counts.
- **Planning assumption (unsourced):** a mid-size operator runs **20–200 configurations**, and the 4,800 configurations are spread across perhaps **a few hundred operators**. This must be validated with primary interviews.
- **[Derived] Management "wallet":** retention of 10–20% on the national incentive flow gives:
  - About **€2.7–5.4m/yr** today (on €27m)
  - About **€10–20m/yr** after PNRR (on about €98m)
  - About **€28–56m/yr** at 5 GW (on about €281m)
  - This wallet has to pay for all administration (people, accounting, legal, metering, software), so software can take only a fraction of it.
- **Price pressure:** a free incumbent (Hopee) and about 10 or more paid vendors already exist. Differentiation has to come from workflow depth: GSE portal/SPC integration, PNRR reporting, member onboarding, allocation and payout, accounting/ETS compliance, and multi-configuration portfolio operations.

## Q6. How do comparable EU markets (France, Spain, Germany, Portugal) compare, as an expansion signal?

### Takeaway
**France** is the most comparable and largest adjacent market for the same back-office job: about 2,276 collective self-consumption (ACC) operations, more than 25,000 participants and more than 380 MW at mid-2026, with volumes more than doubling each year. **Spain** has 262 subsidised energy communities covering more than 111,000 participants. **Germany** opened §42c EnWG energy sharing on 1 Jun 2026, without any network-charge relief. I found no Portugal data.

### Cited Findings
- **France (ACC):**
  - End-Q1 2026: 1,945 operations in service, 325,924 kVA; +320 new operations in Q1 2026.
  - Operation count grew +129% in 2024 and +133% in 2025.
  - End-Jun 2026: **2,276 operations, more than 25,000 participants, more than 380 MW**, 98% PV. This figure comes from the search summary; the exact source page among those listed was not confirmed.

- **Spain:**
  - IDAE's CE Implementa programme closed after 6 calls: **262 subsidised energy communities**, **€108.4m** of recovery-plan (PRTR) funds, **more than 111,166 participants** (citizens, SMEs, local bodies). Catalonia 72, Castilla y León 40, Andalucía 34.

### Inferences
- **[Derived] France:** 380 MW / 2,276 = **about 167 kW per operation**; 25,000 / 2,276 = **about 11 participants per operation**. ## Q7. How do these data translate into TAM/SAM/SOM and a rough unit-economics model?

### Takeaway
Size the market in **POD/members under management** and **configurations**, not in MW. The installed base is about 4,800 configurations and about 51,000 POD (Aug 2026). It plausibly reaches about **11,000–14,500 configurations and 120,000–150,000 POD by end-2027**, with a theoretical ceiling of about **60,000 configurations and about 630,000 POD at the 5 GW cap**. Pure software revenue potential in Italy is therefore modest:
- **About €1–2.5m/yr today**
- **About €2–7m/yr by 2028**
- **About €8–30m/yr at 5 GW**

The addressable wallet grows if the product also captures part of the operator's 10–20% administration retention (a BPO/"managed referente" model).

### Cited Findings
- Base data (all cited above):
  - Configurations and POD: 4,800 configurations, about 400 MW, about 51,000 POD at 31 Aug 2026 [GSE-official] — [ANSA](https://www.ansa.it/ansa2030/notizie/energia_energie/2026/09/07/sul-sito-del-gse-la-vetrina-delle-comunita-energetiche-rinnovabili_1035171a-d4e6-4aa1-bf8a-e6a1aa73e586.html)
  - PNRR admissions: 30,299 applications, 1,763.6 MW, €779m to commission by 31 Dec 2027 — [Energia in Città](https://www.energiaincitta.it/comunita-energetiche-dal-gse-oltre-30mila-le-domande-ammesse/)
  - Tariff cap and deadline: 5 GW contingent, 31 Dec 2027 — [Rinnovabili.it](https://www.rinnovabili.it/energia/comunita-energetiche-rinnovabili/dm-modifica-cacer-testo/)
  - Retention example: 20% — [CER Italy](https://cer-italy.com/regolamento/)
  - Free competitor: [Hopee](https://www.hopee.it/)

### Inferences
**A. Volume scenarios [Derived]**
- **Today (Aug 2026):** 4,800 configurations; 51,000 POD; 400 MW.
- **End-2027, run-rate method:** 4,800 + (400 to 600 per month × 16 months, Sep 2026–Dec 2027) = **11,200–14,400 configurations**. At 10.5 POD per configuration that is **about 118,000–151,000 POD**. The PNRR commissioning wave supports the upper half of this range.
- **5 GW ceiling:** 5,000 MW / 83.3 kW = **about 60,000 configurations**; × 10.5 = **about 630,000 POD**. This is a theoretical maximum at today's average size; larger PNRR plants would reduce the configuration count.

**B. TAM / SAM / SOM framing (suggested, with assumptions)**
- **TAM (Italy, software + ops-services wallet):**
  - The administration retention on the national incentive flow is 10–20% × €27m (today) / €98m (post-PNRR) / €281m (5 GW) = **€2.7–5.4m / €10–20m / €28–56m per year**.
  - Cross-check using per-POD software pricing of €12–48/POD/yr (€1–4 per month; a researcher assumption, not sourced):
    - 51,000 POD → €0.6–2.4m
    - 150,000 POD → €1.8–7.2m
    - 630,000 POD → €7.6–30m
  - The two methods overlap, which supports the sizing.
- **SAM:** configurations run by professional third-party operators (ESCos, utilities, installers, cooperatives, foundations) rather than self-run by a comune or citizen group. E&S's 79% "external subject involved" is a proxy.
  - Exclude utility captives that build in-house (share unknown).
  - Down-weight ≤10 kW micro-configurations (about 46% of the count, but a small share of MW and incentive).
  - Suggested SAM: **about 50–60% of POD** → about 25,000–30,000 POD today and about 60,000–90,000 by end-2027.
- **SOM (3–5 years):** 5–15% of SAM POD → **about 3,000–13,500 POD**. At €24–36/POD/yr that is **about €0.1–0.5m ARR**. With ops-service upsell (capturing, say, 3–5 percentage points of the retention), perhaps 2–3 times that.

**C. Unit economics per customer (operator) [Derived, illustrative]**
- **Example operator:** 50 configurations × 10.5 POD = 525 POD, about 4.2 MW.
- **Its incentive flow:** 50 × €5.7k = **about €285k/yr** (base case).
- **Its retention at 15%:** **about €43k/yr**, which is the ceiling on what it can spend on all administration.
- **SaaS price at €30/POD/yr:** 525 × 30 = **€15.8k ARR**, about 35% of its retention wallet. That is plausible only if the product removes staff time.
- **Small-configuration economics:** a ≤10 kW configuration generates about €680/yr; 15% retention is about €100/yr. Any per-configuration fee above about €50–100/yr is uneconomic, so pricing should scale with kW or incentive: a **%-of-incentive (1–3%)** or tiered per-POD model.
- **PNRR-funded plants:** their tariff is halved, which shrinks the wallet per MWh by about 40–50% (for example from €121 to €71). That hits operators with PNRR-heavy portfolios in small comuni, except where exempt POD (comuni, religious bodies, third-sector entities) dominate.

**D. Timing and risk signals**
- 2026–27 is the demand peak: PNRR reporting and commissioning, a doubling pace of configurations, and GSE tooling (SPC portal, Vetrina) that lowers onboarding friction.
- The main structural risk is the **31 Dec 2027 tariff-access deadline and the 5 GW cap**. The installed base keeps paying for 20 years (recurring back-office need through about 2047), but the growth of new configurations after 2027 depends on a successor decree.

### Gaps
- No observed SaaS price points in Italy other than "free". The €12–48/POD/yr and 1–3%-of-incentive ranges are researcher assumptions, to be validated with vendor quotes or interviews.
- No data on the number of operators or their portfolio sizes. This is critical for customer-count SOM.
- Possible double counting between the 400 MW operational figure and the 1,763.6 MW PNRR pipeline.
- Share of ≤10 kW configurations that are AID or groups (low back-office need) vs CER (higher need): not available.

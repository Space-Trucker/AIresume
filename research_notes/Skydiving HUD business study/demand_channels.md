# Skydiving HUD: Customer Demand, Sentiment and Go-to-Market Channels

Method note (read first): 25 web searches run 2026-09-28. Almost every direct page fetch was refused by the egress proxy (chutingstar.com, skydiveshop.com, deepandsteep.io, fai.org, help.dekunu.tech, aon2.co.uk, jumpsaildive.com) or failed DNS (fishskydiveapparel.com). **Findings below therefore come from search-engine snippets and summaries**, not full-page reads. Reddit r/skydiving, Facebook groups and YouTube comment threads did not show up in search results at all, so the sentiment evidence is thinner than hoped. Treat quotes as paraphrased snippets.

## 1. What skydivers say about HUDs / smart goggles / helmet displays, and common objections

### Takeaway
A commercial skydiving HUD now exists: Deep and Steep Insight paired with ENGO 2 glasses, about $349 for the glasses on top of the Insight altimeter, sold through ChutingStar and SkydiveShop. So the category is real, but small, gated and positioned as an add-on. Several earlier attempts were distraction-sensitive and failed or stalled: Recon Flight HUD (616 units made; the line was killed when Intel shut Recon in 2017) and AON2 Project Obsidian (£899, promised to ship summer 2023, still showing as pre-order). The main objections I could document are distraction, a higher experience threshold (vendors require at least a B-license), reliability (units that never worked), sunlight readability, battery life and price. I could not directly verify the forum-level objections the brief lists (fogging, snag, "look at your altimeter", instructors' views) because the forums could not be reached.

### Cited Findings
**Current products and positioning**
- Deep and Steep: ENGO 2 glasses controlled by the Insight GPS altimeter show altitude, glide, distance to LZ and more. The data is projected "a few feet out in front of the user's right eye", described as "much faster and easier to view than turning to the side to look at a wrist mounted altimeter." — [ChutingStar ENGO 2 Insight page](https://www.chutingstar.com/engo-2-insight-heads-up-display-glasses)
- The glasses can show up to 3 data points: altitude AGL, ground speed, ground track, distance to LZ, vertical speed, current glide. — [Deep and Steep HUD Connect](https://deepandsteep.io/knowledge/heads-up-display-hud-connect/)
- Insight HUD Glasses (Photochromatic) cost $349.00. They only work with an Insight GPS altimeter. — [Deep and Steep shop](https://deepandsteep.io/shop/engo-2-photochromatic-glasses/); [Deep and Steep HUD category](https://deepandsteep.io/product-category/hud/)
- The glasses weigh about 36 g, use a MicroOLED display and have a photochromic lens. — [SkydiveShop ENGO 2 page](https://skydiveshop.com/products/engo-2-hud-glasses-deep-steep-insight)
- Vendor safety gate: anyone connecting HUD glasses to an Insight should hold at least a USPA B-license or equivalent. — [SkydiveShop ENGO 2 page](https://skydiveshop.com/products/engo-2-hud-glasses-deep-steep-insight); [Deep and Steep](https://deepandsteep.io/shop/engo-2-photochromatic-glasses/)
- SkydiveShop's 2026 altimeter roundup describes Insight as "HUD-glasses ready" but adds "not everyone needs that feature". HUD is treated as a niche add-on, not a main purchase driver. — [SkydiveShop: I Tested 12+ Skydiving Altimeters 2026](https://skydiveshop.com/pages/best-digital-skydiving-altimeters-2026)
- Wingstore.aero (wingsuit-focused EU retailer) also sells Engo 2 HUD glasses. — [wingstore.aero](https://wingstore.aero/en/product/engo-2-hud-glasses/)

**Earlier attempts**
- Recon Instruments Flight HUD (2012): a goggle-mounted display of speed, glide ratio and altitude for skydivers, wingsuit pilots and BASE jumpers. Pre-orders were $299 including goggles. — [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/recon-instruments-gps-goggles-skydiving/24669/); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2012-10-18-recon-instruments-offering-flight-hud-goggles.html); [Dropzone.com news](https://www.dropzone.com/articles/news/in-flight-hud-to-assist-wingsuit-pilots-r769/)
- Recon said it would only manufacture if 250 people pre-ordered (a demand-validation threshold). — [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2012/10/19/84972/a-heads-up-display-for-skydivers/)
- Only 616 Flight HUD units were made. Intel shut the Recon division in 2017, which ended support. — [Smart Clothing Lab / Wikipedia via search summary](https://smartclothinglab.com/brands/what-happened-to-recon-instruments/); [Recon Instruments – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recon_Instruments). The 616-unit figure came from the search summary and I could not confirm it on the primary page.
- A buyer's "two years later" review: the 2013 Flight HUD "never worked". A replacement came, but the buyer never used it for skydiving. This is a reliability and support-risk anecdote. — [Jump-Sail-Dive](https://jumpsaildive.com/2014/12/19/recon-flight-hud-two-years-later/)
- Press coverage at launch flagged distraction as the main concern: "Some might see this as a distraction, particularly in a sport like wingsuiting, where you're flying just a few feet over certain death." — [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/recon-instruments-gps-goggles-skydiving/24669/) (via search snippet)
- AON2 Project Obsidian (UK) calls a HUD "the holy grail in skydiving altimeters". It projects altitude into the visor or goggles "no matter where your arms are", with Bluetooth to a wrist flight computer. — [AON2 Project Obsidian](https://www.aon2.co.uk/project-obsidian/)
- Obsidian price: £899, with £100 off for pre-orders. Shipping was "starting summer 2023". The product was still listed under pre-orders when searched. A Facebook update says "tooling fully funded". — [AON2 product page](https://www.aon2.co.uk/products/project-obsidian/); [AON2 pre-orders](https://www.aon2.co.uk/product-category/pre-orders/); [AON2 Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/aonsquared/photos/-project-obsidian-update-heres-whats-newtooling-fully-fundedoriginally-we-aimed-/1135696691453460/). A retailer (Skydive Korea) also lists it. — [Skydive Korea shop](https://www.skydivekoreaeng.com/shop_view/?idx=69)
- Obsidian development history: a 2017 prototype at 3 mm thickness was "too dim to see in bright sunlight". A breakthrough came in 2020. The optics partner is tooz (per YouTube title "AO(N²) x tooz: Smart Glasses for Skydivers"). — [AON2](https://www.aon2.co.uk/project-obsidian/); [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KEykkAnVG70)
- Helmet vs glasses: AON2 chose glasses so they would fit "most full-face skydiving helmets, including the new impact-rated models". They are "not in the business of making skydiving helmets". — [AON2 helmet or glasses](https://www.aon2.co.uk/project-obsidian-helmet-or-glasses/)
- Smart-helmet adjacent: Bonehead CS1 is a full-face comms helmet with Bluetooth, radio options and audible-altimeter connectivity. It has no visual HUD. — [Bonehead Composites CS1](https://boneheadcomposites.com/products/cs1-communications-system-full-face-helmet)
- Military precedent: the Marine Corps MFF navigation system puts GPS on the helmet with a goggle-mounted display giving steering cues under canopy. Patents place the display in peripheral vision so it does not block views of the canopy, lines, handles or ground. — [GlobalSecurity MFF NavAid](https://globalsecurity.org/military/systems/aircraft/systems/mff-navaid.htm); [Nanohmics patent](https://www.freepatentsonline.com/y2010/0204910.html); [US 6934633 helmet-mounted nav](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6934633)
- A 1995 patent (US5423215) already describes a heads-up altimeter on eyewear, so the concept is decades old. — [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5423215A/en)
- Adjacent eyewear sentiment: instructors generally prefer contacts to glasses under goggles, because frames "can shift or fog up". — [Skydive Orange](https://www.skydiveorange.com/2024/11/12/can-you-skydive-with-glasses-or-contacts/)

### Inferences
- Objections that show up in the evidence: (1) distraction / information overload (Recon press, Dekunu "potentially distracting information"); (2) experience gating (B-license minimum), which shrinks the addressable market to non-students; (3) reliability and vendor survival (Recon units that never worked, Recon shut down, Dekunu on hold, Obsidian late); (4) sunlight readability; (5) price (£899 Obsidian vs $349 ENGO add-on vs $299 Recon).
- Glasses that fit inside existing full-face helmets are how today's players deal with helmet fit and certification risk. A dedicated "smart helmet" would compete with established helmet brands (Cookie, Bonehead) and with impact-rating requirements.
- The only HUD shipping at scale in 2026 is an add-on to an altimeter ecosystem sold through existing dealers. A new entrant likely needs either a display-agnostic flight computer or a partnership with an altimeter brand.

### Gaps
- No direct Reddit r/skydiving, dropzone.com thread text, Facebook group or YouTube comment sentiment could be retrieved. Specific objections like fogging, snag, battery in cold, "look at your altimeter" and instructor/S&TA views on HUDs are unverified.
- No ENGO/Insight sales volumes or customer review counts.
- Obsidian's current (2026) shipping status is unconfirmed.

## 2. Reception of Dekunu One, FlySight 2, L&B/Alti-2 apps, Paralog; willingness to pay for subscriptions

### Takeaway
There is willingness to pay about $350–400 for data-rich hardware: FlySight 2 at $384 was frequently sold out, and Dekunu One was about $390. Recurring software payment has only thin evidence: Paralog uses an annual license and Jumplog has tiered accounts, while Skyderby's scoreboards are community-run and appear free. Dekunu, the closest analog (smart altimeter plus cloud with 3D visualization), was loved for its cloud and 3D replay. Users complained about battery and sync, and the company went "on hold" in 2022, which is a cautionary tale for this product concept.

### Cited Findings
**Dekunu One**
- Reviewers called it "an awesome piece of technology that is going to take the sport to a whole new level". It covers flat, freefly, wingsuit and canopy disciplines. — [Skydivemag Dekunu One Review (2018)](https://www.skydivemag.com/new/2018-12-12-dekunu-one-review/)
- The standout feature was the Dekunu Cloud: easy access to jump stats and a "3D viz option of your jump showing all the speeds and movement". Price was about $390 US for the Dekunu One Pack. — [Skydivemag](https://www.skydivemag.com/new/2018-12-12-dekunu-one-review/); [Fish Skydive Apparel 3-year review](https://www.fishskydiveapparel.com/blogs/fish-blog/dekunu-one-review-after-3-years)
- Battery was called "meh": it lasts a full jump day but needs an overnight charge on weekends. One user had it almost flat after 5 jumps. — [Fish Skydive 3-year review](https://www.fishskydiveapparel.com/blogs/fish-blog/dekunu-one-review-after-3-years); [Dekunu Community battery thread](https://community.dekunu.tech/t/all-battery-related-issues/119?page=2)
- Each log can take up to 60 s to sync over WiFi. Troubleshooting docs cover sync failures and re-login. — [Dekunu Post-Jump Troubleshooting](https://help.dekunu.tech/article/280-post-jump-troubleshooting)
- "Many people had questions about how necessary it was to include more potentially distracting information." — [Fish Skydive 3-year review](https://www.fishskydiveapparel.com/blogs/fish-blog/dekunu-one-review-after-3-years) (via snippet)
- "Dekunu On Hold": online stores closed as a cost-cutting measure and support reduced, while the cloud and device API "remain fully functional". The most recent dated info is May 2022. — [Dekunu KB: Dekunu On Hold](https://help.dekunu.tech/article/301-dekunu-on-hold)
- An "Explorer Edition" (early-adopter program) had separate purchase terms. — [Dekunu Explorer Edition T&Cs](https://s3.amazonaws.com/helpscout.net/docs/assets/5a4ec11d0428631938009d9b/attachments/5ac5c39e0428630750921cb6/Terms-&-Conditions-of-Purchase---One-Explorer-Edition.pdf)

**FlySight 2**
- Audible GPS feedback (glide ratio, horizontal or vertical speed) for wingsuit pilots, canopy pilots and swoopers. Logging: GNSS up to 25 Hz, pressure up to 200 Hz, IMU up to 6 kHz. — [SkydiveShop FlySight 2](https://skydiveshop.com/products/flysight-2-audible-gps)
- Selling point: "changes in body position can be heard immediately, instead of waiting until after the jump". — [SkydiveShop](https://skydiveshop.com/products/flysight-2-audible-gps)
- It is a redesign with BLE, better audio, extra sensors and an IP67 case. — [Basik Air Concept](https://basik.fr/en/products/flysight-2-gps); [Skydiving Market](https://skydivingmarket.com/products/flysight-2)
- Price is $384 USD (ChutingStar/others) or €280 in France. It showed as sold out at several retailers. — [Skydiving Market](https://skydivingmarket.com/products/flysight-2); [Basik](https://basik.fr/en/products/flysight-2-gps); [ChutingStar](https://www.chutingstar.com/flysight-2-audible-gps)
- It is stocked widely: ChutingStar, SkydiveShop, Sky-Shop EU, DZ Sports UK, ParaFunalia (CA), Wingstore, Ram Air, CSC Pro Shop (a DZ shop). — [Sky-Shop](https://sky-shop.eu/flysight-audible-gps); [DZ Sports](https://www.dzsports.co.uk/Flysight-gps); [ParaFunalia](https://parafunalia.ca/product/flysight-2-gps/); [CSC Pro Shop](https://store.skydivecsc.com/products/flysight-2)
- Pro endorsements are used in marketing. Jeff Nebelkopf (Chief Wingsuit Test Pilot, Tony Suits): "I highly recommend the FlySight". Canopy competitor Dan Raymond says the 5 Hz rate, accuracy and small size made it "an effective tool for swoop training." — [SkydiveShop](https://skydiveshop.com/products/flysight-2-audible-gps)

**L&B / Alti-2 apps**
- The L&B Viso II+ is described as the "goto" altimeter worldwide, with an electronic logbook. — [L&B](https://www.lbaltimeters.com/consumer/visual-altimeters/viso-ii.html)
- Alti-2 N3, Atlas and Atlas II are the same altimeter with different cases and charging ports. — [SkydiveShop guide](https://skydiveshop.com/blogs/articles/buying-skydiving-altimeter-ultimate-guide)
- An app-store review (Mar 2026) says the Atlas app was "too much and messy" and the reviewer switched to a simpler third-party app. This suggests OEM app UX is a pain point. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/cn/app/id1511161657) (single review, low weight)
- ChutingStar keeps a digital-altimeter feature comparison blog. — [ChutingStar Rant & Rave](https://www.chutingstar.com/blog/stacking-up-the-latest-digital-altimeters/)

**Paralog / Jumplog / Skyderby (software monetization)**
- Paralog Desktop license: "one year usage … for one user on multiple computers with one logger", plus one month of free premium jumplog.net and 30 days of email support. This is effectively an annual license. — [Paralog purchase](https://www.paralog.net/purchase.php)
- Jumplog.net tiers: Basic (new jumpers, public log), Premium (more jumps, Paralog sync), Professional (unlimited jumps, automatic jump creation from FlySight, GPX, ProTrack II tracks). — [Jumplog](https://www.jumplog.net/)
- Skyderby lets users upload FlySight tracks, compare with others and run virtual competitions. — [Skyderby](https://skyderby.io/); [Skyderby virtual competition](https://www.skyderby.io/virtual_competitions/9/overall?page=6)

### Inferences
- Hardware willingness to pay clusters around $300–400 (FlySight 2, Dekunu One, ENGO add-on, Recon). £899 (Obsidian) is well above the proven range and has not been proven.
- Dekunu shows that users value cloud 3D replay, but hardware economics, battery and sync reliability can sink the company. A pro membership should be additive and must not be required for core safety functions.
- The strongest existing paid-software precedent is Paralog/Jumplog's tiered model, where auto-importing FlySight/ProTrack tracks sits in the top tier. That supports pricing "auto-log + analysis" as premium features.

### Gaps
- Exact Paralog and Jumplog tier prices were not visible in snippets.
- No data on Dekunu subscription or paid-tier uptake. I could not confirm whether Dekunu Cloud ever charged.
- Skyderby pricing/donation model is unconfirmed.
- No FlySight unit sales figures.

## 3. Early-adopter segments, athletes and competitions

### Takeaway
Wingsuit pilots and canopy pilots/swoopers are the clearest early adopters. They already buy FlySight for real-time feedback, and the wingsuit performance competition ecosystem runs on GPS logging devices (PLDs), with FlySight plus Skyderby used for scoring. Military freefall has a documented, long-standing need for goggle-mounted navigation displays. FAI competitions hand out judge-controlled PLDs, so a consumer HUD would be a training tool, not a competition-mandated device.

### Cited Findings
- FAI recognizes wingsuit performance flying (time, horizontal distance, horizontal speed). A PLD records the real-time 3D position. At FAI events the judges supply the PLD and competitors "are not allowed to interfere with it". — [FAI 2025 Wingsuit Competition Rules](https://www.fai.org/sites/default/files/isc/documents/2025/2025_isc_cr_wingsuit_flying_v2.pdf); [UK Wingsuiting competitions](https://wingsuiting.uk/competitions-wingsuit-performance/)
- "Most competitors use FlySight as a PLD". Both InTime and FlySight/Skyderby scoring are used. — [UK Wingsuiting](https://wingsuiting.uk/competitions-wingsuit-performance/); [Wingsuit World rules](https://wingsuit.world/rules/)
- Canopy piloting courses use gates only about 30 ft apart over a swoop pond. Leagues have classes such as intermediate (≤270° turn, wing loading <2.50). — [Wikipedia: Canopy piloting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canopy_piloting); [FLCPA Scoring & Classes](https://swoopleague.com/scoring-classes/)
- Canopy coaching curricula exist (CSPA Coach 3 Canopy Piloting manual). — [CSPA](https://www.cspa.ca/sites/default/files/Coach%203%20-%20Canopy%20Piloting%202022.pdf)
- Taya Weiss (wingsuit coach) publishes FlySight intro resources, so coach-led adoption is already happening. — [Taya Weiss](https://tayaweiss.com/wingsuit-resources/2017/11/introduction-to-flysight)
- Military: the MFF NavAid provides in-flight navigation integrated with the JPADS Mission Planner, for landing precisely in cloud and poor weather. There is a US Army solicitation for an "MFF Parachutist Handheld Navigation Device". — [GlobalSecurity](https://globalsecurity.org/military/systems/aircraft/systems/mff-navaid.htm); [GovTribe](https://govtribe.com/opportunity/federal-contract-opportunity/military-freefall-mff-parachutist-handheld-navigation-device-w911qyfy13a01); [HAHO-PNS](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/systems/aircraft/systems/haho-pns.htm)
- Recon targeted skydivers, wingsuit pilots and BASE jumpers from the start. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2012-10-18-recon-instruments-offering-flight-hud-goggles.html)

**Market size context (aggregator, verify before use)**
- About 3.47–3.65 million US jumps in 2025. About 40,000–50,000 licensed skydivers jump regularly, against about 300,000 first-time tandem students a year. — [SkydiveGuides 2026 stats](https://www.skydiveguides.com/how-many-people-skydive-a-year-statistics-2026/). These are secondary and inconsistent figures. The primary source is the [USPA 2024 Annual Survey](https://www.uspa.org/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=4DJYjAyLi3Y%3D&portalid=0), which I could not read.

### Inferences
- The beachhead is wingsuit plus canopy-piloting training: glide/speed/trajectory guidance and distance-to-LZ. These users already pay about $384 for FlySight and value real-time feedback. Freefly and belly (RW) users care more about altitude awareness, which a $50–400 wrist or audible altimeter already covers.
- Competition use: under FAI, organizers supply the PLD, so an "official device" play means getting accepted as a PLD or scoring-compatible logger (FlySight-format export for Skyderby), not being mandatory.
- Military/government is a separate B2B channel with procurement cycles and very different requirements (NVG compatibility, JPADS integration).

### Gaps
- I could not read FAI or USPA canopy piloting rules to check whether electronic displays or audible devices are allowed during competition rounds.
- No named sponsored-athlete deals for HUD or altimeter brands beyond the FlySight endorsements.
- The size of the wingsuit and swoop population is unknown.

## 4. Distribution: how gear sells (dealers, DZ shops, events, PIA, sponsorship, margins, seasonality)

### Takeaway
Electronics reach skydivers through a small set of specialist online/loft retailers: ChutingStar, SkydiveShop, Sky-Shop EU, DZ Sports UK, ParaFunalia, Wingstore, Basik. Dropzone pro shops attached to rigging lofts also carry them (CSC Pro Shop, The Ranch PROshop, Skydive New England). The key industry trade show is the biennial PIA Symposium; the next one is 8–11 Feb 2027 in Cincinnati, with 80+ exhibiting manufacturers. Retailers also produce influential comparison content ("I tested 12+ altimeters 2026", ChutingStar's comparison blog). I found no public data on dealer margins or seasonality.

### Cited Findings
- DZ gear stores act as "proud dealer[s] for all of the major skydiving manufacturers". — [Skydive New England gear store](https://www.skydivenewengland.com/experienced/gear-store/)
- Dealers hold dealerships with Sun Path, Aerodyne, UPT, Parachute Systems, PD and CYPRES. — search summary of dealer pages, e.g. [Skydive Carolina gear sales](https://www.skydivecarolina.com/blog/patrick-mercier-to-oversee-gear-sales-at-skydive-carolina/)

- ChutingStar started in 1999 as one rigger. It now runs a full loft with a DPRE, combining retail with rigging services. — [ChutingStar Our Story](https://www.chutingstar.com/chs-our-story)
- Gear stores often sit on DZs: The Ranch PROshop is open every day next to Skydive The Ranch, and Expanding Horizons is at Skydive Perris. — [The Ranch PROshop](https://www.theranchproshop.com/); [Skydive Perris rigging](https://skydiveperris.com/facilities/gear-rigging/rigging-services/)
- The PIA Symposium is biennial and "primarily an exhibition and trade show, with seminars". The 2027 edition is 8–11 Feb 2027 at First Financial Center, Cincinnati, with 120+ seminars, 100+ speakers and 80+ exhibiting manufacturers, and draws manufacturers, riggers, DZOs, athletes and fun jumpers. — [Skydivemag PIA 2027](https://www.skydivemag.com/new/pia-symposium-returns-to-cincinnati-in-2027/); [PIA events](https://www.pia.com/events/); [Skydive-safety PIA](https://www.skydive-safety.com/Parachute-Industry-Association-Symposium.htm)

- Skydiving media such as Skydivemag run product reviews (Dekunu One review), and dropzone.com carried Recon HUD news. ### Inferences
- A go-to-market plan would have four parts: (1) list with the top 5–8 specialist e-tailers in the US and EU; (2) seed DZ pro shops at the big wingsuit/swoop hubs (Perris, Eloy, DeLand, CSC); (3) get into retailer comparison articles; (4) launch or demo at PIA Feb 2027. Coach and athlete endorsements from wingsuit and canopy pilots follow the FlySight playbook.
- Because PIA is biennial (next Feb 2027), launch timing matters. Northern-hemisphere buying likely peaks in spring, before the season, but this is unverified.

### Gaps

- Seasonality of gear sales: no data.
- Boogie/event sales volume: no data.

## 5. Marketing benchmarks: CAC, crowdfunding

### Takeaway
I found no public CAC benchmarks for niche sports hardware, and no successful Kickstarter or Indiegogo campaign for skydiving or wingsuit instruments. The pre-order pattern is the relevant precedent: Recon required 250 pre-orders, AON2 took discounted pre-orders, and Dekunu ran an "Explorer Edition". These pre-orders have a record of long delays.

### Cited Findings
- Recon Flight HUD was built only if 250 pre-orders came in, at $299. — [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2012/10/19/84972/a-heads-up-display-for-skydivers/); [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/recon-instruments-gps-goggles-skydiving/24669/)
- AON2 Obsidian took pre-orders at £100 off £899. It said tooling was "fully funded" but had not shipped by the promised summer 2023. — [AON2](https://www.aon2.co.uk/products/project-obsidian/); [AON2 Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/aonsquared/photos/-project-obsidian-update-heres-whats-newtooling-fully-fundedoriginally-we-aimed-/1135696691453460/)
- Dekunu ran an "Explorer Edition" early-adopter sale with its own T&Cs. — [Dekunu Explorer T&Cs](https://s3.amazonaws.com/helpscout.net/docs/assets/5a4ec11d0428631938009d9b/attachments/5ac5c39e0428630750921cb6/Terms-&-Conditions-of-Purchase---One-Explorer-Edition.pdf)
- The only skydive-adjacent crowdfunding hit found was the Final Frontier Design / Sol-X space-diving suit Kickstarter, which is not a comparable consumer instrument. — [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/space-diving-iron-man-suit/27659/)

### Inferences
- Vendor-run pre-orders with DZ/coach validation seem more common in this niche than public crowdfunding. The prior failures (Recon quality, Obsidian delay, Dekunu hiatus) probably make buyers skeptical of pre-orders. A working demo at events is likely needed to build trust.

### Gaps
- CAC figures for niche sports hardware: none found.
- Kickstarter/Indiegogo skydiving instrument campaigns and results: none found.
- Paid-social performance in the skydiving vertical: no data.

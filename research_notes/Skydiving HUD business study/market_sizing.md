# Skydiving HUD Product Line: Addressable Market Sizing (Worldwide), notes as of Sept 2026

Method note: 25 web searches. Every page fetch failed because the egress proxy blocked it (uspa.org, wikipedia, paramag.fr, apf.com.au, chutingstar.com, sky-shop.eu), so most figures below come from search-engine snippets of the named pages. I could not read the primary documents in full, so treat snippet-derived numbers as "reported by" and not verified. Low-quality aggregators (zipdo, gitnux, skydiveguides.com, and "market research" press releases such as openPR, VerifiedMarketReports and WiseGuyReports) are flagged wherever they appear.

## 1. Active licensed skydivers and jump volumes (worldwide and by country)

### Takeaway
The US is the anchor market: USPA has about 42,400 members and 220+ dropzones, and US jump volume was 3.88M in 2024 but fell to about 3.47M in 2025. Outside the US, the published federation counts are small, about 6,000–6,500 each for the UK and Germany. France's jump volume is shrinking (506k jumps in 2024, down from 547k in 2023). A defensible worldwide pool of active licensed sport skydivers is roughly 90k–130k (inference). The widely repeated "70,000 skydivers worldwide" figure is stale and understates the total.

### Cited Findings
- **USA, members:** USPA has "more than 42,400 members and more than 220 affiliated dropzones" in the US (this boilerplate appears in both the 2024 and 2025 fatality-summary snippets) — [USPA, A Milestone in Safety: 2024 Fatality Summary](https://www.uspa.org/a-milestone-in-safetythe-2024-fatality-summary); [USPA, A Need for Vigilance: 2025 Fatality Summary](https://www.uspa.org/uspa-news/a-need-for-vigilance-the-2025-fatality-summary)
- **USA, jumps in 2024:** 3.88 million skydives at USPA dropzones, 9 fatalities (0.23 per 100k), the lowest on record — [AVweb, Jan 2025](https://avweb.com/features/u-s-skydiving-fatalities-reach-historic-low-in-2024/); [Skydive Arizona](https://www.skydiveaz.com/2024-skydivings-lowest-fatality-rate-ever/). This matches the brief's "~3.9M US jumps in 2024". I found no source for the brief's "~1.3M abroad" figure, and it remains UNVERIFIED (see Gaps).
- **USA, jumps in 2025:** about 3.47 million skydives and 16 fatalities (0.46 per 100k), with zero student fatalities — [Skydive Fundamentals summary of the USPA 2025 report](https://www.skydivefundamentals.com/blog/skydiving-fatalities-2025); [USPA 2025 Fatality Summary](https://www.uspa.org/uspa-news/a-need-for-vigilance-the-2025-fatality-summary). This is a secondary summary; the drop of about 10.6% year on year should be confirmed against the primary USPA text.
- **USA, member profile (2024 USPA survey):** average member experience about 750 jumps; 82% male and 18% female, with women "increasing at 2% annually"; discipline participation: formation skydiving 48%, freeflying 40%, tracking 31%, working jumps 27%, angle flying 23% — [Skydive Fundamentals, citing the USPA 2024 survey](https://www.skydivefundamentals.com/blog/skydiving-fatalities-2025); primary: [USPA 2024 Annual Survey Results PDF](https://www.uspa.org/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=4DJYjAyLi3Y%3D&portalid=0) (blocked)
- **USA, purchase behaviour:** 32% of USPA members bought new gear in 2024, up from 29% in 2023. 40% attended a boogie and 23% earned a new license in 2024. More than 5,000 members responded to the survey — [USPA 2024 Annual Survey Results](https://www.uspa.org/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=4DJYjAyLi3Y%3D&portalid=0) (snippet)
- **UK:** British Skydiving (BPA) has about 6,400 full members, about 60,000 students a year, and 28 affiliated Parachute Training Organisations (PTOs, i.e. dropzones) — [British Skydiving, Overview](https://britishskydiving.org/overview/). The page is undated and was likely current in 2024–25.
- **Germany:** DFV has "over 6,000 members" — [dfv.aero](https://www.dfv.aero/DFV) (snippet, undated)
- **France, jumps:** FFP recorded 505,576 jumps in 2024, down from 546,544 in 2023 (−40,968). This was the second consecutive annual fall of more than 40k, and the total is "approaching 500,000, a level last seen nearly 30 years ago" — [Paramag, Activité FFP 2024](https://paramag.fr/activite-ffp-2024/) (snippet)
- **France, licences:** 65,591 licence holders in 2019 — [Wikipedia, FFP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9d%C3%A9ration_fran%C3%A7aise_de_parachutisme) (snippet). The figure almost certainly includes temporary and tandem/discovery licences, so it is NOT a count of licensed sport jumpers.
- **Australia:** the APF publishes a 2024 Yearbook and audited 2024 financial statements (surplus of A$202,996 for FY2024), but I could not read the member and jump counts — [APF Publications](https://www.apf.com.au/members/members/publications/publications); [APF FY2024 financials](https://www.apf.com.au/ArticleDocuments/1260/Audited%20Financial%20Statements%20-%20Australian%20Parachute%20Federation%20Limited%20YE%2031%20December%202024.pdf.aspx). The only descent figure found is old: "over 300,000 parachute descents annually" — [APF submission, Jan 2014](https://www.infrastructure.gov.au/sites/default/files/migrated/aviation/asrr/submissions/files/187_australian_parachuting_federation_31_jan_2014_redacted.pdf)
- **Worldwide:** "more than 70,000 skydivers worldwide, 41,000 USPA members" (undated and likely more than 5 years old; snippet from search aggregation) — [Wikipedia, Formation skydiving / FFP pages via search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formation_skydiving). FAI has had worldwide Certificate of Proficiency standards since 2001, but it publishes no worldwide count of licence holders — [FAI Certificates of Proficiency](https://www.fai.org/page/certificates-proficiency)
- **Experience distribution:** "D-license holders are 10% of USPA members" and "450,000 tandem jumps and 3.2M sport jumps in the US in 2023" appear only via a snippet from [zipdo.co](https://zipdo.co/skydiving-statistics/). **LOW QUALITY: do not use without verification.** The USPA survey PDFs ([2023](https://www.uspa.org/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=NfjLUI_ciRk%3D&portalid=0), [2024](https://www.uspa.org/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=4DJYjAyLi3Y%3D&portalid=0)) hold the real distribution but were blocked.

### Inferences
- **US active base:** 42.4k members includes students and inactive members. Licensed members who jump in a given year are plausibly about 30–35k. A mean of about 750 career jumps implies a right-skewed distribution, and a large minority (plausibly 30–45%) likely has 200+ jumps. This is an inference that needs the USPA PDF to confirm.
- **Worldwide licensed active sport skydivers, rough build:** US about 42k; France about 10–15k licensed sport jumpers (inferred from 506k jumps); UK about 6.4k; Germany about 6k+; Australia about 3–5k; Russia, Brazil, Italy, Spain, the Netherlands, Switzerland, Scandinavia, the UAE, NZ, South Africa, Canada and others about 20–40k combined. **Total about 90k–130k.** Use about 100k as the base case for TAM head-count, flagged as an estimate.
- **Worldwide jumps:** US 3.5–3.9M plus France 0.5M plus others. A plausible total is about 6–8M jumps a year worldwide (inference, not sourced).
- **Growth trend:** mixed to negative. US jumps fell about 10% from 2024 to 2025 and French jumps fell about 7.5% from 2023 to 2024. Women's share is rising. Plan for a flat to slightly declining head-count and model unit growth on attach rate, not on population growth.

### Gaps
- USPA license-level distribution (A/B/C/D), the share with 200+ or 1000+ jumps, and the tandem/AFF split: the primary PDF was blocked.
- USPA "jumps abroad by members" (the brief's ~1.3M) was not found in any snippet.
- No 2024–26 member counts found for Italy (FIPS/ENAC), Spain (RFAE), Brazil (CBPq), Russia, UAE (Skydive Dubai operates as a commercial centre), or current Australian counts.
- No IPC/FAI worldwide census exists.

## 2. Sub-segments most likely to buy (wingsuit, canopy, camera, instructors, competition, BASE, military, paragliding)

### Takeaway
Core early adopters are the GPS-feedback-using disciplines: wingsuit, swooping/canopy piloting, tracking/angle, and navigation-heavy military HAHO. The whole skydiving HUD niche is small; an incumbent ENGO 2 HUD plus Insight altimeter bundle already exists. The military has a 20-year history of wanting helmet HUD navigation for HAHO jumps (a 2,000-unit target in the HAHO-PNS program). Paragliding is a larger adjacent market, with DHV alone at about 39–40k members.

### Cited Findings
- **Tracking/angle:** in the 2024 USPA survey, 31% of members do tracking and 23% do angle flying. These are proxies for demand for glide and trajectory feedback — [Skydive Fundamentals citing the USPA 2024 survey](https://www.skydivefundamentals.com/blog/skydiving-fatalities-2025)
- **Wingsuit "market size":** "$2.89B in 2026, reaching $5.38B by 2034, 7.14% CAGR"; key players named are Squirrel, Phoenix-Fly, TonySuits, Intrudair, FlySight and GoPro — [VerifiedMarketReports](https://www.verifiedmarketreports.com/product/wingsuit-flying-market/); [openPR](https://www.openpr.com/news/4545287/wingsuit-flying-market-size-accelerated-by-9-4-cagr-by-key). **LOW QUALITY and implausible.** With perhaps a few thousand active wingsuit pilots worldwide buying suits at about $1–2k, the real suit market is plausibly in the low tens of $M. Do not use these figures.
- **Existing skydiving HUD competitors:**
  - Deep & Steep Insight (GPS altimeter and logger) plus ENGO 2 HUD glasses showing altitude, glide and distance to landing zone, sold via ChutingStar and SkydiveShop — [Deep and Steep](https://deepandsteep.io/); [ChutingStar ENGO 2 Insight listing](https://www.chutingstar.com/engo-2-insight-heads-up-display-glasses)
  - AO(N²) "Project Obsidian", a visor/goggle altitude HUD in development — [AON2](https://www.aon2.co.uk/project-obsidian/)
  - Recon Instruments' skydiving goggle HUD, pre-ordered at US$349 with speed, glide and altitude display. Adoption was low and it "soon fell out of use"; Recon was later acquired and shut down by Intel — [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/recon-instruments-gps-goggles-skydiving/24669/)
  - An earlier academic prototype — [MIT Technology Review, 2012](https://www.technologyreview.com/s/429707/a-heads-up-display-for-skydivers/)
- **FlySight 2:** this is the de facto audible GPS for wingsuit and canopy pilots. It gives real-time tone and speech feedback on glide ratio and horizontal/vertical speed, and adds Bluetooth Low Energy and an inertial measurement unit. It is sold by essentially all major retailers — [SkydiveShop](https://skydiveshop.com/products/flysight-2-audible-gps); [ChutingStar](https://www.chutingstar.com/flysight-2-audible-gps)
- **Military HAHO-PNS:** Naval Surface Warfare Center Panama City developed a helmet-mounted navigation pod with a goggle microdisplay (Navy patent 6,934,633, 2005). It sought a manufacturer to produce **2,000 systems** — [GlobalSecurity, HAHO-PNS](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/systems/aircraft/systems/haho-pns.htm). The SOCOM/Natick navigation-aid program (Gentex HAHO helmet with HUD, processor and GPS) was started in 2003 with fielding planned for 4Q FY07 — [GlobalSecurity MFF NavAid](https://globalsecurity.org/military/systems/aircraft/systems/mff-navaid.htm); [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/go/4024/)
- **Current military incumbents:** Airborne Systems sells the jTrax Navaid, a single-device navigation aid for long-offset jumps. The US Army MFF ARAPS NAVAID integrates with the Joint Precision Airdrop System Mission Planner (MP JPADS) — [Airborne Systems](https://airborne-sys.com/personnel-parachute-systems/); [PM SSV MFF](https://cpeground.army.mil/Equipment/Equipment-Portfolio/PM-OCIE-PPE-Portfolio/Military-Free-Fall-Parachute-System/). Unit prices were NOT found.
- **Paragliding, Germany:** DHV has about 39,000–40,000 members and 280–320 clubs, calls itself the world's largest hang-gliding and paragliding association, and more than 90% of German pilots are members — [de.wikipedia DHV](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutscher_H%C3%A4ngegleiterverband); [Natursport-BW](https://natursport.kultus-bw.de/,Lde/Startseite/Natursportarten/Deutscher+Haengegleiterverband+e_V_+DHV)
- **Paragliding, France:** more than 25,000 active pilots in 2011 (old) — [Wikipedia, Paragliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paragliding)
- **Paragliding, worldwide:** no reliable global count. Licensing varies by country; for example, Bulgaria has no requirement outside FAI competitions — [Wikipedia, Paragliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paragliding)

### Inferences
- **Beachhead segment:** wingsuit, canopy/swoop and tracking pilots who already own a FlySight or Insight (plausibly 10–20% of active licensed jumpers, i.e. about 10–20k worldwide), plus instructors and camera flyers (a helmet-integrated HUD suits camera flyers who already wear a helmet with camera mounts).
- **Military:** a niche with high average selling price. Program-scale quantities are in the low thousands of units, per the HAHO-PNS 2,000-unit target. Military procurement cycles are long and specs are demanding (NVG compatibility, secure GPS, oxygen masks). Treat it as a separate SAM rather than part of consumer SOM.
- **Paragliding and speedflying** are an adjacent market plausibly 2–4× the size of skydiving by head-count (Germany alone about 40k versus about 6k skydivers). However, they already have mature varios and flight computers (XCTrack, Flymaster, Skytraxx), and existing HUD glasses (ENGO) are sold to endurance sports.
- **Lessons from Recon and ENGO:** HUD adoption has historically been low. Friction points were the field of view in freefall, goggle and helmet fit, and trust relative to proven altimeters.

### Gaps
- No count of wingsuit pilots, swoopers or BASE jumpers worldwide (BASE numbering systems such as the BASE# registry were not searched).
- No military unit prices or current contract values for jTrax or ARAPS NAVAID.
- No count of military free-fall-qualified personnel worldwide.
- No worldwide paragliding or speedflying pilot count.

## 3. Annual gear spend per jumper and price points

### Takeaway
A full new rig costs $8k–14k and a helmet $320–$690. Instruments sit at $259–$439 for L&B/Alti-2 audibles and visuals and about $275–$299 for the FlySight 2. This establishes a $300–$700 price anchor for a HUD accessory and $700–$1,200+ for a smart helmet (inference). About a third of USPA members buy new gear in a given year.

### Cited Findings
- **Full rig:** new setup (container, main, reserve, automatic activation device, accessories) $8,000–$14,000; container $3,000–$8,000; main about $2,500; reserve $1,500–$2,000 — [Skydive Danielson, Skydiving Equipment Cost](https://www.skydivedanielson.com/blog/skydiving-equipment-cost-a-breakdown/); see also [ChutingStar price comparison blog](https://www.chutingstar.com/blog/skydiving-gear-price-comparison-info/)

- **FlySight 2:** "typically about $275" per a search summary of retailer listings — [SkydiveShop](https://skydiveshop.com/products/flysight-2-audible-gps); [CSC Pro Shop](https://store.skydivecsc.com/products/flysight-2). The brief's ~$299 could not be confirmed; the price varies by retailer.

  - L&B Optima II audible: from $259 — [SkydiveShop](https://skydiveshop.com/products/l-b-optima-ii)
  - L&B Viso II+: price not shown in snippets — [L&B](https://www.lbaltimeters.com/consumer/visual-altimeters/viso-ii.html)
- **Purchase frequency:** 32% of USPA members bought new gear in 2024 (29% in 2023) — [USPA 2024 Survey](https://www.uspa.org/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=4DJYjAyLi3Y%3D&portalid=0) (snippet)
- **Recon HUD precedent:** priced at $349 — [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/recon-instruments-gps-goggles-skydiving/24669/)

### Inferences
- **Instrument stack per experienced jumper:** visual altimeter ($250–$400) plus audible ($150–$260) plus optional GPS ($275–$300) comes to about $700–$950 of electronics. A HUD priced at $400–$600 competes for the same wallet, as a complement or replacement for the visual altimeter.
- **Replacement cycles (typical industry practice, not sourced here):** helmets about 3–5 years; altimeters 5–10 years (battery or sensor lifetimes); containers 10+ years; mains roughly 500–1,000 jumps. The resulting annual electronics spend is roughly $100–$250 per active jumper.
- **Annual spend pool:** 42k US members × 32% buying gear gives about 13.5k US gear buyers a year. Scaled to about 100k worldwide, that is about 30k buyers a year.

### Gaps
- Prices for Viso II+, Deep & Steep Insight and ENGO 2 were not visible because retailer pages were blocked.
- No survey data on average annual spend per jumper.
- No published replacement-cycle data from manufacturers.

## 4. Dropzones and wind tunnels (channel)

### Takeaway
The US has 220+ USPA Group Member dropzones, the UK 28, and there are 200+ wind tunnels worldwide. The worldwide dropzone count is not reliably published (plausibly about 600–900).

### Cited Findings
- **US:** more than 220 USPA-affiliated dropzones — [USPA 2024 Fatality Summary](https://www.uspa.org/a-milestone-in-safetythe-2024-fatality-summary)
- **UK:** 28 affiliated PTOs — [British Skydiving](https://britishskydiving.org/overview/)
- **Worldwide dropzones:** DropzoneFinder claims dropzones "in 194 countries", which is marketing copy and implausible. Another directory lists dropzones across 54 countries — [DropzoneFinder](https://dropzonefinder.com/); [SkydiveWorld](https://www.skydiveworld.net/dropzone/). Both are low reliability.
- **Wind tunnels:** "over 200 wind tunnels worldwide", up from fewer than 50 in 2012 — [Wikipedia, Bodyflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodyflight); see also [FAI Indoor Skydiving](https://www.fai.org/page/indoor-skydiving). iFLY is the largest chain — [iFLY](https://www.iflyworld.com/)

### Inferences
- **Channel concentration:** a few hundred dropzone pro shops plus 5–10 major online retailers (ChutingStar, SkydiveShop, Sky-Shop.eu, Para Gear, wingstore.aero) reach most of the market.
- **Wind tunnels** are useful for demos and as a training channel, though a GPS HUD has limited function indoors (it could show body attitude only).

### Gaps
- No authoritative worldwide dropzone count.
- Dropzone counts for Germany, France (FFP centres), Australia, Spain and Brazil were not found.

## 5. Tandem and student volumes (future buyers; training-device angle)

### Takeaway
The UK alone trains about 60,000 students a year, most of them tandem or first jumps. US tandem volume is reportedly about 450k jumps a year, but only via a low-quality aggregator. Few tandem customers convert: the USPA membership base is stable at about 42k.

### Cited Findings
- **UK:** about 60,000 students a year — [British Skydiving](https://britishskydiving.org/overview/)
- **US:** 450,000 tandem jumps in 2023 — [zipdo](https://zipdo.co/skydiving-statistics/). **LOW QUALITY, unverified.**
- **US licences:** 23% of USPA members earned a new licence in 2024 (a proxy for progression activity) — [USPA 2024 Survey](https://www.uspa.org/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=4DJYjAyLi3Y%3D&portalid=0) (snippet)
- **Student safety:** 2025 had zero US student fatalities, only the second such year since 1961 — [Skydive Fundamentals](https://www.skydivefundamentals.com/blog/skydiving-fatalities-2025)
- **Existing student products:** Alti-2 sells a dedicated "Atlas II Student" altimeter at $439, a precedent for dropzone fleet and student SKUs — [Alti-2](https://alti-2.com/products/sports/atlas-ii-purple-special-edition/)

### Inferences
- **Student training device:** sold to dropzone schools at perhaps 1–3 units per 1,000 annual students, with instructors viewing the student's altitude and heading. This is a B2B fleet market of hundreds of dropzones × 2–10 units each, i.e. low thousands of units worldwide.
- **Tandem customers** are not a HUD buyer pool. The funnel into licensed status is small, roughly a few thousand new A-licences a year in the US (inferred from the 23% new-licence stat spread across all licence levels).

### Gaps
- USPA's annual count of new A licences and tandem and AFF jump totals from primary sources.
- FFP tandem share of the 506k jumps.

## TAM/SAM/SOM scaffold (inference only; all inputs above)
- **TAM (sport skydiving, head-count):** about 100k active licensed jumpers × about $500 average HUD price = **about $50M installed-base value**. Annualised at a 4-year replacement cycle, about **$12M a year**.
  - Adding military free-fall users (low thousands of units at $2–5k, unverified) could add about $5–15M.
  - Paragliding and speedflying could add a multiple, but they have strong incumbents.
- **SAM (GPS-feedback disciplines, English- and EU-speaking markets):** about 15–25k jumpers (wingsuit, canopy, tracking and angle, camera, instructors). That is **about $8–12M installed base**, or about $2–4M a year.
- **SOM (years 1–3):** assuming 5–10% of SAM, about **1,000–2,500 units cumulative**, i.e. about 300–1,000 units a year at a $400–$700 average selling price, before military contracts.
- These are rough analyst inferences and must be refreshed with the USPA survey PDF (licence and experience distribution) and competitor sell-through data (Insight/ENGO, FlySight).

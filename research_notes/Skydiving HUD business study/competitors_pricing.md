# Competitors and Price Benchmarks: Skydiving AR/HUD Product Line (glasses + smart helmet, premium/standard, app + pro membership)

Research date: 2026-09-28. Currency USD unless stated. **Method caveat:** the egress proxy blocked every page fetch attempted (ChutingStar, SkydiveShop, The Ranch, Dekunu, FlySight, Deep and Steep, Cookie, DC Rainmaker, GlobalSecurity). All figures below come from **search-engine snippets** of the cited pages, not from reading the pages. Snippet prices are generally the retailer's current listed price (i.e. **street/retail price**). MSRP is marked where the snippet said so. Treat single-snippet numbers as indicative and re-check before final pricing. 27 web searches were used.

## 1. Direct skydiving competitors (HUD attempts, smart altimeters, audibles, GPS, logbook software, military nav)

### Takeaway
No one currently sells a dedicated skydiving HUD goggle or helmet. The only one on the market is a bolt-on: Deep and Steep's Insight GPS altimeter (about €589) plus ENGO 2 HUD glasses (about $330 standalone as cycling glasses), so about $900+ for a full HUD setup. Premium skydiving instruments sit in a tight $350–$440 band (Viso II+, Optima 3, Atlas II+, Protrack II, FlySight 2, Dekunu One). That band is the price anchor that skydivers already accept for one electronic instrument.

- Deep and Steep Insight GPS altimeter: **€589** at SkydiveShop (snippet; retail). Price of the HUD glasses sold as a skydiving accessory was not shown in snippets. — [SkydiveShop Insight](https://skydiveshop.com/products/insight-gps-altimeter)

**Military HALO/HAHO navigation**
- HAHO-PNS (US Navy prototype): a goggle-mounted microdisplay and optic, a helmet-mounted conformal navigation pod, and a controller, with custom embedded navigation software. — [GlobalSecurity HAHO-PNS](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/systems/aircraft/systems/haho-pns.htm)
- Patents cover parachutist navigation displays (satellite-map window plus look-ahead "roadmap" steering path) and high-altitude parachute navigation flight computers. — [US 8,244,461](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8244461); [US 7,302,340](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7302340)
- The NAVAID gives in-flight GPS navigation and integrates with the JPADS Mission Planner. — [GlobalSecurity HALO/HAHO](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/ops/airborne-halo-haho.htm)

### Inferences
- The current "HUD for skydivers" option costs about €589 (Insight) plus about $330 (ENGO 2). That is roughly **$900–$1,000 street price** for a two-device, bolt-on setup. A single integrated premium glasses SKU at $599–$899 would undercut that and remove a device.
- Every mainstream skydiving instrument costs $350–$440. A standard glasses SKU near $399–$499 would feel like "one more instrument". Anything above about $700 needs clearly new capability (navigation, glide/LZ guidance, visual safety alerts) to justify it.
- Incumbent altimeter makers rely on free desktop software or cheap logbook licenses. The only precedent for recurring fees is Paralog's annual license. Skydivers are not used to subscriptions, so the pro tier should be modest and clearly add value.

### Gaps

- No confirmed prices for the Altimaster N3, Alti-2 N3, or the L&B "LB Jump" software (whether it is free or paid). No confirmed price for FlySight software; it is believed to be free and open source (FlySight Viewer), but I found no source.
- No details on Project Obsidian, and no crowdfunded skydiving HUD campaigns (Kickstarter or Indiegogo) turned up in searches.
- No public prices for military HAHO/HALO navigation HUDs (Airborne Systems, etc.). These are probably contract-only.
- No standalone price for the Insight-branded ENGO 2 HUD glasses.

## 2. Adjacent sports AR/HUD hardware and app subscriptions

### Takeaway
Sports HUD eyewear clusters at **$250–$500** (Form $249–$279, ENGO 2 about $330, Rekkie $399, Oakley Meta Vanguard $499). Display-equipped AI glasses reach $799 (Meta Ray-Ban Display). Smart helmets sit at about **$1,099** (Forcite MK1S), and add-on helmet comms at about $290–$395. Standard wearable subscriptions are **$70/yr** (Garmin Connect+, Oura) up to **$80/yr** (Strava) and **$119/yr** (Form). Whoop runs a membership-only model at $199–$359/yr.

### Inferences
- A pro membership priced at **$59–$99/yr** (about $6.99–$9.99/mo) matches the Garmin, Oura and Strava norm. $119/yr (Form) is the upper bound for a single-sport, coaching-heavy tier.
- A smart helmet at $1,099 MSRP (Forcite) is the adjacent ceiling. Add-on comms at $290–$395 show that buyers pay about $300–$400 for "electronics bolted to a helmet".
- The Garmin Connect+ backlash shows the risk of putting existing or core safety features behind a paywall. Keep altitude and safety alerts free and charge for analytics, coaching, navigation and cloud.

## 3. Premium skydiving helmets and goggles (partner/competitor for the helmet SKU)

### Takeaway
Full-face skydiving helmets retail at about **$295–$700** (Cookie Fuel $295, Bonehead Aero $465, Cookie G4 about $400–$450 used, Tonfly TFX custom $691). Goggles cost about $27. A smart helmet at $999–$1,499 would be 2–3x the premium helmet price, so partnering (a HUD module for an existing shell) may be more viable than a full helmet.

### Cited Findings
- Cookie Fuel: **$295**. Bonehead Aero: **$465**. Custom Tonfly TFX full face: **$691** (ChutingStar snippets; retail). — [ChutingStar helmets](https://www.chutingstar.com/skydive/helmets); [Tonfly TFX](https://tonfly.com/helmet.php?id=9)
- Cookie G4: used about **$400–$450** on eBay. The new price was not in snippets. — [eBay skydiving helmets](https://www.ebay.com/b/Skydiving-Paragliding-Hang-Gliding-Helmets-Goggles/184377/bn_78213001); [Cookie G4](https://www.flycookie.com/shop/skydiving-helmets-parts/g4-skydiving-helmet/)
- Square1 / Kroops I.K. 91 skydiving goggles: **$27**. — [Skydiving Gear Store helmet accessories](https://skydivinggearstore.com/collections/helmet-accessories)

### Inferences
- The helmet SKU's HUD "delta" over a $300–$700 shell compares with Forcite's value add. A premium smart helmet at about $1,199–$1,499 and a standard one at about $899–$999 would be consistent. Alternatively, a retrofit HUD module (about $499–$699) that clips into a Cookie, Bonehead or Tonfly visor avoids competing with established helmet brands.
- Goggles are a commodity at about $27, so the glasses SKU is really priced against instruments ($350–$440), not against goggles.

## 4. Failed smart-helmet/HUD companies and lessons

### Takeaway
Smart helmets fail on execution and cash, not demand. Skully sold about 3,300 units at $1,499 but shipped about 200 before its 2016 Chapter 7 bankruptcy. Recon was bought by Intel and then shut down in 2017. Nuviz units were bricked in 2020 when a map license expired. BMW and LiveMap ran into production and certification problems.

### Cited Findings
- Skully AR-1 cost **$1,499** and had a 180° rear camera and GPS HUD. About **3,300 helmets were sold and about 200 shipped**. Causes were production delays, failure to raise follow-on funding, and misuse-of-funds lawsuits against the founders. Chapter 7 filing came in Oct 2016. Most backers got neither product nor refund. — [Wikipedia: Skully](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skully_(helmet)); [CNN Money](https://money.cnn.com/2016/08/11/technology/skully-indiegogo-weller-lawsuit/index.html); [Jalopnik](https://www.jalopnik.com/failed-hud-helmet-maker-skully-spent-r-d-money-on-strip-1785093398/); [Asphalt & Rubber](https://www.asphaltandrubber.com/news/skully-helmets-chapter-7-bankruptcy/)
- Recon Instruments: acquired by Intel on 17 Jun 2015. Intel announced in June 2017 that all Recon products would be discontinued by year end. — [Wikipedia: Recon Instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recon_Instruments)
- Nuviz: delivered a product that worked well, but units stopped booting from 1 Jan 2020 when its map license expired, and they vanished from retailers. — [RideApart: Nuviz HUDs stop working](https://www.rideapart.com/news/392362/nuviz-hud-stop-working-company-tanks/); [RideApart: Where has Nuviz gone](https://www.rideapart.com/news/374492/where-helmet-hud-nuviz-gone/)
- The LiveMap CEO said BMW's smart-helmet project "failed by production and certification problems". — [RideApart LiveMap interview](https://www.rideapart.com/news/247410/understanding-hud-motorcycle-helmets-exclusive-interview-with-livemap-ceo/)
- Rekkie (a counterexample) survived, selling snow goggles at $399 direct-to-consumer. — [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1791531/what-happened-rekkie-ar-ski-goggles-shark-tank-season-15/)

### Inferences
- Lessons:
  1. Don't take crowdfunded pre-orders for a helmet before certification and manufacturing are locked.
  2. Avoid third-party runtime licenses (maps) that can brick devices. Design for offline or fail-safe operation, which is essential for a safety instrument.
  3. Being acquired by a big company can end the product.
  4. The glasses and add-on route (Rekkie, Form, ENGO) has a better survival record than full integrated helmets.

### Gaps
- No reliable BOM or cost data for Skully, Nuviz, or LiveMap. No sourced details on Vuzix sports eyewear. The Livemap outcome (it apparently never shipped) was not confirmed.

## 5. Subscription attach and conversion rates

### Takeaway
Hard attach-rate data is scarce. Strava's free-to-paid conversion is described only as "historically low" (single digits of a 180M base), and no Garmin or Form attach rates are public. The strongest models (Whoop, Oura) make membership mandatory or bundle it.

### Cited Findings
- Strava's free-to-premium conversion is "historically low, a persistent structural concern". About 90% of revenue comes from subscriptions, and 2025 revenue was $415M. — [Business of Apps](https://www.businessofapps.com/data/strava-statistics/); [Sacra Strava report, updated 31 Jul 2026](https://sacra-pdfs.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/strava.pdf)
- Whoop is membership-only, with the hardware included in the fee. Oura requires membership for most insights on top of a $349+ ring. — [Whoop](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/membership/); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/04/11-billion-oura-ceo-subscriptions-tom-hale-peloton-tesla-elon-musk/)

### Inferences
- Rough arithmetic: $415M revenue at about $80/yr implies roughly 4–5M paying users out of 180M registered, about **2–3% conversion**. This is my own back-of-envelope estimate, not sourced. Hardware-linked apps usually convert far higher than free-app freemium, because owners are already invested. Still, plan conservatively (about 10–25% of hardware owners) unless membership is bundled for the first year.

### Gaps
- No sourced attach rates for Form, Garmin Connect+, or Peloton (Peloton's all-access attach is historically near-universal among hardware owners, but this is unverified here).
